What is Carricante?

Carricante is a Sicilian white grape best known for Etna wines. Learn about its acidity, flavours, label terms, food matches and emerging Australian context.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Carricante is a white wine-grape variety from Sicily, Italy. It is best known for the dry, fresh white wines of Mount Etna, where it is one of the region’s signature grapes.

Carricante wines are commonly associated with lively acidity, citrus flavours and a firm, refreshing texture. Depending on where and how the wine is made, you may also notice floral, herbal, saline or mineral-like impressions.

The important thing to remember is that Carricante is a grape, not a region, wine-making method or fixed flavour profile.

Highlights

  • Carricante is a white Vitis vinifera wine grape.
  • It comes from Sicily and is especially associated with Mount Etna.
  • It usually makes dry, high-acid white wines.
  • Citrus, flowers, herbs and saline or mineral-like notes are common descriptions.
  • Carricante is different from Catarratto, although the names have sometimes been confused.
  • It can appear in Etna wines without the word “Carricante” being prominent on the label.
  • Australian plantings and trials exist, but Carricante is still a niche variety here.

What does Carricante taste like?

Carricante often makes white wines that feel bright, dry and energetic. Acidity is usually the first thing you notice: it gives the wine freshness and makes your mouth water.

Commonly associated flavours and aromas include:

  • lemon and other citrus;
  • white flowers;
  • fresh herbs;
  • green or slightly savoury notes;
  • saline impressions;
  • a mineral-like or stony character.

These are tendencies, not guarantees. A Carricante from a cool, high-elevation vineyard may feel quite lean and precise, while another may have more weight or texture. Harvest decisions, blending and wine-making also influence the finished wine.

“Mineral” is best understood as a tasting description rather than a literal taste of volcanic rock. Carricante is strongly associated with Mount Etna, but it is too simple to say that volcanic soil directly creates one particular flavour.

Where does Carricante come from?

Carricante is a Sicilian grape, with its strongest modern identity linked to Mount Etna. Etna is the active volcano in eastern Sicily, and Carricante is particularly associated with vineyards on its eastern side and at higher elevations.

This is why you will often see Carricante discussed alongside Etna Bianco. The two names describe different things:

  • Carricante is a grape variety.
  • Etna Bianco is a protected regional wine category.
  • Milo is a locality within the Etna wine area.

Under the cited Etna DOC specification, Etna Bianco must contain at least 60% Carricante. Etna Bianco Superiore must contain at least 80%, and this category is restricted to the defined area of Milo.

That means an Etna Bianco bottle may contain plenty of Carricante without naming the grape as its main front-label identity. It also means that not every wine labelled Carricante is necessarily an Etna wine: the grape can be grown outside Etna.

Is Carricante the same as Catarratto?

No. Carricante and Catarratto are different white grape varieties.

The names have sometimes been confused, and the grapes can appear together in Etna white wines. But seeing Catarratto on a technical sheet or label does not mean the wine contains Carricante, or vice versa.

Carricante is also recorded under names including Caricanti, Carricanti, Catanese bianco and Nocera bianca. These names are useful when reading older references or researching the variety, but Carricante is the clearest name for consumers.

Can Carricante age?

Some well-made Etna examples can develop greater complexity in bottle. Their freshness and acidity can give the wine a firm backbone, but ageing potential varies considerably.

The result depends on the vineyard, vintage, fruit condition, blending and wine-making. Carricante is not automatically a long-lived wine simply because it comes from Etna, so treat bottle-age claims as possibilities rather than promises.

For most drinkers, the variety’s immediate appeal is its combination of freshness, citrus character and savoury texture.

How should you serve Carricante?

Serve Carricante chilled, but not so cold that its aroma and texture disappear. If a bottle is refrigerator-cold, allowing it a few minutes in the glass can make it easier to taste.

Its acidity makes it a useful partner for foods such as:

  • seafood and shellfish;
  • grilled or lightly cooked fish;
  • salads with moderate acidity;
  • vegetables;
  • lighter Mediterranean dishes.

A fuller or more textured Carricante may also work with richer preparations. The best match depends on the wine’s weight and the dish’s intensity, rather than on the grape name alone.

Will you find Carricante in Australia?

Australian drinkers are most likely to encounter Carricante in imported Sicilian or Etna wines. Australian nursery and trial activity has also been documented, including material associated with Chalmers Wine and reported planting activity in Heathcote, Victoria.

Carricante remains an emerging, niche variety in Australia. There is not enough evidence to describe a standard Australian Carricante style or claim widespread national availability. An Australian example should not be expected to taste exactly like a wine from Mount Etna: place, climate, vineyard conditions and wine-making all matter.

The short version

Carricante is a Sicilian white grape, best known for Etna Bianco wines. Expect freshness, lively acidity and possible citrus, floral, herbal or savoury-mineral notes, but remember that every bottle reflects its site and wine-making choices.

If you see Carricante on a wine list, it is reasonable to expect a dry white with energy and food-friendly acidity. If the label says Etna Bianco instead, Carricante may still be an important part of the wine—even if the grape name is not front and centre.

Frequently asked questions

Is Carricante a white or red grape?

Carricante is a white wine grape.

Is Carricante sweet?

Carricante is generally used to make dry white wines. The fruit flavours may smell or taste citrusy, but that does not mean the wine is sweet.

Is Carricante only grown on Mount Etna?

No. It is most strongly associated with Etna and Sicily, but Carricante can be grown elsewhere.

Is Carricante always blended?

No. It can be used in different ways. Etna Bianco is a regional category with permitted composition rules, while a wine labelled Carricante may be made in a different style or outside Etna.

What is the difference between Carricante and Etna Bianco?

Carricante is a grape variety. Etna Bianco is a protected regional wine category that must contain a minimum proportion of Carricante under the cited specification.
