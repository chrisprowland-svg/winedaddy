What is Falanghina?

Falanghina is a southern Italian white grape associated with Campania. Learn its typical flavours, styles, serving temperature and food-pairing starting points.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Falanghina is a white wine-grape variety from southern Italy.
  • It is especially associated with Campania, including the Benevento and Sannio areas.
  • The familiar style is a dry, fresh and aromatic white wine.
  • Common tasting notes include citrus, orchard or stone fruit, floral and herbal characters.
  • Falanghina can also be made in sparkling, late-harvest and passito styles.
  • A bottle’s place, vintage and winemaking choices can make it taste quite different from another Falanghina.

Quick answer

Falanghina is an Italian white grape variety, particularly associated with Campania in southern Italy. Most wines labelled Falanghina are dry, fresh and aromatic, with noticeable acidity and flavours or aromas that may suggest lemon, other citrus fruits, pear, apple, peach, flowers or herbs.

Those descriptions are useful starting points, not guarantees. Falanghina is a grape, while the finished wine also reflects its vineyard site, climate, harvest timing and winemaking. A young, fresh white may feel light and lively; a riper or more textured example may feel broader. The grape can also be used for sparkling, late-harvest and passito wines, which may be sweeter or richer than the familiar dry white style.

For an Australian wine drinker, the simplest way to understand Falanghina is: expect an Italian white with freshness and aromatic character, then use the label and the wine’s actual style to refine that expectation.

Where is Falanghina from?

Falanghina is most strongly associated with Campania, a region in southern Italy. Benevento province, including the Sannio area, is one of the most useful place references to recognise on a label.

This is where the regulated denomination Falanghina del Sannio DOC comes in. It is a denomination for wines from Benevento province, not another name for the grape itself. The grape is Falanghina; Sannio describes a legally defined place and wine category.

You may also encounter other geographic wording connected with Falanghina, such as Beneventano or names referring to areas within Campania. A place name can tell you where the wine was made or how it is classified, while the grape name tells you the variety used. They answer different questions.

What does Falanghina taste like?

A typical dry Falanghina may combine:

  • lemon or other citrus;
  • pear, apple or peach-like fruit;
  • floral aromas;
  • herbal notes; and
  • a fresh, lively finish.

Its acidity is often noticeable. In wine, acidity is the quality that gives a drink freshness and makes your mouth water. That freshness is one reason Falanghina can work well with food.

The wine is generally white and non-tannic, but its body and texture are not fixed. Some examples may feel lean and crisp, while riper fruit, lees ageing or other winemaking choices can create a rounder, fuller texture. Oak is not an automatic feature or an automatic exclusion: the producer’s choices matter.

The best approach is to treat tasting notes as a map rather than a promise. If a label says Falanghina, you have a useful clue about the grape, but not a complete description of the wine in the bottle.

Is Falanghina always dry?

No. Dry still white wine is the usual reference point, but Falanghina is not limited to one style.

Falanghina del Sannio DOC includes still white and sparkling categories, as well as late-harvest and passito styles. Late-harvest and passito wines can be richer and sweeter because of how the grapes are harvested and made.

This is a helpful reminder that a grape variety does not determine every feature of a wine by itself. Falanghina can contribute to a wine’s likely aroma and acidity, but place, ripeness, fermentation, maturation and residual sugar also affect the result.

How should you serve Falanghina?

For a fresh, dry Falanghina, start by serving it lightly chilled, around 8–12°C. You do not need to treat that as an exact rule.

A very light, young bottle may be enjoyable towards the cooler end of that range. A fuller, sweeter, sparkling or more developed style may show more clearly when it is not excessively cold. If the wine seems muted straight from the refrigerator, giving it a few minutes in the glass can help its aromas emerge.

What food goes with Falanghina?

Freshness and acidity make a dry Falanghina a sensible starting point for:

  • seafood and shellfish;
  • simply prepared fish;
  • fish-based pasta;
  • white meats;
  • fresh or grilled vegetables; and
  • mild cheeses.

These are starting points, not rigid rules. The sauce, seasoning and cooking method may matter as much as the main ingredient. A delicate fish dish may suit a crisp style, while a richer preparation may call for a Falanghina with more body and texture.

What should Australian shoppers look for?

If you see Falanghina on a bottle list or wine label in Australia, think first of a southern Italian white grape commonly associated with fresh, dry and aromatic wines. Then look for the producer, vintage and geographic designation for more context.

The grape has been considered in Australia’s alternative-variety and wine-industry context, but the available evidence does not establish current Australian planting levels, popularity or national availability. It is therefore better not to assume that every Falanghina will be easy to find or taste the same.

The practical takeaway is simple: Falanghina gives you a useful starting expectation, not a guarantee. If you enjoy fresh white wines with citrus-like fruit, floral notes and lively acidity, it is a variety worth exploring.

Falanghina FAQs

How do you pronounce Falanghina?

It is commonly pronounced fa-lan-GHEE-na, with the emphasis on the middle syllable.

Is Falanghina a region or a grape?

Falanghina is a grape variety. Campania is the broader Italian region associated with it, while Falanghina del Sannio DOC is a regulated geographic wine denomination.

Is Falanghina similar to Sauvignon Blanc or Pinot Grigio?

It can share freshness with other crisp white wines, but grape comparisons are only broad guides. Falanghina may show citrus, orchard fruit, floral and herbal character, and its texture can vary with ripeness and winemaking.

Should Falanghina be served cold?

It is best lightly chilled rather than served warm. Around 8–12°C is a useful starting range for a fresh, dry example, with some variation for fuller, sweeter or sparkling styles.

What does Falanghina pair with?

Try seafood, shellfish, fish, white meats, vegetables or mild cheeses. Match the wine’s acidity and body to the preparation rather than following a fixed pairing rule.
