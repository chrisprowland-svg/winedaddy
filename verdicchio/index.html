What Is Verdicchio? A Beginner’s Guide

Learn what Verdicchio is, where it comes from, how it tastes and how to read Verdicchio labels in Australia.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Verdicchio is a white wine grape variety from Italy.
  • It is especially associated with the Marche region, on Italy’s Adriatic side.
  • Its two best-known Italian wine denominations are Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi and Verdicchio di Matelica.
  • Typical dry examples show noticeable freshness, citrus or orchard-fruit flavours and a dry, sometimes gently bitter finish.
  • Verdicchio can also be made as sparkling, passito and Riserva wine under the relevant Italian appellation rules.
  • Australian drinkers may encounter imported bottles and occasional Australian examples, but current evidence does not establish Verdicchio as a major Australian variety.

Quick answer: what is Verdicchio?

Verdicchio is an Italian white grape variety, principally associated with Marche. It is best known through the protected Italian denominations Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi and Verdicchio di Matelica.

That distinction matters. Verdicchio is the grape; Castelli di Jesi and Matelica are places and protected appellation names. A bottle labelled Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi, for example, identifies both the grape-based wine and its regulated geographic origin.

The usual beginner reference point is a dry, still white wine with lively acidity, citrus and orchard-fruit flavours, and sometimes herbal, savoury or almond-like notes. A gently bitter finish is a traditional association, particularly in descriptions of Castelli di Jesi wines. These are tendencies, not guarantees: site, climate, ripeness, winemaking and ageing can all change the result.

Where does Verdicchio come from?

Verdicchio is strongly linked with Marche, a region in central-eastern Italy. Two principal appellation contexts are:

  • Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi, associated with the hills around Jesi and designated areas of Ancona and Macerata.
  • Verdicchio di Matelica, associated with the inland Matelica area and designated municipalities in Macerata and Ancona.

They are separate appellations, even though both are built around Verdicchio. Jesi and Matelica are place names, not alternative names for the grape. Marche is the wider Italian region, not a synonym for either denomination.

This is similar to the difference between a grape and a regional wine name elsewhere in the wine world. The grape tells you what variety is central to the wine; the appellation tells you more about where it comes from and which production rules apply.

What does Verdicchio taste like?

Verdicchio is often a useful choice for someone who enjoys white wine with freshness and structure rather than obvious sweetness.

Typical examples may show:

  • Acidity: lively or firm freshness that gives the wine energy.
  • Fruit: lemon or other citrus, green apple, pear and sometimes stone fruit.
  • Aromas: herbs, flowers or savoury notes, depending on the wine.
  • Texture: crisp and linear in some examples, broader or lightly waxy in others.
  • Finish: dry, savoury or gently bitter, sometimes with an almond-like impression.

It is best not to treat any of these characteristics as compulsory. Not every Verdicchio tastes mineral, oily, almond-like or light-bodied. A younger, simply made wine may feel bright and brisk, while a more concentrated or aged example can feel broader and more textured.

The wine’s acidity is one of its most useful practical cues. It can make Verdicchio feel refreshing with food, while its savoury finish can add interest beyond straightforward fruitiness. If you are learning to taste wine, notice how the wine’s freshness changes your impression of its body and finish.

What styles of Verdicchio are available?

Most Australian drinkers will first encounter Verdicchio as a dry, still white wine. However, the Italian appellation specifications also recognise other styles.

Depending on the denomination and the specific label, you may see:

  • Spumante: sparkling Verdicchio.
  • Passito: wine made from dried grapes, generally richer and sweeter in style.
  • Riserva: a regulated category involving additional requirements within the relevant appellation.
  • Classico or Classico Superiore: terms used within the Castelli di Jesi framework.

The label term is important because “Verdicchio” alone does not tell you whether a wine is sparkling, sweet, aged or made in a particular appellation style.

The Castelli di Jesi specification cited in the research allows at least 85 per cent Verdicchio, with other permitted white varieties potentially making up the balance. Matelica’s specification also permits limited proportions of other authorised or recommended white varieties. So it is safer not to assume that every appellation wine is necessarily 100 per cent Verdicchio.

How might Australians encounter Verdicchio?

On an Australian shelf or wine list, an imported bottle may use the Italian appellation name, such as Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi or Verdicchio di Matelica. You may also see Verdicchio Bianco, the formal variety name recorded in the OIV variety material used by Wine Australia.

An Australian wine may identify Verdicchio or Verdicchio Bianco as a grape variety where the wine and label comply with Australian requirements. Wine Australia evidence also confirms industry interest in alternative Mediterranean varieties and records Verdicchio Bianco vine material in the National Grapevine Collection.

However, the available evidence does not establish significant Australian planting area, crush volume or national market share. In practical terms, look for imported Italian examples and treat local examples as an occasional discovery rather than assuming Verdicchio is a mainstream Australian variety.

A label mentioning Marche identifies the broader regional context. It does not mean the wine is automatically from Castelli di Jesi or Matelica.

What should you serve with Verdicchio?

Serve a dry still Verdicchio chilled, but not so cold that its aromas disappear. A standard white-wine glass is suitable.

Its acidity makes it a natural starting point for seafood, shellfish, white fish, salads, vegetables and lighter pasta dishes. Its savoury or gently bitter finish can also work with Mediterranean-style flavours and soft cheeses. More concentrated examples may suit richer fish or poultry.

These are starting points, not rules. The best match depends on the wine’s weight, texture and level of acidity as well as the dish.

Is Verdicchio the same as Trebbiano?

No. The research supplied for this guide identifies Verdicchio as a distinct recognised grape variety. It should not be casually relabelled as Trebbiano or treated as an interchangeable name.

The safest way to read a Verdicchio label is to separate three things:

  1. The grape: Verdicchio or Verdicchio Bianco.
  2. The place or appellation: such as Castelli di Jesi or Matelica.
  3. The style term: such as Spumante, Passito, Classico or Riserva.

That simple distinction makes the wine easier to understand before you open the bottle.

Frequently asked questions

Is Verdicchio a dry wine?

Often, yes. Dry still Verdicchio is the main style a beginner is likely to encounter, but the appellation rules also allow sparkling and passito expressions.

Is Verdicchio sweet?

Not usually in its standard still form. Passito Verdicchio is a different style made from dried grapes and can be richer and sweeter.

Is Verdicchio a region?

No. Verdicchio is a grape variety. Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi and Verdicchio di Matelica are protected Italian appellation names associated with the grape.

Is Verdicchio an ageworthy wine?

Some concentrated or well-made examples may develop with age, but it is not accurate to assume that every Verdicchio is designed for long cellaring. The individual wine’s structure, winemaking and vintage matter.

What is the main thing to remember?

Think of Verdicchio as an Italian white grape known for freshness, dry texture and a distinctive savoury or gently bitter finish, especially in wines from Marche.
