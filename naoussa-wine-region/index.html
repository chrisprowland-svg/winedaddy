Naoussa wine region: a beginner’s guide

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Naoussa is a protected wine region in northern Greece, best known for red wines made from the Xinomavro grape. Its wines are often marked by bright acidity, firm tannin, savoury and red-fruited aromas, and the ability to develop with bottle age.

The important distinction is simple: Naoussa is the place; Xinomavro is the grape. For PDO Naoussa, the cited regional specification requires red wine made from 100% Xinomavro.

Highlights

  • Naoussa is a Greek Protected Designation of Origin (PDO).
  • It sits in Central Macedonia, within the regional unit of Imathia.
  • The designation takes its name from the town of Naoussa but covers surrounding communities.
  • PDO Naoussa red wine is made from 100% Xinomavro under the cited regional specification.
  • The wines are commonly associated with high acidity, firm tannin and pale-to-medium colour.
  • Xinomavro is also grown in other Greek regions, so not every Xinomavro wine is from Naoussa.
  • Site, altitude, soils and winemaking can produce noticeably different styles.

Where is Naoussa?

Naoussa is in northern Greece, in the administrative region of Central Macedonia and the regional unit of Imathia. In broad wine-geographic terms, it is part of Greek Macedonia.

The wine region is centred on the town of Naoussa and extends across surrounding communities on and around the south-eastern foothills of Mount Vermio. Examples named by regional sources include Kopanos, Lefkadia, Marina, Anthemion, Giannakohori, Stenimachos, Rodochori, Trilofos and Fytia. Published sources differ slightly in how some localities are grouped or transliterated, so this is not an exhaustive statement of the legal boundary.

For orientation, Naoussa lies roughly west of Thessaloniki. A journey by road is commonly around 1–1½ hours, depending on the route, traffic and starting point. Treat that as a general sense of direction rather than a precise travel promise.

On a bottle, you may see Naoussa, PDO Naoussa or the Greek form ΠΟΠ Νάουσα. The European Union recognises Naoussa as a PDO, listed as PDO-GR-A1610. The designation is documented as having been recognised in 1971.

What grape is Naoussa wine made from?

The defining grape of PDO Naoussa is Xinomavro. The name Naoussa therefore carries two kinds of information: it identifies protected geographic origin, while Xinomavro identifies the grape variety.

This is similar in principle to how an Australian drinker might read a geographic indication and variety as separate pieces of information. A regional name describes where the wine comes from and, where relevant, the rules that apply. A grape name describes the variety used.

Naoussa is strongly associated with Xinomavro because the PDO specification described by recognised Greek wine bodies requires 100% Xinomavro. However, Xinomavro is not exclusive to Naoussa. It is also important in separate Greek PDOs including Amyndeon, Goumenissa and Rapsani.

That means Naoussa is not another name for Xinomavro, and Xinomavro is not another name for Naoussa.

What do Naoussa wines taste like?

Naoussa is often a good choice for someone who enjoys red wine with structure rather than softness alone. Its typical profile is built around several components:

  • Acidity: usually prominent, giving the wine freshness and energy.
  • Tannin: often firm, particularly when young, creating a drying or grippy sensation.
  • Colour: commonly pale to medium rather than deeply opaque.
  • Body: medium to full, depending on the site and winemaking.
  • Aromas: red fruits such as strawberry and sour cherry may sit alongside savoury, earthy or vegetal notes, including tomato and olive. Spice and floral characters are also documented.

The combination can surprise Australian drinkers. A red wine may be pale in the glass yet have substantial tannin and acidity. Colour alone does not tell you how powerful or ageworthy a wine will feel.

These are regional tendencies, not guarantees. A particular Naoussa may be more fruit-driven, lighter in body, more oak-influenced or more approachable in its youth.

Why can Naoussa wines vary?

Naoussa includes vineyards at different elevations and in different landscape positions. Regional sources describe many vineyards as sitting roughly between 100 and 400 metres, with a significant concentration on the south-eastern slopes of Mount Vermio.

The climate is generally described as Mediterranean with continental influence: cold winters, relatively hot summers, seasonal rainfall and summer dryness. Soils vary too, including sandy-clay, clay-loam, calcareous and schistose forms.

It is best to think of these factors as a set of influences, not a flavour formula. Slope, exposure, altitude, soil, vine balance, yields, maceration, wood maturation and release decisions can all affect the final wine. This is the practical meaning of terroir: place matters, but it works together with human choices rather than dictating one inevitable taste.

Traditional approaches may produce more austere, tannic wines that benefit from further bottle development. More modern interpretations can emphasise fruit and earlier approachability. No single style represents every Naoussa wine.

How should an Australian learner approach a bottle?

If the label says PDO Naoussa, a reasonable expectation is a red wine from the defined Naoussa area, made under the cited PDO rules from 100% Xinomavro.

Look for the relationship between the place and the grape, then consider the wine’s structure. If you enjoy wines with noticeable acidity and tannin, Naoussa may be an interesting style to explore. It is not necessary to assume that every bottle needs long-term cellaring, though the region has documented ageing potential.

Do not confuse Naoussa with:

  • the town alone, since the PDO covers surrounding communities;
  • the wider Macedonia region;
  • Xinomavro, which is also used elsewhere;
  • Amyndeon, Goumenissa or Rapsani, which are separate Greek PDOs.

Frequently asked questions

Is Naoussa a grape variety?

No. Naoussa is a place and a protected wine designation. Xinomavro is the grape variety most closely associated with it.

Is Naoussa wine always red?

The cited PDO material describes PDO Naoussa as red wine made from 100% Xinomavro. It documents dry, semi-dry and semi-sweet red categories, so the place name alone should not be treated as a guarantee of one sweetness level.

Is Naoussa the same as Xinomavro?

No. PDO Naoussa is made from Xinomavro, but Xinomavro is also grown in other Greek regions.

Can Naoussa wine age?

Many Naoussa wines have the acidity and tannin to develop with bottle age. The timing will vary with the wine, vintage and winemaking, so the region should not be treated as a fixed cellaring instruction.

What does PDO mean?

PDO means Protected Designation of Origin. In plain terms, it is a legally recognised geographic designation connecting a product with a defined place and production rules.
