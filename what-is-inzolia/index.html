What is Inzolia?

Inzolia is an Italian white wine grape associated with Sicily. Learn its other names, typical wine style, Australian context and how to serve it.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Inzolia is a white wine grape variety from Italy.
  • It is especially associated with Sicily, particularly western Sicily.
  • You may also see the names Insolia and Ansonica.
  • It can be made as a varietal wine or blended with other white grapes.
  • Typical wines can be fresh, structured and lightly savoury, with citrus, stone-fruit or tropical-fruit flavours.
  • The grape’s natural acidity is not necessarily high, so wines can vary considerably.
  • In Australia, Inzolia remains a small and relatively uncommon variety.

The quick answer

Inzolia is an Italian white wine grape best known for its long association with Sicily. It is used to make white wines that are often dry, fresh and moderately structured, although the grape name alone does not guarantee one exact flavour, sweetness or body.

Inzolia is also known as Insolia. In Tuscany and parts of coastal central Italy, the same recognised grape is commonly called Ansonica. That means a bottle labelled Ansonica may be made from the grape many Sicilian producers call Inzolia.

The grape has a historical connection with Marsala production, but Inzolia is not the same thing as Marsala. Inzolia is a grape; Marsala is a separate Sicilian fortified-wine style and designation. Today, Inzolia is also made into still table wines, either on its own or in blends.

What does Inzolia taste like?

A typical Inzolia wine may show:

  • citrus flavours;
  • stone fruit, such as peach;
  • some tropical-fruit character;
  • fresh or crisp sensations;
  • moderate structure;
  • a savoury, soft or slightly saline impression in some examples.

These are tendencies, not promises. Inzolia does not produce one uniform style. Some wines may feel bright and easy-drinking, while others have more weight, texture or savoury complexity.

One useful point for beginners is that freshness does not always mean very high natural acidity. Inzolia’s natural acidity may be modest, so the finished wine’s freshness depends partly on where and when the grapes are grown and how the wine is made.

Why can two Inzolia wines taste different?

Several decisions and conditions can change the result.

Climate and vineyard site

Cooler conditions can help preserve freshness in a grape whose natural acidity is not necessarily high. Warmer conditions may lead to riper fruit, more alcohol and a softer impression. The vineyard’s site and growing conditions also influence how concentrated or delicate the wine feels.

Harvest timing

Earlier-picked grapes can contribute more citrus-like freshness, while later-picked fruit may bring riper flavours, greater weight and softness. Neither approach is automatically better; it simply creates a different style.

Winemaking

A stainless-steel fermented wine may emphasise primary fruit and freshness. Lees contact can add a rounder texture, while skin contact can contribute more texture and phenolic grip. Oak maturation, where used, can broaden the wine and add oak-derived flavours.

Blending also changes the result. In Sicily, Inzolia may be blended with varieties such as Catarratto or Grillo. A blend can be designed to adjust aroma, acidity, body or balance.

Inzolia, Insolia and Ansonica: what is the difference?

For most drinkers, these names refer to the same recognised grape variety, but the spelling often reflects regional tradition.

  • Inzolia is the preferred term for this article and is strongly associated with Sicily.
  • Insolia is another recognised Italian and international spelling.
  • Ansonica is the name commonly associated with Tuscany, Maremma and nearby islands such as Elba and Giglio.

The name on the label can therefore tell you something about the wine’s regional context, but it does not by itself describe the wine’s exact flavour. A Sicilian Inzolia and a Tuscan Ansonica may share a grape identity while expressing different local and winemaking choices.

Where is Inzolia grown?

Sicily is Inzolia’s best-known home, particularly western Sicily. The variety was traditionally used in Marsala production and is now also used for dry white table wines.

It is found elsewhere in Italy too, including Tuscany. In coastal Tuscany and Maremma, the name Ansonica is more familiar. The islands of Elba and Giglio are also associated with the variety.

A map of Italy can be useful here because the different names make more sense when you see the distance between Sicily and Tuscany. They are regional names rather than automatically separate grape varieties.

What should Australian wine drinkers know?

Inzolia has been introduced to Australia, but it remains a minor variety compared with familiar grapes such as Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc. Australian wine-industry material describes it as a relatively recent arrival with only a few producers in its 2022 reference.

There is no current national planting total in the evidence reviewed, so it is better to describe Australian Inzolia as uncommon rather than attach a precise acreage figure. You may encounter it on an Australian or imported label as Inzolia, Insolia or Ansonica, depending on the wine’s origin and labelling tradition.

Italian regional naming rules should not automatically be applied to Australian wine. In Australia, read the full label and treat the grape name as a useful starting point, not a complete tasting forecast.

How should you serve Inzolia?

For a lighter, fresh style, start by serving Inzolia chilled at about 8–12°C. A fuller, more textured or oak-influenced example may show better at about 10–13°C, rather than being served extremely cold.

A standard white-wine glass is all you need. It gives the wine enough room for citrus, fruit and savoury aromas while remaining practical for everyday drinking.

For food, try Inzolia with:

  • grilled or baked fish;
  • prawns and other lighter seafood;
  • shellfish;
  • salads;
  • vegetable dishes;
  • fresh cheeses;
  • lightly seasoned Mediterranean-style food.

These are starting points, not rules. Rich sauces, chilli, sweetness and strong savoury flavours can change how the wine tastes with food.

Frequently asked questions

Is Inzolia a sweet wine?

Not necessarily. Inzolia is a grape variety, not a sweetness category. Many examples are dry, but the grape name alone does not guarantee a particular level of sweetness.

Is Inzolia the same as Marsala?

No. Inzolia is a grape variety. Marsala is a separate Sicilian fortified-wine style and designation. Inzolia has a historical connection with Marsala production, but not every Inzolia wine is Marsala.

Is Ansonica the same grape as Inzolia?

Authoritative wine references support treating Ansonica as a regional name for the same recognised variety, particularly in Tuscany and Maremma. The exact label name reflects local usage.

Is Inzolia a high-acid grape?

Its wines can taste fresh or crisp, but its natural acidity is not necessarily high. Climate, harvest timing, site and winemaking all affect the final impression.

Is Inzolia grown in Australia?

Yes, but it remains a small and relatively uncommon variety in Australia. The evidence reviewed does not support a current national planting total.
