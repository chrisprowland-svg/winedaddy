What Is Riesling? A Beginner’s Guide to This Aromatic White Wine

Riesling is an aromatic white grape that can make dry, off-dry and sweet wines. Learn how acidity, fruitiness and Australian regions shape its style.

Highlights

  • Riesling is a white grape variety, not a single sweetness level.
  • It is often aromatic, light- to medium-bodied and high in acidity.
  • Common flavours include lime, lemon, green apple, peach and floral notes.
  • Riesling can be bone-dry, off-dry, sweet or occasionally sparkling.
  • Most Australian Riesling is made in a crisp, dry style.
  • Clare Valley and Eden Valley are important Australian examples, but Riesling is grown in many regions.
  • Some Rieslings can age and develop honeyed, toasty or nutty flavours.

Quick answer: what is Riesling?

Riesling is a white wine grape variety. The name is also commonly used for wines made primarily from that grape.

Riesling is best known for its lively acidity and fragrant character. A young Riesling may smell or taste of lime, lemon, green apple, peach or flowers. Those flavours can appear in a completely dry wine, so Riesling is not automatically sweet.

The grape can produce several styles, from bone-dry to off-dry, sweet and dessert wines. In Australia, Riesling is especially associated with crisp, dry wines from regions such as Clare Valley and Eden Valley, although Tasmania, Great Southern, Canberra District and other regions also produce it.

The most useful beginner lesson is simple: the word “Riesling” tells you the grape, but not necessarily how sweet the finished wine will be.

What does Riesling taste like?

Young Riesling is often light- to medium-bodied, fresh and aromatic. Common descriptions include:

  • lime and lemon;
  • green apple;
  • peach;
  • citrus blossom or other floral notes;
  • a clean, mouth-watering finish.

These are typical possibilities rather than a fixed checklist. Climate, ripeness, region, winemaking and bottle age all influence the final wine.

Riesling’s acidity is one of its defining features. Acidity creates freshness and makes your mouth water, rather like the sensation from biting into a crisp green apple. It can also balance residual sugar, allowing a wine to be sweet without feeling heavy.

Riesling is therefore a useful variety for understanding the difference between fruitiness, sweetness, acidity and body:

  • Fruitiness describes aromas and flavours.
  • Sweetness mainly comes from residual sugar left in the wine.
  • Acidity provides freshness and structure.
  • Body describes how light or full the wine feels in your mouth.

A wine can smell of ripe peach and still be dry. Another can contain some residual sugar but remain fresh because of its acidity.

Is Riesling sweet?

Not necessarily. Most Australian Riesling is dry, meaning it contains little perceptible residual sugar. It can still taste intensely of citrus, apple or flowers.

Riesling is also made in off-dry, sweet and dessert styles. Off-dry wines contain some residual sugar but may taste balanced rather than obviously sweet. Sweeter styles can be made by harvesting grapes later or by retaining some of the grape’s sugar during fermentation.

When choosing a bottle, look for words such as:

  • dry;
  • off-dry;
  • sweet;
  • late harvest;
  • dessert wine.

The grape name alone is not a reliable sweetness guide. If the label is unclear, the producer’s description or a retailer’s tasting note may provide more useful information.

Why can Riesling taste so different from bottle to bottle?

Riesling’s final style is shaped by a chain of decisions and conditions:

Climate and site → ripeness and acidity → harvest timing → fermentation and maturation → finished wine.

Cooler conditions can help the grapes retain acidity and aromatic freshness. Warmer conditions may encourage riper fruit and a broader texture, although no climate produces exactly one style. Vintage conditions, vineyard site and harvest decisions matter as well.

The winemaker’s choices also influence the result. Cool, temperature-controlled fermentation in stainless steel is common for Australian Riesling because it helps preserve fresh fruit and aromatic purity. New oak is not common, although some producers use lees ageing, skin contact or alternative vessels to add texture.

Many Australian Rieslings are bottled relatively early to preserve their bright, direct character. A screwcap, also called a Stelvin closure, is very common for Australian Riesling. It can help limit oxygen exposure and preserve freshness, but it is not a guarantee of quality or a particular style.

Can Riesling age?

Some Rieslings can age very well. Their acidity provides a firm structure that can help the wine remain fresh as it develops.

Young Riesling tends to emphasise citrus, apple and floral characters. With bottle age, some examples develop honeyed, lemon-curd, nutty or toasty notes. A distinctive petroleum-like character may also appear in some aged Rieslings, although it is not present in every bottle and is not, by itself, proof that a wine is faulty or superior.

Ageing potential varies with the wine, vintage, sweetness, acidity, closure and storage conditions. Not every bottle needs to be cellared, and ageing is not a requirement for enjoying Riesling.

Australian Riesling

Riesling has an established place in Australian wine. It is grown in several parts of the country and is particularly associated with dry, cool-climate styles.

Clare Valley

Clare Valley is one of Australia’s major Riesling regions. Wines from the region are often associated with bright acidity, light body and lime or citrus flavours. Some can be enjoyed young, while well-made examples may also develop complexity with age.

Eden Valley

Eden Valley, within the Barossa Zone, is another important Australian Riesling region. Its elevated vineyard sites are associated with lemon and lime characters, fresh acidity and a steely finish. “Mineral” is sometimes used as a tasting description for these wines; it should not be read as proof that minerals from the soil directly enter the wine and create a specific flavour.

Other regions

Riesling is also produced in Tasmania, Great Southern, Canberra District, Langhorne Creek, Padthaway, the Riverland and other Australian regions. These examples show why Australian Riesling should not be reduced to one regional style.

How should a beginner choose a Riesling?

Start with the style you want rather than assuming all Riesling tastes the same.

  • For a crisp, refreshing wine, look for an Australian dry Riesling, particularly from a cool-climate region.
  • For a softer or fruitier impression, look for an off-dry description.
  • For dessert or a richer occasion, look for a sweet or late-harvest style.
  • For a more developed experience, look for a bottle with some age, remembering that older does not automatically mean better.

A regional name can offer a clue, but it is not a guarantee. The vintage, producer description and stated sweetness style can tell you more.

Riesling is usually served chilled. Dry examples can work well with oysters, shellfish, lighter fish, salads and goat’s cheese. Off-dry or sweeter examples may suit spicy dishes, pâté or soft cheese. These are starting points, not rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is Riesling a white wine?

Yes. Riesling is a white grape variety used to make white wines.

Is Riesling always dry?

No. Most Australian Riesling is dry, but the variety can also produce off-dry, sweet, dessert and occasionally sparkling wines.

Is Riesling a sweet wine?

It can be, but the grape name does not determine sweetness. Check the label or producer description for clues.

What is the difference between Riesling and other white wines?

Riesling is commonly recognised for its pronounced acidity and aromatic citrus, apple, peach and floral characters. Its style can still vary considerably with climate, ripeness, winemaking and age.

Is Riesling oaked?

New oak is uncommon in Australian Riesling. Stainless-steel fermentation is more typical, although some producers use techniques such as lees ageing or alternative vessels to build texture.

Can Riesling be aged?

Some Rieslings age very well and can develop honeyed, nutty, toasty or lemon-curd characters. Many are also enjoyable young for their fresh citrus and floral character.
