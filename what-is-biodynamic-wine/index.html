What Is Biodynamic Wine?

Learn how biodynamic wine relates to organic farming, certification and cellar practices, and what the label can and cannot tell you.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Biodynamic wine generally comes from grapes grown using biodynamic vineyard practices.
  • Biodynamic farming includes organic principles, plus additional preparations and whole-farm practices.
  • Biodynamic describes how grapes are grown—not one guaranteed flavour or wine style.
  • Biodynamic wine is not automatically natural, vegan, additive-free, low-sulphur or healthier.
  • Some biodynamic farms use lunar or cosmic calendars, but these ideas should not be confused with established scientific effects.
  • In Australia, a producer’s biodynamic claim and an independent certification mark are not necessarily the same thing.

Quick answer

Biodynamic wine is wine made from grapes grown using biodynamic viticulture: a farming system that incorporates organic requirements and adds specific preparations, compost management, biodiversity and whole-farm principles.

The word mainly tells you something about the vineyard. It does not, by itself, tell you whether the wine is red or white, what it tastes like, how much alcohol it contains or how it was made in the winery.

Biodynamic wine is related to organic wine, but it is not simply another word for organic. Biodynamic systems add practices such as specially prepared materials applied to vines, soil or compost. Some also use agricultural calendars based on lunar or cosmic timing.

The evidence for biodynamic farming is mixed and context-dependent. Some studies report benefits for soil properties or biodiversity in particular situations, but biodynamic farming is not proven to guarantee better wine, greater sustainability or a health benefit.

What does biodynamic mean in the vineyard?

Biodynamic viticulture treats the farm as an interconnected system rather than viewing the vineyard as a collection of vines. In practice, this can involve:

  • composting and careful manure management;
  • using specific biodynamic preparations;
  • encouraging biodiversity in and around the vineyard;
  • integrating animals, plants, soil and compost;
  • reducing reliance on outside inputs through closed-loop planning; and
  • following seasonal or biodynamic timing systems.

Not every biodynamic vineyard will operate in exactly the same way. The details depend on the relevant standard, certification system and producer.

A useful way to understand biodynamics is to separate practice, philosophy and evidence. Using a preparation is a documented farming practice. Seeing the property as a self-contained organism is part of the farming philosophy. Claiming that a preparation or lunar calendar reliably changes vine growth is a scientific claim—and one for which evidence remains limited or mixed.

How is biodynamic wine different from organic wine?

Organic farming generally focuses on production methods such as restrictions on synthetic fertilisers and pesticides, along with requirements for permitted inputs and processing. Biodynamic farming incorporates organic principles but adds its own preparations and whole-farm approach.

In other words:

Term What it mainly describes
Organic Organic farming and, depending on the claim, organic processing
Biodynamic Organic-style farming plus biodynamic preparations and whole-farm principles
Natural Usually a low-intervention approach to winemaking, without one universal definition
Sustainable Environmental, social or economic goals whose meaning depends on the programme or claim

These terms can overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A biodynamic wine may be made with conventional yeast, filtered, fined or stabilised if the applicable production rules permit it. A natural wine may use biodynamically farmed grapes, but natural-wine language does not prove biodynamic certification.

What are biodynamic preparations?

Biodynamic preparations are distinctive materials used as part of biodynamic farming systems. They may involve compost, manure or plant material and are prepared and applied according to a particular method or standard.

The Australian National Standard for Organic and Bio-Dynamic Produce includes requirements for biodynamic preparations, including their preparation, stirring, application and equipment. For a certified operator, these requirements are part of the production system.

Some biodynamic traditions also use lunar or cosmic calendars to time vineyard work. This is best understood as a practice or belief within some biodynamic systems, not as a scientifically established guarantee of better soil, grapes or wine.

The preparations are important to what makes biodynamics distinct. That does not mean every claimed mechanism behind them has been scientifically demonstrated.

Does biodynamic wine taste different?

Not necessarily. Biodynamic farming does not create one flavour profile.

The taste of a wine depends on many factors, including grape variety, region, climate, vintage, picking decisions, fermentation, maturation and other winemaking choices. A biodynamic vineyard can produce red, white, rosé or sparkling wine in many different styles.

Some research has reported differences in soil, vine or wine characteristics between farming systems. However, results vary between studies and locations. It is difficult to separate the effect of biodynamic preparations from the effects of composting, reduced synthetic inputs, biodiversity or the producer’s broader management.

So biodynamic wine may taste distinctive—but the label alone cannot promise that it will taste better, earthier, more expressive or more “pure”.

Is biodynamic wine healthier or more sustainable?

There is no sound basis for treating biodynamic wine as automatically healthier. Farming method does not determine alcohol content, calories, sulphite sensitivity, allergen status or hangover risk.

Biodynamic farming may include practices that support soil activity, habitat or biodiversity in some settings. But the label alone does not prove that a wine is more sustainable overall. Water use, energy, machinery, yields, packaging, transport and disease management also matter.

It is more accurate to say that biodynamics is a farming system with environmental intentions and some promising, but not universally conclusive, evidence than to call it automatically superior.

What does “biodynamic” mean on an Australian wine label?

For Australian domestic sales, certification is not universally mandatory for an organic or biodynamic claim. However, businesses must be able to substantiate claims and avoid misleading consumers under Australian Consumer Law.

The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission identifies AS 6000–2015 as a voluntary domestic standard for organic and biodynamic products. Private certification organisations may use their own standards, marks and certification scopes.

Australia also has a National Standard for Organic and Bio-Dynamic Produce. It is mandatory for relevant Australian products intended for export and includes biodynamic requirements and a wine-production section.

When reading a label, distinguish between:

  • “Certified biodynamic” — look for the named certifier or certification mark.
  • A certification logo — check which organisation issued it and what its standard covers.
  • “Biodynamically farmed” — this may describe vineyard practice without proving certification of the finished wine.
  • A producer’s own description — potentially meaningful, but not the same as independent certification.

The Bio-Dynamic Research Institute manages the Demeter trademark in Australia and certifies operators under its stated system. A certification mark can provide useful information, but it is still worth understanding whether it applies to the grapes, the wine, or both.

What biodynamic wine does not automatically mean

Biodynamic wine is not automatically:

  • natural or low-intervention;
  • vegan;
  • additive-free;
  • sulphur-free or low-sulphur;
  • unfiltered or unfined; or
  • healthier.

Certification rules may allow particular cellar additions and processing methods. If you need to know about sulphur, fining agents, filtration or dietary suitability, check the producer’s technical information rather than assuming from the word “biodynamic”.

Frequently asked questions

Is biodynamic wine organic?

Biodynamic systems generally incorporate organic requirements and add further practices. However, the exact certification claim depends on the relevant standard and certifier.

Is biodynamic wine the same as natural wine?

No. Biodynamic mainly describes vineyard farming. Natural usually refers to a low-intervention approach to winemaking, and the term does not have one universal definition in Australia.

Is biodynamic wine always certified?

No. Domestic certification is voluntary, although claims must be substantiated and must not mislead. A certified claim and a producer’s own description should be treated as different forms of information.

Does biodynamic wine contain no sulphites?

No. Biodynamic status does not automatically mean sulphite-free. Check the wine’s technical information if this matters to you.

Should I buy biodynamic wine?

If the farming approach interests you, look for a clear description of the producer’s practices and any named certification. Then choose the wine by its grape variety, region and style—as you would with any other bottle. Biodynamic tells you something important about how the vineyard is managed, but it does not decide whether you will enjoy the wine.
