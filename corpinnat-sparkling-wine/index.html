Corpinnat sparkling wine: a beginner’s guide

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Corpinnat is a collective brand for sparkling wines from the historic Penedès area of Catalonia, Spain.
  • It is not a grape variety, a generic term for Spanish sparkling wine or another name for Cava.
  • Corpinnat’s current published rules emphasise organic vineyards, hand harvesting, defined Penedès origin, winery control, bottle ageing and external audits.
  • Historic Penedès varieties such as Xarel·lo, Macabeu and Parellada are central to its identity.
  • Corpinnat wines are made using the traditional method, but “traditional method” describes production—not the wine’s regional or collective identity.
  • Corpinnat and Cava are separate systems, even though both are associated with Spanish sparkling wine and may share geographic connections.
  • The name tells you about origin and production requirements; it does not guarantee one universal flavour or quality level.

Quick answer: what is Corpinnat?

Corpinnat is a European Union collective brand for sparkling wines made in the Penedès area of Catalonia, Spain. The name identifies wines made under the collective brand’s requirements for grape origin, farming, harvesting, winemaking, ageing and auditing.

That makes Corpinnat different from three related terms:

  • Penedès is the geographic context.
  • Traditional method is the production method, involving a second fermentation in the bottle and subsequent bottle ageing.
  • Corpinnat is the specific collective identity applied to qualifying wines.

Corpinnat is not a Spanish Denominación de Origen, DOP, PDO or generic synonym for Cava. It is best understood as a defined Penedès sparkling-wine identity operating through a collective brand.

Where does Corpinnat come from?

Corpinnat’s territory is in the historic Penedès, within Catalonia in north-eastern Spain. The collective brand describes its territory as covering about 997 square kilometres and all or part of 46 municipalities across Alt Penedès, Baix Penedès, Alt Camp, Baix Llobregat and Anoia.

Those figures describe the territory defined by the Corpinnat organisation. They should not automatically be read as a statutory geographical-indication boundary equivalent to the Cava designation.

For an Australian wine learner, the useful label hierarchy is:

Spain → Catalonia → Penedès → Corpinnat

A bottle made in Catalonia or the Penedès is not automatically Corpinnat. The word “Corpinnat” communicates a more specific collective-brand identity.

What rules shape Corpinnat wines?

Corpinnat’s published requirements are designed to connect the wine with the Penedès and give the collective brand control over how it is made.

Current or recently published requirements include:

  • grapes must come from the defined Corpinnat territory;
  • supplying vineyards must be certified organic;
  • grapes must be harvested by hand;
  • each winery must own at least one hectare of vineyard;
  • where grapes are purchased, the winery must maintain a stable grower relationship, including a contract of at least three years;
  • vinification must take place at the property or within the winery’s own operation;
  • sparkling wines must be aged for at least 18 months before sale;
  • wineries undergo at least three external audits each year, with Bureau Veritas identified as the external certification body.

These are production and certification requirements, not tasting promises. Organic farming does not mean a wine is automatically biodynamic, natural or environmentally superior. Similarly, longer ageing does not mean every bottle will taste the same.

Which grapes are used?

The 2024 Corpinnat profile identifies a group of historic varieties that must make up at least 90 per cent of the blend. They include:

  • Xarel·lo
  • Macabeu
  • Parellada
  • Malvasia Subirat Parent
  • Garnatxa Tinta
  • Monastrell
  • Sumoll
  • Xarel·lo Vermell

The same published rules allow up to 10 per cent of other authorised varieties, including Malvasia de Sitges, Trepat, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir.

This is an important distinction: the rules describe what is permitted, while individual wines can still differ in blend and style. Xarel·lo is strongly associated with the Penedès, but not every Corpinnat wine must be presented as the same grape combination.

A 19 August 2026 harvest report states that Chardonnay and Pinot Noir will no longer be permitted from the 2034 harvest onwards. Because this is a future and potentially changing rule, it should be checked against the latest regulations when buying or assessing a later release.

Corpinnat and the traditional method

Corpinnat wines are made as sparkling wines using the traditional method. In simple terms, the wine undergoes a second fermentation in the bottle, creating its bubbles, followed by bottle ageing.

The method explains how the sparkling wine is made. Corpinnat explains which collective identity and requirements the wine belongs to.

So:

  • a Corpinnat wine can be traditional-method sparkling wine;
  • not every traditional-method sparkling wine is Corpinnat;
  • “traditional method” alone does not guarantee organic grapes, Penedès origin, Corpinnat certification or a minimum 18 months’ ageing.

For a neutral explanation of the production process, see traditional-method sparkling wine and traditional-method sparkling wine production.

How is Corpinnat different from Cava?

Cava is a separate Spanish protected designation with its own legal framework and regulatory council. Corpinnat is a European Union collective brand operated under its own rules.

The two identities are connected historically. Corpinnat developed from the Penedès sparkling-wine sector, and its founding producers included wineries that left the Cava system after disagreements about how their regional and production requirements could be communicated.

But they are not interchangeable terms:

  • Cava means the wine qualifies under the Cava designation.
  • Corpinnat means the wine belongs to the Corpinnat collective brand and meets its requirements.
  • Traditional method describes a production technique that can apply to either system—or to sparkling wine from elsewhere.

This difference does not make one category automatically better than the other. It means they communicate different regulatory and collective identities.

You can read more about the broader category in Cava and Cava wine regions.

What does Corpinnat taste like?

There is no single Corpinnat flavour profile. Wines vary according to grape blend, producer, vintage, dosage and ageing.

Historic Penedès varieties may give the wines a strong sense of regional identity. Bottle ageing can also contribute developed flavours and textures, which may include savoury, nutty, bread-like or creamy notes. These are tendencies to look for, not guarantees.

The most useful approach is to treat the label as information about origin and production commitments, then allow the individual wine to show its own character. Corpinnat does not automatically mean dry, rich, mineral, Champagne-like or superior to other sparkling wines.

How should Australians read the label?

If you encounter an imported bottle in Australia, read the key terms as separate layers:

  • Corpinnat: a specific collective-brand identity with defined Penedès origin and production requirements.
  • Cava: a separate Spanish protected designation.
  • Traditional method: a description of how the bubbles were produced.
  • Penedès: geographic context, but not automatic proof that the wine is Corpinnat.

A wine made in Catalonia is not automatically Corpinnat, and a traditional-method sparkling wine from another country is not automatically Cava or Corpinnat. Australian labelling and retail practice should not be assumed to be identical to the systems used in Spain or the European Union.

Australian learners may encounter Corpinnat on imported bottles, specialist lists or restaurant wine lists, but availability varies. If you see the term, the simplest question to ask is: Is this describing the place, the method, or the collective brand? In Corpinnat’s case, it is primarily the collective brand, with Penedès origin and production rules behind it.

The takeaway

Corpinnat identifies a particular sparkling-wine model from the Penedès: organic grapes, hand harvesting, historic regional varieties, winery control, bottle ageing and external auditing under a collective European Union brand.

It sits alongside Cava rather than inside it, and it uses the traditional method without being synonymous with that method. For a drinker, the name is a useful guide to origin and production requirements—but the wine’s actual taste will still depend on the blend, producer, vintage, dosage and ageing.

Frequently asked questions

Is Corpinnat the same as Cava?

No. Cava is a Spanish protected designation, while Corpinnat is a separate European Union collective brand with its own requirements.

Is Corpinnat a grape variety?

No. Corpinnat is a collective identity for qualifying sparkling wines. Its rules centre on historic Penedès grape varieties, including Xarel·lo, Macabeu and Parellada.

Is Corpinnat always organic?

The current published Corpinnat requirements state that supplying vineyards must be certified organic. This describes the collective brand’s production rules, not a guarantee that every wine is biodynamic or “natural”.

Is Corpinnat traditional-method sparkling wine?

Yes, Corpinnat wines are made using the traditional method. However, traditional method is a production description, not a synonym for Corpinnat.
