What does flabby wine mean?

Learn what “flabby” means in a wine tasting note, how acidity and balance shape the impression, and why it differs from soft, sweet, full-bodied or faulty.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Flabby is a negative tasting term for wine that feels broad, soft, flat or lacking freshness.
  • Low perceived acidity is often part of the impression, but low acidity alone does not automatically make a wine flabby.
  • A flabby wine can be dry, sweet, full-bodied, low in tannin or faulty—but none of those words means the same thing as flabby.
  • Soft, round, plush and broad can be positive descriptions. They become negative when the wine lacks lift and definition.
  • “Flabby” describes how a wine feels and tastes. It is not a precise chemical measurement or formal Australian label category.

Quick answer

A flabby wine feels soft, broad and lacking in freshness or shape on the palate. The wine’s acidity often does not provide enough lift or tension for its other components, such as alcohol, sweetness, fruit weight or tannin.

Imagine a wine that spreads across your palate but does not make your mouth water or leave a focused, energetic finish. That is the kind of impression a taster might call flabby.

The word is a judgement about balance, not a diagnosis with a fixed pH or acidity threshold. A wine with low acidity may still be enjoyable and balanced, while a wine with plenty of acidity can have other problems. Context matters.

What does flabby wine taste like?

On the first sip, a flabby wine may seem gentle or generous rather than sharp and lively. Through the middle of the palate, its fruit, alcohol, sweetness and texture may feel broad or heavy without enough definition.

The finish can be the clearest clue. Instead of freshness carrying through, the wine may fade quickly, feel loose or leave a warm, dull impression.

These are not strict tasting-test results. Wine vocabulary is comparative: a taster is judging how the wine’s parts work together and comparing the sensation with other wines.

Is flabby the same as soft, sweet or full-bodied?

No. These terms describe different things:

  • Soft can be positive, suggesting a smooth, gentle texture. It becomes negative when the wine also seems dull or shapeless.
  • Round or plush usually suggests generous fruit and a smooth, full texture. A round wine can still be fresh and balanced.
  • Broad describes how widely the wine spreads across the palate. Broadness may be attractive when the wine has enough freshness; it may feel flabby when it does not.
  • Sweet describes perceived sweetness, usually related to residual sugar. A dry wine can be flabby, and a sweet wine can have lively acidity.
  • Low-tannin describes a wine with little perceived grip, especially a red. Low tannin does not mean low acidity.
  • Full-bodied describes weight or volume on the palate. A full-bodied wine can also be fresh, structured and balanced.

In short, flabby is about a lack of perceived lift and definition, not one individual characteristic.

How can you taste for flabbiness?

Try this simple three-part check:

  1. First sip: Does the wine feel lively and mouth-watering, or does it arrive softly without much lift?
  2. Mid-palate: Do the fruit, alcohol, sweetness, acidity and tannin feel integrated, or does the wine seem broad and heavy without shape?
  3. Finish: Does freshness continue, or does the wine quickly become loose, warm or dull?

Serving temperature, food, glassware and your own sensitivity can change how balance feels. For example, a rich meal may make a wine’s freshness seem particularly noticeable—or make a wine lacking freshness seem heavier by comparison.

Is flabby wine faulty?

Not necessarily. Flabby is a sensory description, not a diagnosis of cork taint, oxidation, Brettanomyces, volatile acidity or another specific fault. A wine may be flabby without being spoiled, and the word alone cannot tell you why the wine feels that way.

In Australia, “flabby” is ordinary tasting vocabulary rather than a formal wine-label category or regulated definition. You are most likely to encounter it in a tasting note, review or conversation about a wine’s structure.

Frequently asked questions

Can a dry wine be flabby?

Yes. Dryness and freshness are different sensations. A dry wine can seem flabby if it lacks enough perceived acidity or structural definition.

Is flabby wine undrinkable?

No. “Flabby” is a negative assessment of balance, but taste is personal and the impression can change with serving conditions and food. It simply tells you that the taster found the wine lacking lift or shape.

Does flabby mean the wine has low acidity?

Low acidity is often associated with flabbiness, but it is not an automatic rule. The overall impression also depends on the wine’s alcohol, sweetness, fruit concentration, tannin and other components.
