Can wine be frozen?

Yes, wine can freeze—but a full bottle may leak, damage its closure or break. Learn how freezing affects wine and the safest way to handle leftovers.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Yes, wine can freeze.
  • It freezes below 0 °C because alcohol, sugar and other dissolved substances lower its freezing point.
  • A full glass bottle can leak, push out its cork, damage its screw cap or break as the water in the wine expands.
  • Freezing may change the wine’s aroma, flavour, balance or texture.
  • Leftover wine is safer to freeze in a freezer-safe container with room for expansion.
  • Frozen bottled wine is not the same as Icewine, which is made from grapes naturally frozen on the vine.

Quick answer

Yes, wine can be frozen—but freezing a full bottle is risky and may reduce its drinking quality.

Wine does not all freeze at one identical temperature. Its alcohol level, sugar and other dissolved compounds affect when ice begins to form. In a household freezer, some wines may become slushy or partly frozen rather than turning into a solid block.

The biggest practical risk is the container. As water freezes, it expands. In a full, sealed glass bottle, that expansion can force out a cork, compromise a screw-cap seal, cause leakage or, in extreme cases, break the bottle.

A bottle that has accidentally frozen is not automatically unsafe simply because it froze. However, inspect it carefully after thawing. If the glass is cracked, the closure is damaged or the wine has been contaminated, do not drink it. Even if the bottle remains intact, the wine may no longer taste as it did before.

Why does wine freeze below 0 °C?

Pure water freezes at 0 °C, but wine is a mixture of water, alcohol and other dissolved substances. Alcohol and sugar lower the freezing point, so wine generally needs to become colder than water before ice starts to form.

Freezing is often gradual. The first ice crystals are mostly water, leaving the unfrozen liquid more concentrated in alcohol and other dissolved compounds. That is why wine can become thick or slushy, rather than freezing evenly all at once.

The exact freezing point varies from wine to wine, so a single temperature is not a reliable rule for every bottle.

What happens to a full bottle?

Avoid deliberately putting a full glass bottle in the freezer. There may not be enough empty space—known as headspace—to accommodate expansion.

The result could be a raised cork, a leaking bottle or a damaged screw-cap seal. Sparkling wine deserves particular caution because it is already under pressure. Never assume that a screw cap makes a bottle immune to freezing damage.

If you discover a frozen bottle, handle it gently. Let it thaw in the refrigerator, then check the bottle and closure for cracks, leakage or other damage before opening it. Do not use heat or hot water to speed up the process.

Can leftover wine be frozen?

If you want to freeze leftover wine, transfer it to a freezer-safe container or an ice-cube tray rather than freezing the original bottle. Leave room at the top for expansion and seal the container to prevent spills and odour transfer.

Thawed wine may be perfectly usable, but freezing is not a dependable way to preserve its original drinking quality. Aroma, flavour, balance or texture may change, and the wine may be less enjoyable on its own. Frozen leftover wine can be a practical ingredient for cooking, where small changes in character are usually less important.

There is no universal post-thaw storage life that applies to every wine. Once thawed, treat it as a quality-sensitive leftover and use your judgement.

Is frozen wine the same as Icewine?

No. Icewine, or Eiswein, is made from grapes that freeze naturally on the vine. The frozen grapes are pressed while still frozen, concentrating their juice before fermentation.

That is very different from placing finished bottled wine in a freezer. Freezing a bottle does not turn it into Icewine.

FAQs

Does freezing wine make it poisonous?

Freezing alone does not prove that wine has become unsafe. The more immediate concerns are bottle damage, a compromised seal and quality changes. Do not drink wine from a cracked, leaking or contaminated container.

Is thawed wine still drinkable?

It may be. If the bottle and closure are intact, thaw the wine gently in the refrigerator and assess its appearance, aroma and flavour. It may be better suited to casual use or cooking if its quality has changed.

Can I freeze wine in an ice-cube tray?

Yes, provided the tray or container is freezer-safe and you leave room for expansion. This is more suitable for leftover wine than freezing a full glass bottle.
