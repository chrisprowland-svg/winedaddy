What is Ruchè?

Ruchè is a Piedmont red grape known for dry, aromatic, medium-bodied wines. Learn the difference between the grape and Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato DOCG.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Ruchè (pronounced here with its Italian spelling) is a red wine-grape variety from Piedmont in north-west Italy. It is best known in the wine Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato DOCG, a protected regional designation associated with seven municipalities in Asti province.

In simple terms: Ruchè is the grape; Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato is a regulated wine designation. They are related, but they are not the same thing.

Highlights

  • Ruchè is a red grape variety.
  • Its preferred spelling is Ruchè, with a grave accent on the final “è”.
  • It is principally associated with Piedmont and Asti province in north-west Italy.
  • Wines made in the classic regional style are usually dry, aromatic and medium-bodied.
  • Floral, fruity and spicy impressions may occur, but no bottle will taste exactly the same.
  • Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato DOCG is a protected designation, not another name for the grape.
  • It is an unusual variety for many Australian wine drinkers, rather than a mainstream label style.

What does Ruchè taste like?

The recognised Ruchè style is generally dry, rounded and medium-bodied, with an aromatic character. You may find fruity, floral or lightly spicy impressions, along with modest tannin. Some examples can also show characters from maturation in wood.

That does not mean Ruchè is always sweet or tastes like a particular list of fruits and flowers. A wine can smell strongly of fruit and still be dry. In the DOCG expression, the wine is specified as secco, or dry.

The finished wine can change according to vineyard site, vintage conditions, grape ripeness, extraction, maturation and blending. Treat the usual descriptions as useful starting points rather than a promise about every bottle.

Where does Ruchè come from?

Ruchè is strongly associated with Piedmont, the Italian region known in English as Piedmont and in Italian as Piemonte. Its most important geographic association is the province of Asti, particularly the area around Castagnole Monferrato.

This is where the protected name Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato DOCG comes from. The designation refers to wine made under defined rules in a particular area. It does not mean that Castagnole Monferrato is the name of the grape.

The regional history is not completely certain. Piedmont’s official information describes Ruchè as an indigenous variety but also notes that detailed historical documentation is limited. It is therefore safer to say that the grape is associated with Piedmont than to claim a precisely proven ancient origin or a definite etymology.

What does the label mean?

If you see Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato DOCG on a label, you are looking at a regulated designation. The wine must contain at least 90% Ruchè, with Barbera and/or Brachetto permitted to make up the remaining proportion, up to 10%.

A wine made from Ruchè elsewhere may use the grape name without being entitled to that protected designation. This distinction is useful when reading a label: a grape name tells you what variety is involved, while a protected regional name can also indicate where and under what rules the wine was made.

How should you serve and pair Ruchè?

Try Ruchè with savoury food that will not overwhelm its fragrance or medium body. Good starting points include savoury pasta, mushroom dishes, poultry, pork and moderately mature hard or savoury cheeses.

Traditional Piedmont guidance includes regional dishes and cheeses such as Castelmagno and Grana Padano. These are suggestions, not rules. The best pairing depends on the particular bottle and the intensity of the dish.

For Australian drinkers, current national data on Ruchè plantings and availability was not verified for this guide. You may encounter it as a specialist imported wine or an occasional alternative-variety offering, but it should not be assumed to be widely grown or consistently available.

Is Ruchè the same as Ruche?

Ruchè is the preferred specialist spelling. Ruche without the accent may appear in English-language writing, databases or URLs, usually as a simplified version of the same name. It should not automatically be treated as a separate grape.

A definitive grape-specific pronunciation was not verified, so it is better not to rely on a rigid phonetic spelling. If you are ordering it, saying “Ruchè” and pointing to the label is perfectly practical.

Is Ruchè a sweet wine?

Usually not. The classic Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato DOCG style is dry. Its aromatic, fruity character can make it seem generous, but fragrance and fruit flavour do not necessarily mean sweetness.

Is Ruchè a region?

No. Ruchè is a grape variety. Ruchè di Castagnole Monferrato is the name of a protected wine designation associated with part of Piedmont.
