# What is Hondarrabi Zuri?

Hondarrabi Zuri is a white wine-grape variety from the Basque Country in northern Spain. It is best known as the principal white grape associated with Txakoli, also spelled Txakolina—the fresh regional wine style and protected wine designations linked with the Basque Country.

## Highlights

- **Hondarrabi Zuri is a grape variety**, not a wine region or style.
- It is strongly associated with **Txakoli and Txakolina**.
- Wines made with it are commonly fresh, bright and noticeably acidic.
- A bottle may show **Getariako Txakolina**, **Bizkaiko Txakolina** or **Arabako Txakolina** rather than the grape name.
- **Hondarrabi Beltza** is a separate red Hondarrabi variety.
- Spelling varies, so you may also see **Hondarribi Zuri** or **Ondarrabi Zuri**.

## Where does Hondarrabi Zuri come from?

Hondarrabi Zuri is associated with the Basque Country, or Euskadi, in northern Spain. Spain’s official variety and geographical-indication records list it as a principal white grape in three Basque protected designations:

- **Getariako Txakolina**, associated with Gipuzkoa
- **Bizkaiko Txakolina**, associated with Biscay
- **Arabako Txakolina**, associated with Álava

A protected designation is a regulated regional name. In European wine terminology, it is similar to a promise about where a wine comes from and the production rules it follows—not simply a grape name.

## Is Hondarrabi Zuri the same as Txakoli?

No. The easiest way to remember the difference is:

- **Hondarrabi Zuri** = the grape
- **Txakoli or Txakolina** = the associated regional wine identity
- **Getariako Txakolina, Bizkaiko Txakolina and Arabako Txakolina** = specific protected regional designations

Hondarrabi Zuri is the main white grape associated with Txakoli, but not every Txakoli wine has to be made entirely from it. The designations can also permit other grape varieties under their production rules.

For an Australian wine drinker, this explains why a bottle or menu may highlight “Txakoli” rather than “Hondarrabi Zuri”. European protected-origin labelling does not always work like Australian varietal labelling, where the grape is often prominent on the front label.

## What does Hondarrabi Zuri taste like?

There is no single fixed flavour profile, but wines associated with Hondarrabi Zuri are commonly fresh and acid-driven. You may find:

- prominent or lively acidity;
- moderate or restrained alcohol;
- a youthful, clean style;
- white-fruit, citrus, floral or herbal impressions.

Some Txakoli wines can also have a slight spritz, but this is not a rule for every wine made with Hondarrabi Zuri. The finished style depends on the designation, vineyard site, vintage, harvest decisions, blending and winemaking.

The official Getariako Txakolina material describes its wines as young, moderately alcoholic and balanced in acidity. For that regional style, it recommends serving the wine chilled, around **6–10°C**.

## Names that can cause confusion

The main spelling for this page is **Hondarrabi Zuri**, although **Hondarribi Zuri** and **Ondarrabi Zuri** appear in published wine material.

The similar-looking **Hondarrabi Beltza** is not another name for Hondarrabi Zuri. It is a separately listed red variety. Nor should Hondarrabi Zuri be confused with **Hondarrabi Zuri Zerratia**, also known as Petit Courbu, which is separately listed in Txakoli regulations.

## How should you serve it?

Serve a fresh Hondarrabi Zuri or Txakoli-based wine chilled. Its acidity makes it a natural starting point with seafood, shellfish, fried fish, salty snacks and other light dishes. Think of this as a useful pairing direction rather than a strict rule: individual wines can differ considerably.

If you see **Txakoli**, **Txakolina** or one of the three protected designation names on a list or label, think “Basque regional wine associated with Hondarrabi Zuri”—not “a completely different grape”.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Hondarrabi Zuri a white wine?

Hondarrabi Zuri is a white wine grape. The wines made from it are generally white, fresh and high in acidity.

### Is Txakoli always made from Hondarrabi Zuri?

No. Hondarrabi Zuri is the principal white grape associated with Txakoli, but the protected designations may allow other varieties as well.

### Is Hondarrabi Zuri the same as Hondarrabi Beltza?

No. Hondarrabi Beltza is a distinct red Hondarrabi variety.
