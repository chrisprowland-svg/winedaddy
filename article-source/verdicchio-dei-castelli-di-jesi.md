# Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi: a beginner’s guide

## Highlights

- Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi is a protected Italian wine designation from Marche.
- It is made principally from the Verdicchio grape.
- The appellation covers a defined area in Ancona and Macerata provinces—not just the town of Jesi.
- The standard designation is Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC, also recognised as a European PDO.
- Classico, Superiore, Spumante and Passito are regulated category terms, not guaranteed promises of a particular flavour.
- The wine is generally dry, fresh and harmonious, with official descriptions that include a pale colour, almond association and pleasantly bitter finish.
- “Verdicchio” alone does not necessarily mean Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi.

## Quick answer

Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi is a place-based Italian white-wine designation from the Marche region. The wine is made principally from Verdicchio, a white grape variety, but the full name tells you more than the grape: it identifies a legally defined growing area and a set of production rules.

The appellation’s official European status is **PDO**, or Protected Designation of Origin. In Italian terminology, you will commonly see **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC**. Its production area includes defined municipalities in Ancona Province and two in Macerata Province.

The name can be confusing because **Verdicchio** is both the grape and a broader wine reference. A bottle labelled simply “Verdicchio” is not automatically from Castelli di Jesi. Look for the complete geographic name if you want this particular appellation.

In the glass, expect a dry white wine with noticeable freshness and a distinctive finish. Official descriptions refer to pale straw-yellow colour, a delicate aroma, a harmonious palate, almond-like character and a pleasantly bitter aftertaste. Individual wines can vary with vineyard site, vintage and winemaking.

## Where is Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi?

Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi is in **Marche**, on Italy’s Adriatic side. Its formal geographic hierarchy is:

**Italy → Marche → Ancona and Macerata provinces → defined municipalities → Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi PDO/DOC**

Jesi is the most useful practical orientation point because it gives the appellation its historical name. It is also a helpful regional centre for understanding the surrounding Esino valley and hill country. However, Jesi is not the whole appellation, and the current legal municipality list does not include the city of Jesi itself.

The defined area includes places such as Cupramontana, Staffolo, Castelbellino, Castelplanio, Montecarotto, San Paolo di Jesi, Serra San Quirico, Senigallia, Apiro and Cingoli. These towns help orient you, but no single town defines the entire GI.

For a traveller, Ancona is the nearest major-city reference. The area is broadly west to south-west of Ancona. A drive to Jesi is roughly 35–45 minutes, while inland destinations may take longer. Treat those times as broad orientation only: the exact destination, route and traffic matter.

## Verdicchio the grape versus Verdicchio the appellation

This is the key distinction.

**Verdicchio** is the grape variety. **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi** is the regulated wine name connecting that grape with a defined place and production rules. Under the current evidence, the wine must be predominantly Verdicchio: at least 85% Verdicchio is supported, with up to 15% other authorised white grapes from Marche.

That means the geographic name matters. A wine made from Verdicchio outside the defined area cannot automatically use the Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi designation.

You can learn about the variety itself in the separate [Verdicchio guide](/verdicchio/). That page and this one are related, but they answer different questions: one is about the grape; this one is about the place-based wine identity.

## What does DOC and PDO mean here?

**DOC** means *Denominazione di Origine Controllata*, Italy’s controlled designation terminology. **PDO** is the European Union term, often shown in English as Protected Designation of Origin. They describe the regulated origin system from different levels.

For the main appellation, the useful shorthand is:

- **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC** in Italian classification language
- **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi PDO/DOP** in European designation language

Do not confuse this with **Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva**, a separate related DOCG designation with its own specification. “Riserva” is not simply another spelling of the standard DOC. The [Italian DOC and DOCG guide](/italian-doc-and-docg/) explains the classification background in more detail.

## Styles and label terms to know

The appellation includes several regulated categories:

- **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi:** the standard still-wine category
- **Classico:** a regulated category associated with the historic core of the appellation
- **Superiore:** a regulated category with separate requirements
- **Classico Superiore:** combines those category terms
- **Spumante:** quality sparkling wine
- **Spumante Riserva:** a sparkling Riserva category
- **Passito:** a style made using raisined grapes

Classico and Superiore should not be read as automatic guarantees that a wine will taste “better”. They provide geographic or regulatory information, but the flavour still depends on the wine, vintage and producer’s choices.

You may also see **Marche** on a label under specified conditions, and **Vigna** followed by a place or vineyard name under applicable rules. These terms do not replace the appellation name.

Recent European and Italian records include amendments from 2025 and 2026. Classification and label rules should therefore be checked against the operative specification when buying a current release.

## What does it taste like?

The safest starting point is a dry, pale white wine with freshness and a distinctive finish. Official descriptions support:

- pale straw-yellow colour;
- delicate and distinctive aroma;
- dry, harmonious flavour;
- almond association;
- a pleasantly bitter aftertaste.

That bitter note is not necessarily a fault. In context, it can provide shape and contrast at the end of the palate.

Do not expect every bottle to taste identical. The appellation includes inland and hill-country sites rather than one uniform vineyard. Elevation, exposure, soil, vintage and winemaking can all influence the result. Some wines may feel lighter and fresher, while others may have more texture or concentration. Those are tendencies, not guarantees.

## Food pairing for Australian tables

A dry, fresh white wine with a firm finish is a natural starting point for seafood. Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi can often work well with oysters, prawns, calamari, grilled fish and simply prepared white fish. Fresh salads and herb-led dishes are also sensible options.

Think of these as pairing suggestions rather than rules. Preparation, sauce and spice can matter as much as the main ingredient. The existing [seafood pairing guide](/wine-and-seafood-pairing/) and [oysters guide](/wine-with-oysters/) can help with those decisions.

For Australian buyers, range and naming can vary by importer, retailer and vintage. The most useful label habit is to look for the full name **Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi**, rather than assuming that “Verdicchio” alone identifies this appellation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi a grape or a region?

It is a regulated wine designation. Verdicchio is the grape; Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi is the place-based appellation wine.

### Is Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi the same as Verdicchio di Matelica?

No. Verdicchio di Matelica is a separate Marche appellation based on the same principal grape.

### Does Classico mean the wine is better?

Not automatically. Classico is a regulated geographic or category term, not a universal quality ranking.

### Is Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi always sparkling?

No. Still wine is the standard form, but the appellation also recognises Spumante and Spumante Riserva categories.

### Is Passito dry?

Passito is a raisined-grape category and is associated with a sweet or dessert style. Individual labels and current rules should be checked for the exact wine.
