# How to pour wine properly

## Highlights

- Use a clean glass and keep it steady on the table.
- Pour still wine slowly and in a controlled stream.
- Leave space above the wine; never feel obliged to fill the glass to the rim.
- Finish by lifting the bottle smoothly and checking for drips.
- Pour sparkling wine more slowly to manage the foam.
- Sedimented wine needs a gentler pour, with attention to the bottle’s neck.
- Good pouring is about control and hospitality, not performance.

## Quick answer

To pour wine neatly, place the bottle and glass securely, keep the glass still, and tip the bottle gradually. Pour into the bowl of the glass in a controlled stream, stopping while there is still plenty of room above the wine. Then lift the bottle smoothly and check the bottle lip for drips.

There is no single correct amount for every glass of wine. The right practical pour depends on the glass, the wine style and the setting. A glass should be comfortable to hold and swirl without spilling, rather than filled to the rim.

## How to pour still wine

### 1. Prepare the bottle and glass

Use a clean glass with enough room for the intended pour. Place it on a stable table or surface. A stemmed glass is usually held by the stem or base when drinking, but there is no required hand position for pouring.

If the bottle has been opened, check the lip and neck for stray cork or residue before you begin.

### 2. Keep the glass steady

For ordinary still wine, leave the glass on the table while pouring. This gives you better control and avoids trying to aim at a moving target.

Hold the bottle securely enough to regulate the flow. You do not need to copy a formal sommelier grip. The useful principle is simple: choose a position that feels stable and lets you see how quickly the wine is running.

### 3. Pour slowly

Tip the bottle gradually and pour in a smooth stream. Aim towards the inside of the bowl rather than splashing wine against the rim. As the wine approaches the level you want, ease the flow rather than allowing the final part of the pour to rush.

Leave headspace — the empty area between the wine and the rim. It makes the glass easier to carry and gives the drinker room to smell and, if appropriate, gently swirl the wine.

### 4. Finish cleanly

When you have poured enough, lift the bottle smoothly. A slight turn can help some people reduce drips, but it is an optional technique, not a rule or ritual.

Check the bottle lip. If wine has run down the neck, wipe it with a clean cloth before continuing. If someone would like more wine, it is usually neater to top up a partly filled glass than to overfill it at the start.

## How much wine should you pour?

Do not treat a particular millilitre figure as the universal “correct” pour. Wine-education guidance recognises that serving amounts vary with the setting and the glass. In Australia, use millilitres when discussing quantity, but remember that a practical glass pour is not automatically a venue measure or a standard-drink calculation.

As a visual guide, stop well below the rim. The wine should sit comfortably in the bowl, with enough room to handle the glass without sloshing.

## When the method changes

### Sparkling wine

Sparkling wine produces foam, so pour more slowly than you would for still wine. Champagne’s official industry guidance recommends a slow, regular pour in two stages, with the glass filled to about two-thirds. That is useful Champagne-service guidance, not a universal rule for every sparkling wine or every Australian household.

### Wine with sediment

Some older or lightly filtered wines can contain sediment. Pour these gently and watch the bottle neck as the wine leaves the bottle. Stop when the deposit begins approaching the neck rather than allowing it to flow into the glass.

If the wine has already been decanted, pour from the decanter steadily and avoid splashing or overfilling. Detailed decanting is a separate skill; the key here is the same controlled approach.

## Common pouring problems

**The wine drips:** Slow the final part of the pour, lift the bottle smoothly and check the lip. Wipe away any residue before serving another glass.

**The pour is running too fast:** Lower the bottle angle slightly and pause. It is easier to add more wine than to rescue an overfilled glass.

**The glass is nearly full:** Stop. A little less wine is easier to carry, smell and enjoy than a glass filled to the brim.

**A bottle has visible sediment:** Pour gently and stop before the sediment reaches the neck.

The best pour is the one that leaves the drinker with a comfortable glass and the host without a spill.

## Suggested FAQs

### Should I pour wine into a tilted glass?

For ordinary still wine, keeping the glass on the table is the simplest option. Sparkling-wine service can follow different conventions, so do not apply one method to every wine.

### Should I rotate the bottle at the end?

A slight turn may help reduce drips, but it is optional. A controlled lift and a quick check of the bottle lip matter more than performing an exact movement.

### Should I fill a wine glass to the top?

No. Leave room above the wine so the glass is easier to handle and the wine can be smelled comfortably.
