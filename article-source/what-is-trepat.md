# What is Trepat?

## Highlights

- Trepat is a red wine-grape variety from Catalonia, Spain.
- It is especially associated with the Conca de Barberà wine region.
- It can make light, fresh, red-fruited red wines and rosés.
- Trepat is also an authorised grape for Cava, particularly rosé Cava.
- “Trepat” names the grape; “Cava” names a regulated sparkling-wine designation.
- Its style is not fixed: ripeness, vineyard conditions, blending and winemaking all matter.

## Quick answer: what is Trepat?

Trepat is a red wine grape associated most strongly with Catalonia, particularly the Conca de Barberà designation of origin in Tarragona province.

It is used in several ways. As a still wine, Trepat can produce red wines that are relatively light, fresh and fruity. It is also used for rosé, where it can contribute bright colour and red-fruit character. Trepat is one of the authorised grape varieties for Cava and is especially relevant to rosé Cava.

The important distinction is that **Trepat is a grape variety, not a general style of wine**. A wine labelled Trepat identifies the grape. A wine labelled Cava identifies a regulated sparkling-wine designation. A bottle mentioning Conca de Barberà identifies a regional wine context. These names can appear together, but they do not mean the same thing.

## Where is Trepat from?

Trepat is a Catalan variety from north-eastern Spain. Its strongest regional association is with **Conca de Barberà**, an inland wine region in Tarragona province, west of Barcelona and closer to Tarragona.

Conca de Barberà is known for Trepat as a characteristic local variety. The grape is also associated with other Catalan wine areas, including Costers del Segre and Penedès. These are separate regional designations, not different names for Conca de Barberà.

For a drinker, the label distinction is useful:

- **Catalonia** is the broader geographic and cultural region.
- **Conca de Barberà** is a protected regional wine designation.
- **Trepat** is the grape variety.
- **Cava** is a regulated sparkling-wine designation.

Trepat may therefore appear in a still wine from a Catalan region, a rosé from Conca de Barberà, or a sparkling rosé made under the Cava designation.

## What names might you see?

**Trepat** is the safest and most widely useful name for the red variety.

You may also encounter **Trepat Negre**, meaning black or red Trepat, particularly where it needs to be distinguished from a white Trepat reference. Synonym lists are not completely consistent, however. Some historical or regional names have been linked with Trepat in certain sources but are treated as different varieties elsewhere.

That means names such as Trepadell, Pansa Roja, Bonicaire or Embolicaire should not automatically be read as synonyms for Trepat.

There is also a reference to **Trepat Blanc**, a less common white grape. It should not casually be treated as the same thing as the red Trepat discussed here.

## What wines does Trepat make?

### Still red wine

Trepat’s red wines are often described as light to medium-bodied, fresh and fruity. Red-fruit notes such as strawberry, raspberry and cherry are common reference points in official Cava material.

Compared with a deeply concentrated red, a Trepat-based red may feel more lifted and easy-moving, with freshness playing a central role. That does not mean every Trepat is pale, simple or low in tannin. Extraction, ripeness and blending can create a broader range of styles.

### Rosé

Rosé is one of Trepat’s most notable uses. In Conca de Barberà, the variety is associated with rosés that can show bright raspberry-pink colour, freshness and red-fruit character.

The grape’s colour and acidity also help explain its place in sparkling rosé. These wines can be refreshing and fruit-driven, but the final impression will also reflect the sparkling-making method, dosage and time spent ageing.

### Cava

Trepat is an authorised grape variety for Cava. It is particularly associated with rosé Cava, although not every Cava containing Trepat will necessarily be rosé.

When Trepat is used in sparkling wine, it can contribute colour, freshness, moderate alcohol and red-fruit aromas. The finished wine may also contain other varieties, so the presence of Trepat does not determine the whole flavour profile.

## What does Trepat taste like?

A useful starting point is to expect **freshness, red fruit and relatively light structure**.

You might notice aromas or flavours reminiscent of:

- strawberry;
- raspberry;
- cherry;
- other bright red fruits.

Trepat is also commonly described as having balanced acidity and moderate or relatively low alcohol in its regional context. These are tendencies, not guarantees. A warm growing season, later harvest, more extraction or time in oak can make the wine feel fuller and more structured. A lightly made rosé and a more concentrated red will not taste alike simply because both contain Trepat.

Tannin is another area where caution helps. Trepat is often associated with a lighter structure, but it is better to describe the grape as **often relatively gentle in structure** than to claim that all Trepat wines are low in tannin.

## How vineyard and winemaking choices change the style

Trepat is described as a productive, late-ripening variety with large, compact bunches. Vineyard conditions and decisions therefore matter to the balance of the grapes at harvest.

Style can change according to:

- how ripe the grapes become;
- the vineyard’s altitude, soils and climate;
- crop levels and canopy management;
- the amount of skin contact and extraction;
- whether the wine is blended;
- whether it is made as red, rosé or sparkling wine;
- how the sparkling wine is matured.

This is why “Trepat” is a useful clue, rather than a complete tasting note. It tells you about the variety, but not precisely how the winemaker has interpreted it.

## What might Australian drinkers encounter?

No reliable current figure for Australian Trepat plantings or production was verified in the supplied evidence. It is therefore best not to claim that the grape is absent from Australia or to attach a national acreage figure to it.

Australian drinkers are more likely to encounter Trepat in imported Catalan wines—especially wines from Conca de Barberà—or in Cava that uses the variety than as a familiar mainstream Australian grape. That is a cautious market inference, not a measured statement about every retailer.

When reading a label, look for the difference between the grape and the place. Trepat may be the variety named on the bottle, while Conca de Barberà or Cava tells you about the wine’s regional or regulatory context. A Cava label may not make Trepat prominent because Cava is the designation, not necessarily the grape displayed most clearly on the front label.

## How should you approach a bottle of Trepat?

If you are trying Trepat for the first time, begin with the idea of a fresh, red-fruited wine rather than a heavy, powerful red. A still red may suit occasions where you want brightness and moderate weight; a rosé or sparkling rosé may emphasise the grape’s colour and refreshing character.

Then check the label:

1. Is it a still red, rosé or sparkling wine?
2. Is a region such as Conca de Barberà named?
3. Does the label identify Trepat as a single variety or part of a blend?

Those details will usually tell you more about the expected style than the grape name alone.

## The short version

Trepat is a Catalan red wine grape, especially associated with Conca de Barberà. It can produce fresh, red-fruited still reds and rosés, and it is an authorised grape for Cava, particularly rosé Cava. Its typical identity is light, lively and fruit-driven, but vineyard conditions and winemaking can change the result. On a label, remember: **Trepat is the grape, Conca de Barberà is a place, and Cava is a sparkling-wine designation.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Trepat a red or white grape?

The Trepat discussed here is a red wine-grape variety. Trepat Blanc is a separate or unresolved white-variety reference and should not automatically be equated with red Trepat.

### Is Trepat the same as Cava?

No. Trepat is a grape variety. Cava is a regulated sparkling-wine designation that permits Trepat among its authorised varieties.

### Is Trepat always a light red wine?

No. Light, fresh and fruity reds are an important expression, but Trepat is also used for rosé and sparkling wine. Ripeness, extraction, blending and winemaking affect the final style.

### Where is Trepat mainly grown?

It is most strongly associated with Catalonia, especially Conca de Barberà. It is also associated with other Catalan areas, including Costers del Segre and Penedès.
