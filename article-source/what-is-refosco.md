# What is Refosco?

## Highlights

- Refosco is a name used for a group of related dark-skinned grape varieties.
- It is associated especially with north-eastern Italy.
- The specific variety most relevant to modern wine drinkers is usually **Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso**.
- It commonly produces deeply coloured red wines with freshness, fruit character and noticeable tannin.
- “Refosco” does not guarantee one fixed flavour or style.
- The full variety name and the wine’s region can tell you much more than “Refosco” alone.

## Quick answer

Refosco is a grape name associated with several related varieties from north-eastern Italy and nearby areas. When wine references use the name in a specific sense, they often mean **Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso**, a red grape strongly associated with **Friuli Venezia Giulia**.

Wines made from Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso are often deeply coloured, fresh and structured, with red or dark fruit flavours and noticeable tannin. That is a broad guide, not a guarantee. The exact variety, growing conditions, ripeness and winemaking can all change the finished wine.

The important point is that **Refosco is not necessarily one single grape**. Historically and regionally, several Refosco-named varieties have existed. So a bottle labelled simply “Refosco” should not automatically be treated as identical to one labelled “Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso”.

## Where does Refosco come from?

Refosco is most closely associated with north-eastern Italy, particularly Friuli Venezia Giulia. Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso is also found in parts of Veneto and other northern Italian areas.

You may see the grape alongside regional names such as **Friuli DOC** or **Friuli Colli Orientali**. These names provide information about where the wine comes from or the denomination under which it is made. They are not interchangeable with the grape name: “Refosco” identifies a grape reference, while a regional designation describes origin and production rules.

A map can help orient you to Friuli Venezia Giulia, but it should not be read as showing the boundary of every Refosco-named grape.

## What does Refosco taste like?

A typical Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso may be dark in colour, with red or dark fruit, lively acidity and firm but noticeable tannin. You might encounter flavours such as sour cherry or blackberry, alongside savoury or earthy notes.

Think of acidity as the quality that gives wine freshness, while tannin creates a drying, grippy sensation on your gums. Together, they can make Refosco a good match for savoury food.

These characteristics vary. If grapes are not fully ripe, more vegetal flavours may show through. Vineyard conditions, crop levels, extraction and maturation can also affect the wine’s concentration, texture and balance. The grape name is therefore a useful starting point, not a promise of exactly what will be in your glass.

## How should you serve and pair it?

For a red Refosco, try serving the wine slightly cool, around **16–18°C**, unless the producer gives different advice. If the bottle has been stored at room temperature, a short period in a cool place may help it feel fresher.

Its acidity and tannin can work well with savoury dishes such as pork, sausages, grilled or braised meat, mushrooms and game. Pairing depends on the individual wine and the preparation, so lighter examples may suit less intense dishes than fuller, more structured ones.

## How might Australians encounter Refosco?

Australian drinkers may come across Refosco on imported Italian wines, restaurant lists or specialist retailer pages. Australian examples may also use a specific variety name or a broader “Refosco” reference.

Read the full label where possible, including the variety wording, country or region of origin and any denomination. Do not assume that every bottle labelled Refosco is Italian, or that every one contains Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso. Australian and Italian labelling contexts are not identical, and the supplied evidence does not establish a single Australian rule for using the name.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Refosco a red or white grape?

Refosco is generally discussed as a group of dark-skinned grape varieties used for red wine. The specific variety most commonly encountered in modern references is Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso.

### Is Refosco the same as Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso?

Not necessarily. Refosco can be a broader historical or family name, while Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso identifies a particular variety.

### Is Refosco always tannic?

No. Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso often has noticeable tannin, but the level depends on the variety, ripeness and winemaking.

### Should Refosco be served at room temperature?

Usually, it is worth trying a red Refosco slightly cool, around 16–18°C. “Room temperature” can be warmer than ideal, particularly in an Australian summer.
