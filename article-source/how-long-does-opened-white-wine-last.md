# How long does opened white wine last?

## The quick answer

Opened still white wine usually keeps its best drinking quality for about **two to three days** when it is resealed promptly and stored in the fridge.

That is a quality guide, not a guaranteed expiry date. A delicate or already-aged wine may lose freshness sooner, while some sweeter wines can remain enjoyable for longer. Refrigeration slows the changes caused by oxygen, but it cannot restore freshness once the wine has started to decline.

## Highlights

- Aim to drink most opened still white wine within two to three days.
- Reseal the bottle straight after pouring and put it in the fridge.
- Warm conditions can speed quality loss, so avoid leaving the bottle on the kitchen bench.
- Oxidation may make the wine smell flat, bruised or vinegary and taste less fresh.
- A wine past its best is not automatically unsafe, but contamination or poor storage is a reason to discard it.
- Sparkling, sweet and fortified wines need different expectations.

## How to store opened white wine

1. **Reseal it promptly.** Replace the cork or screwcap, or use a suitable wine stopper.
2. **Refrigerate it.** Cold storage slows oxidation and is especially useful in warm Australian weather.
3. **Limit the air space.** If only a small amount remains, transferring it to a clean, smaller bottle and sealing it tightly may help slow its decline.
4. **Keep it away from heat and sunlight.** These conditions can encourage faster changes.
5. **Serve it when ready to drink.** The fridge is useful for slowing deterioration, but the wine may taste better after warming slightly from very cold, depending on its style.

The emptier the bottle, the more air sits above the wine. That extra headspace gives oxygen more opportunity to change the wine, although no storage method guarantees a particular number of days.

## How to tell if it has passed its best

Start with the smell. Fresh fruit and floral aromas may seem muted, replaced by flat, stale, bruised or nutty notes. Vinegar-like aromas can also indicate that the wine has deteriorated.

Look at the colour, too. A white wine that has become noticeably darker or duller may have oxidised, although colour varies naturally between wine styles.

Finally, taste a small amount. It may seem flat, tired, sharp or lacking its original fruit and freshness. These signs mainly indicate declining quality, not a diagnosis of contamination. If the wine is unpleasant, there is no reason to finish it simply because it remains technically drinkable.

## Is old opened white wine unsafe?

Opened white wine often becomes less enjoyable before it becomes a conventional food-safety concern. However, quality and safety are not the same thing.

Do not drink the wine if it has been contaminated, stored in conditions that make you unsure about its safety, or otherwise seems concerning. When in doubt, discard it. The two-to-three-day guide is about **best drinking quality**, not a promise that every bottle is safe or unsafe at a precise point.

## What about sweet, sparkling or fortified wine?

Sweet wines can sometimes last longer in the fridge than dry still whites. Sparkling wine is a separate case because it loses its fizz; use a proper sparkling-wine stopper rather than applying the still-white guideline automatically.

Fortified wines, such as Port or Madeira, also have different storage expectations and should not be judged by the same two-to-three-day window.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does putting white wine in the fridge make it fresh again?

No. Refrigeration slows further change; it does not reverse oxidation or restore aromas that have already faded.

### Can I leave opened white wine on the bench?

You can, but it will generally lose quality faster than a promptly resealed bottle kept cold. This matters particularly in a warm Australian home.

### Should I throw out white wine after three days?

Not necessarily. Three days is a cautious guide to best quality for many still white wines. Smell and taste can help you decide, while remembering that style, temperature, closure and remaining air space all affect the result.
