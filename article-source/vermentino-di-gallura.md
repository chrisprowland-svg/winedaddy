# Vermentino di Gallura: a beginner’s guide to Sardinia’s DOCG wine

## Highlights

- Vermentino di Gallura is a protected wine designation from Gallura in north-eastern Sardinia, Italy.
- **Vermentino** is the grape; **Gallura** is the place.
- The designation is an Italian **DOCG** and a European **PDO/DOP**.
- It is not another name for every Vermentino wine.
- Vermentino di Sardegna DOC is a separate, broader Sardinian designation.
- Wines from the area can be still, sparkling, late-harvest or made from dried grapes, depending on the regulated category.
- Australian Vermentino is not automatically Vermentino di Gallura.
- Granite-derived soils, wind, sunlight and maritime influence help explain the region, but they do not guarantee one flavour profile.

## Quick answer: what is Vermentino di Gallura?

Vermentino di Gallura is a protected Italian wine designation from Gallura, a defined area in the north-east of Sardinia. It is made principally from the Vermentino grape under the rules of an Italian DOCG, or *Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita*. It also sits within the European PDO/DOP protected-origin framework.

The name combines two ideas:

- **Vermentino** identifies the grape variety.
- **di Gallura** identifies the legally defined place of origin.

That distinction matters. A bottle labelled simply **Vermentino** could come from Australia, another Italian region or elsewhere. A bottle labelled **Vermentino di Gallura DOCG** points to a specific Sardinian designation with its own production area and rules.

## Where is Gallura?

Gallura is in north-eastern Sardinia, an island region of Italy. **Olbia** is the most useful major-city reference for Australian readers, while **Tempio Pausania** is a useful inland regional centre.

The defined production area includes municipalities such as Olbia, Tempio Pausania, Arzachena, Palau and Santa Teresa Gallura, along with other municipalities across Gallura. These towns help you picture the area, but none of them defines the whole appellation on its own.

As a rough orientation, Tempio Pausania is around a 45- to 60-minute drive north-west of Olbia, depending on the route and starting point. That is simply a sense of scale: the full designation covers a wider area.

If you are exploring Italian wine geography, start with WineDaddy’s [guide to Italy](/italy/). The existing [Vermentino guide](/vermentino/) explains the grape itself; this page focuses on the place-based wine designation.

## DOCG, PDO and the name on the bottle

**DOCG** is an Italian controlled-and-guaranteed designation. **PDO**, or **Protected Designation of Origin**, is the corresponding European framework; Italian sources commonly use the term **DOP**.

For a drinker, the practical meaning is that Vermentino di Gallura is not just a grape-and-place description invented for a label. It is a protected geographic name with defined rules.

That does not mean DOCG wines all taste identical, or that the designation guarantees a particular level of enjoyment for every drinker. It means the wine must meet the requirements associated with that protected name.

The accessible production discipline supports a minimum of 95% Vermentino, with up to 5% other authorised, non-aromatic white grape varieties suitable for cultivation in Sardinia. The current detailed rules should be checked against the latest consolidated Italian specification when precise technical information is required.

For a plain-English explanation of Italian designations, see [Italian DOC and DOCG](/italian-doc-and-docg/).

## What styles can it be?

Vermentino di Gallura is not limited to one simple, still white-wine format. The documented categories include:

- **Vermentino di Gallura** — the standard designation
- **Superiore** — a regulated category within the designation
- **Frizzante** — lightly sparkling
- **Spumante** — sparkling
- **Passito** — made from dried or raisined grapes, generally associated with richer or sweeter wines
- **Vendemmia tardiva** — late harvest

You should not assume that every bottle from Gallura is sweet, sparkling or full-bodied. The category and the individual winemaking choices matter. A still wine, a sparkling wine and a late-harvest wine from the same designated area can offer quite different experiences.

## What does Vermentino di Gallura taste like?

Many examples are described as fresh and aromatic, with white-fleshed fruit, floral notes and sometimes an almond-like impression. Some may feel textured or show a mineral character. More structured examples can have greater weight and persistence.

Treat these as useful tendencies rather than tasting rules. Not every bottle will be salty, mineral, intensely aromatic or full-bodied. Site, vintage, ripeness and winemaking all influence the finished wine.

Gallura’s regional setting helps explain why its wines can show a distinctive sense of place. The area is associated with granite-derived soils, coarse sandy material, strong sunlight, persistent wind and maritime influence. Vineyards range from near sea level to sites around 500 metres above sea level.

These factors contribute to growing conditions; they do not work like flavouring ingredients. Granite does not make every wine taste of rocks, and proximity to the sea does not guarantee a salty flavour.

## Vermentino di Gallura versus other Vermentino

The easiest way to avoid confusion is to separate the grape from the appellation.

| Label wording | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Vermentino** | The grape variety or a general wine reference; origin needs to be checked separately |
| **Vermentino di Gallura DOCG** | A protected designation from the defined Gallura area |
| **Vermentino di Sardegna DOC** | A separate Sardinian designation with different geographic rules |
| **Australian Vermentino** | An Australian wine made from, or associated with, the Vermentino grape |

Vermentino di Sardegna DOC is not simply a lower or higher version of Vermentino di Gallura DOCG. It is a different designation. Likewise, Australian wine laws and labelling rules do not become Italian DOCG rules just because the same grape is used.

This is the key lesson: **a shared grape name does not make wines from different places the same wine.**

## How should an Australian reader read the label?

If you see Vermentino di Gallura on an imported bottle, wine list or specialist retailer listing, read it as a place-and-grape combination.

Look for:

1. **Vermentino di Gallura** — the protected geographic name.
2. **DOCG** — the Italian designation category.
3. A possible category term such as **Superiore**, **Frizzante**, **Spumante**, **Passito** or **Vendemmia tardiva**.

A bottle that says only **Vermentino** does not, by itself, indicate Gallura. For more label-decoding help, see [How to read a wine label](/how-to-read-a-wine-label/).

At the table, the fresher still and sparkling styles may suit seafood, shellfish, salads and lighter Mediterranean-style dishes. That is a starting point, not a rule: a late-harvest or passito example belongs to a different conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Vermentino di Gallura a grape variety?

No. Vermentino is the grape variety. Vermentino di Gallura is a protected wine designation associated with a defined area in Sardinia.

### Is Vermentino di Gallura the same as Vermentino di Sardegna?

No. They are separate Sardinian designations with different geographic rules.

### Is Australian Vermentino the same as Vermentino di Gallura?

No. Australian Vermentino may use the same grape, but it is not automatically connected to the Italian DOCG.

### Is every Vermentino di Gallura wine dry and still?

No. The designation includes documented categories such as Frizzante, Spumante, Passito and Vendemmia tardiva, as well as the standard still category.

### Does DOCG mean the wine will taste a certain way?

No. DOCG describes protected origin and production requirements. Taste still varies with site, vintage, grape ripeness and winemaking.
