# Western Australian Wine Regions: A Beginner’s Guide

Western Australia is a large wine-producing state, but its recognised wine regions are concentrated mainly in the south-west, around Greater Perth and across the Great Southern. The state has nine registered regional wine GIs: Blackwood Valley, Geographe, Great Southern, Manjimup, Margaret River, Pemberton, Peel, Perth Hills and Swan District.

These are separate geographic references, not interchangeable names. They sit within larger zones and can contain smaller subregions. A regional name can help you understand where a wine comes from, but it does not guarantee one grape variety, one style or a particular level of quality.

## Highlights

- Western Australia’s wine regions are concentrated in the cooler and wetter south-western part of the state, rather than spread evenly across its enormous landmass.
- The official hierarchy is **Australia → Western Australia → zone → regional GI → subregion**.
- Margaret River, Great Southern, Geographe, Pemberton, Manjimup, Blackwood Valley, Peel, Perth Hills and Swan District are separate regional GIs.
- Greater Perth contains Peel, Perth Hills and Swan District.
- The South West Australia Zone contains six regional GIs, including Margaret River and Great Southern.
- Great Southern contains five recognised subregions: Albany, Denmark, Frankland River, Mount Barker and Porongurup.
- Climate, latitude, elevation, rainfall and distance from the coast help explain regional tendencies, but every vineyard and wine can differ.

## Quick answer: what should you understand?

Think of Western Australian wine as a collection of distinct south-western and southern landscapes rather than one single style.

The official wine geography identifies Western Australia as a state-level GI with five zones. The nine regional GIs relevant to wine learners are grouped within the Greater Perth and South West Australia zones. Swan Valley is a recognised subregion of Swan District, while Albany, Denmark, Frankland River, Mount Barker and Porongurup are subregions of Great Southern.

Margaret River is strongly associated with Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and Semillon–Sauvignon Blanc blends. Great Southern is much larger and more internally varied, with associations including Riesling, Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. Warmer Greater Perth regions such as Swan District have long associations with full-flavoured Chenin Blanc and Verdelho, as well as Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon. Cooler southern and elevated areas add further variation.

The useful lesson is not to memorise a “signature wine” for every place. Instead, use the region as a starting point, then look at the grape, vintage and producer on the label.

## Where are Western Australia’s wine regions?

Perth is the most useful major-city reference point. Swan District is north of Perth, while Perth Hills lies east of the city around the Darling Scarp. Peel is south of Perth, around the Mandurah and Peel area.

Geographe is farther south-west, around Bunbury, Busselton and Geographe Bay. Margaret River is approximately three to three and a half hours south-west of Perth by road. Blackwood Valley is inland and east of Margaret River. Manjimup and Pemberton are farther south-east, while Great Southern extends around Albany, Denmark and inland areas. Albany is approximately five hours from Perth by road, although destinations within a large GI vary considerably.

These driving times are broad orientation estimates, not precise travel promises. A town or locality can help you picture a region, but it does not automatically define the legal GI boundary.

## The official wine-region hierarchy

### Greater Perth Zone

The Greater Perth Zone contains three regional GIs:

- **Swan District**, including the recognised Swan Valley subregion
- **Perth Hills**
- **Peel**

Swan District sits north of Perth and has a warm to hot Mediterranean climate. Chenin Blanc, Verdelho and Chardonnay are important regional associations, alongside Shiraz and Cabernet Sauvignon. Swan Valley is related to Swan District, but the two names are not identical in the GI hierarchy.

Perth Hills lies in elevated country east of Perth. Its higher setting and different rainfall conditions contribute to a cooler context than the adjacent coastal plain. Shiraz is a prominent regional association, but the region is not defined by one grape alone.

Peel lies south of Perth, between the northern Greater Perth regions and Geographe. Shiraz is its principal regional association, although wines from the region can vary with site.

### South West Australia Zone

The South West Australia Zone contains six regional GIs:

- **Blackwood Valley**
- **Geographe**
- **Great Southern**
- **Manjimup**
- **Margaret River**
- **Pemberton**

South West Australia is a **zone**, not a regional GI equivalent to Margaret River or Pemberton. This distinction matters when reading a label or discussing regional geography.

Geographe includes coastal and inland areas around Geographe Bay and Bunbury. Cabernet Sauvignon, Sauvignon Blanc, Semillon and Shiraz are useful starting points, but site differences mean there is no single Geographe style.

Margaret River occupies the far south-west. Its strong maritime influence helps explain its associations with Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and Semillon–Sauvignon Blanc blends. It is an important Western Australian region, but it does not represent all Western Australian wine.

Blackwood Valley is inland, around the Blackwood River valley and places such as Bridgetown and Nannup. Although it shares a latitude with Margaret River, it is somewhat more continental, with greater temperature variation and spring-frost risk. Sauvignon Blanc and Shiraz are useful regional reference points.

Manjimup is inland and north of Pemberton, in south-west forest and agricultural country. Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Shiraz, Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc are among its broad regional associations.

Pemberton is cooler and wetter than neighbouring Manjimup, with karri-forest country and a cooler growing context. Chardonnay, Merlot and Pinot Noir are associated with the region. Pinot Noir is a useful point of interest, but it should not be treated as an exclusive regional signature.

Great Southern is a very large and diverse GI in the south-east of the state. Its five recognised subregions—Albany, Denmark, Frankland River, Mount Barker and Porongurup—help explain why it cannot be reduced to one climate or style. Riesling, Shiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir are among its important regional associations.

## What might you see on a bottle?

For a qualifying wine, the geographic wording is likely to use a registered GI such as **Margaret River**, **Great Southern**, **Geographe** or **Swan District**. A town name may overlap with a GI name, but it is not automatically interchangeable with it.

Likewise, “south-west Western Australia” is a useful geographic description, while **South West Australia** has a specific meaning as a wine zone. And “Swan Valley” is a recognised subregion within Swan District, not simply another name for the entire regional GI.

A GI tells you about geographic origin. It does not require every wine from that place to use one grape variety or taste the same way.

## How to use the regions as a wine learner

Start with the broad contrast:

- warm Greater Perth contexts, including Swan District;
- maritime Margaret River;
- coastal and inland South West regions such as Geographe;
- cooler, wetter or elevated southern areas including Pemberton, Perth Hills and parts of Great Southern.

Then compare wines by grape variety. A Chardonnay from Margaret River and one from Pemberton may share a state but come from different growing contexts. A Shiraz from Swan District, Peel, Perth Hills or Great Southern may also show different regional influences.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many wine regions are there in Western Australia?

The approved GI structure identifies nine regional GIs: Blackwood Valley, Geographe, Great Southern, Manjimup, Margaret River, Pemberton, Peel, Perth Hills and Swan District.

### Is South West Australia a wine region?

South West Australia is a wine **zone** containing several regional GIs. It is not interchangeable with Margaret River or any one regional wine area.

### Is Swan Valley the same as Swan District?

No. Swan Valley is a recognised subregion within the Swan District regional GI.

### Is Margaret River all of Western Australian wine?

No. Margaret River is one of nine regional GIs. Western Australia also includes Great Southern, Geographe, Pemberton, Manjimup, Blackwood Valley, Peel, Perth Hills and Swan District.
