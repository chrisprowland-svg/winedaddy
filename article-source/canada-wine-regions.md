# Canada wine regions

## Highlights

- Canada’s best-known wine regions sit mainly in British Columbia and Ontario.
- The Okanagan Valley has a dry inland climate with substantial variation from north to south.
- The Niagara Peninsula is moderated by the Great Lakes and is important for both table wine and Icewine.
- Canadian labels commonly emphasise province, region, grape variety and vintage.

## Where Canadian wine is grown

Canada’s commercial vineyards are concentrated in a small number of places where lakes, latitude, elevation and local topography make grape growing possible. British Columbia and Ontario account for the country’s best-known regions, while smaller industries also exist elsewhere.

The country is not one uniform cool-climate zone. Growing conditions vary sharply between provinces and even within individual valleys. Winter cold, frost risk, sunshine, rainfall and lake influence all affect which grapes can ripen reliably.

## Okanagan Valley

The Okanagan Valley is in the interior of British Columbia. It is long and geographically varied: northern areas are generally cooler, while the southern valley is warmer and drier. This range supports several wine styles rather than one single regional profile.

Common grapes include Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Riesling, Pinot Gris, Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Syrah. The most useful way to understand an Okanagan bottle is to consider where in the valley it was grown, not merely the broad regional name.

## Niagara Peninsula

The Niagara Peninsula lies in Ontario between Lake Ontario and Lake Erie. Lake moderation and the Niagara Escarpment help shape local mesoclimates and extend the growing season.

Riesling, Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Cabernet Franc are important reference varieties. Niagara is also internationally associated with Icewine, although its producers make a much broader range of dry still and sparkling wines.

## Reading a Canadian wine label

Look first for the province and regional designation, then the grape variety and vintage. Terms governed by Canada’s provincial appellation systems indicate origin and production requirements. A named region offers more geographic information than a simple country-level statement.
