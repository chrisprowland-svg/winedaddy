# Aglianico del Vulture: A Beginner’s Guide to the Italian Wine

## Highlights

- Aglianico del Vulture is a **protected wine designation**, not just the name of a grape.
- It comes from the Vulture area of **northern Basilicata**, in southern Italy.
- The wine is based on the **Aglianico** grape, a late-ripening red variety.
- The standard designation is **Aglianico del Vulture DOC**.
- **Aglianico del Vulture Superiore DOCG** is a separate, narrower designation with additional rules.
- Expect a dry, deeply coloured red with firm acidity and tannin, although style varies.
- On an Australian bottle-shop shelf, the protected place name is the most useful clue to what you are buying.

## Quick answer: What is Aglianico del Vulture?

Aglianico del Vulture is a protected Italian wine designation from the Vulture area of northern Basilicata. It is made principally from Aglianico, a dark-skinned, late-ripening red grape.

The standard designation is **Aglianico del Vulture DOC**. A separate category, **Aglianico del Vulture Superiore DOCG**, comes from a narrower regulated designation and has additional production and ageing requirements. “Superiore” here is not merely a casual quality claim.

Typical examples are dry, dark in colour and structured, with noticeable acidity and tannin. You may find flavours of dark cherry, plum, liquorice, spice, dried herbs and earthy or savoury notes. These are broad tendencies rather than promises: site, vintage, ripeness, extraction, oak and bottle age can all change the wine.

## Where is Aglianico del Vulture?

Aglianico del Vulture sits within this hierarchy:

**Italy → Basilicata → Province of Potenza → Vulture area → Aglianico del Vulture production area**

The Vulture area is in northern Basilicata, close to Campania and Puglia. It takes its name from Mount Vulture, an ancient volcanic massif. Useful local reference points include Rionero in Vulture, Melfi, Venosa, Barile and Rapolla. These are towns and localities within or associated with the area; no single town defines the entire designation.

Potenza is the capital and nearest useful regional centre for broad orientation. From Naples, Rionero in Vulture is roughly 155 kilometres by road, or around two hours and 20 minutes in favourable conditions. Treat that as a geographical guide rather than a precise travel promise.

The legal DOC production area covers defined municipalities in the Province of Potenza. That legal boundary is not exactly the same thing as the broader cultural or geographical Vulture area.

## What does “Aglianico del Vulture” mean on a label?

The name combines a grape reference with a protected place name:

- **Aglianico** is the principal grape variety.
- **del Vulture** means the wine is associated with the regulated Vulture production area.
- **DOC** indicates an Italian controlled designation of origin.
- **DOCG** indicates an Italian controlled and guaranteed designation of origin.

An imported bottle might show:

- Aglianico del Vulture DOC
- Aglianico del Vulture Superiore DOCG
- Aglianico del Vulture Superiore Riserva DOCG
- Aglianico del Vulture Spumante DOC

DOC and DOCG rules govern origin and production. They are not a universal ranking in which every DOCG wine must be better than every DOC wine. They are also Italian and European systems, not Australian geographical indications.

For more background, see our guides to [Italian DOC and DOCG](/italian-doc-and-docg/) and [protected wine names](/protected-wine-names/).

## What is the landscape like?

The DOC specification describes suitable vineyards as hilly and generally between **200 and 700 metres above sea level**. Mount Vulture’s ancient volcanic geology contributes volcanic materials to a landscape that also includes varied hill soils, including tuff and clay-rich layers in some locations.

Elevation can moderate temperatures and create differences between slopes and exposures. The area also experiences hot, dry summers, wetter parts of the year and useful day–night temperature differences. Aglianico ripens late, potentially extending into October or November.

That helps explain the wine’s combination of ripe colour and flavour with firm acidity. But geography does not dictate one flavour. A volcanic setting does not mean every bottle must taste smoky or mineral, and vineyards within the legal area are not identical.

## What does Aglianico del Vulture taste like?

Aglianico is naturally capable of producing wines with:

- deep colour;
- firm or high acidity;
- substantial tannin;
- concentrated fruit;
- savoury and earthy complexity;
- the ability to develop with time.

Young examples can feel dense, powerful and grippy. “Grippy” is a useful beginner’s word for tannin that creates a drying, textural sensation on the gums. With suitable maturation, tannins may feel more integrated and flavours can become more savoury or leathery.

Common descriptors include black cherry, plum, red berry, liquorice, dried herbs, spice, earth and mineral or smoky impressions. Modern winemaking can also produce more approachable expressions than the old image of relentlessly hard tannin suggests.

The main sources of variation are vineyard elevation and aspect, vintage conditions, crop level, grape ripeness, extraction, oak and maturation. So it is better to think of Aglianico del Vulture as a structured family of wines than as one fixed taste.

## How is it different from other Aglianico wines?

Aglianico is grown in several parts of southern Italy. Aglianico from another area is not automatically Aglianico del Vulture.

Two useful comparisons are:

- **Taurasi DOCG**, in Campania’s Province of Avellino.
- **Aglianico del Taburno DOCG**, in Campania’s Province of Benevento.

These are separate Italian designations with their own geographic boundaries and rules. The grape may be related, but the place name, production rules and growing conditions differ. You can learn more about the surrounding region through our guides to [Campania](/campania/) and [Puglia](/puglia/).

## How should you serve it?

For a typical structured red, start around **16–18°C**. In a warm Australian home, that may mean a short spell in the fridge rather than serving the bottle at room temperature.

A young, tannic example may soften with time in the glass. Opening it before dinner or decanting it is optional, not a rule.

Its acidity and tannin make it a useful starting point for food such as:

- braised or roasted meat;
- grilled red meat;
- sausages and slow-cooked dishes;
- tomato-based pasta;
- mushrooms and earthy vegetables;
- firm, savoury cheeses.

The best pairing depends on the individual bottle and the dish, but rich, savoury food generally gives the wine’s structure something to work with.

## What should Australian wine learners remember?

The pronunciation is approximately **ag-lee-AH-nee-koh del VUL-too-reh**. The final “e” in *Vulture* is pronounced.

When reading an imported label, look for the complete protected wording rather than only the grape name. **Aglianico del Vulture DOC** and **Aglianico del Vulture Superiore DOCG** identify wines from the Italian designation. An Aglianico labelled from Campania, Puglia or another region is a different geographical expression.

Availability in Australia varies by importer, retailer, state and vintage. If you see a bottle, the designation tells you where the wine comes from and which rules apply; it does not tell you exactly how ripe, oaky or tannic it will taste.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Aglianico del Vulture a grape or a region?

It is primarily a protected wine designation associated with a geographic area. It is based on the Aglianico grape, but the complete name refers to both grape identity and place.

### Is Aglianico del Vulture the same as Taurasi?

No. Both are southern Italian Aglianico-based designations, but Taurasi DOCG is a separate appellation in Campania.

### Is Aglianico del Vulture always full-bodied and tannic?

No. It commonly has firm structure and substantial tannin, but body and tannin vary with vineyard site, vintage, ripeness and winemaking.

### Does DOCG always mean better than DOC?

No. DOCG and DOC describe regulated Italian origin and production systems. They are not a guaranteed ranking of quality or enjoyment.

### What food goes with Aglianico del Vulture?

Try braised meat, sausages, tomato-based pasta, mushrooms or firm savoury cheese. These are starting points rather than strict rules.

### Should Aglianico del Vulture be aged?

Some examples can develop with bottle age, but not every bottle is intended for long-term cellaring. Vintage, structure, winemaking and storage all matter.
