# What is Alicante Bouschet?

## Highlights

- Alicante Bouschet is a red wine-grape variety.
- It is a **teinturier** grape, meaning its flesh is red as well as its skin.
- Its naturally coloured juice helps explain its traditional use in deeply coloured wines and blends.
- It can produce structured wines, but its style varies with climate, ripeness, cropping and winemaking.
- It may appear as a varietal wine or as part of a blend.
- The grape name tells you something about the raw material—not exactly how every finished wine will taste.

## Quick answer

Alicante Bouschet is a red wine grape best known for being a **teinturier**. Most red grapes have dark skins but pale flesh, so their juice becomes red mainly when it spends time with the skins during winemaking. Alicante Bouschet has coloured flesh as well as dark skin, so crushed grapes can produce red juice from the outset.

That strong colour helps explain why Alicante Bouschet has traditionally been used to add colour and structure to blends. It can also be made into a varietal wine, meaning the grape is named on the bottle. The finished wine is not automatically one fixed style, however: vineyard conditions and winemaking choices still matter.

## Why is its flesh red?

The word *teinturier* is a useful shortcut for understanding Alicante Bouschet. Its flesh contains colour compounds as well as its skin. This differs from most familiar red wine grapes, whose pulp is usually pale.

That does not mean every Alicante Bouschet wine will be intensely dark. Colour is also affected by ripeness, extraction during fermentation, blending and the stability of the pigments over time. The grape gives the winemaker more colour to work with, but it does not dictate the final appearance on its own.

## What does Alicante Bouschet taste like?

Alicante Bouschet is generally associated with deeply coloured, structured wines. Examples may show tannin, acidity, body and dark-fruited or savoury impressions. These are broad tendencies rather than guarantees.

A warm site, a cooler site, different crop levels or a different winemaking approach can all change the result. One bottle may feel concentrated and firm, while another may be fresher or more blended in style. The variety is not automatically sweet, high in alcohol or heavy.

## Where did it come from?

Alicante Bouschet was created in southern France in the mid-19th century by Henri Bouschet, using Grenache Noir and Petit Bouschet as its parents. It is therefore not the same grape as Grenache or Petit Bouschet.

You may also see the fuller name **Alicante Henri Bouschet**—or the spelling **Alicante Henry Bouschet**—but “Alicante Bouschet” is the most useful name for consumers. **Garnacha Tintorera** is a Spanish name associated with the variety. Alicante Bouschet No. 2 is a distinct but similar selection, so the names should not automatically be treated as identical.

## Is Alicante Bouschet grown in Australia?

Yes. The Australian Wine Research Institute records its introduction to Australia in the 1960s and notes plantings or use in regions including the Barossa Valley, Margaret River, Riverland, Eden Valley, Adelaide Plains, North-East Victoria and the Hunter Valley.

Australian readers may encounter Alicante Bouschet as a grape named on a label or as part of a blend. Remember that “Alicante Bouschet” identifies a grape variety, not a wine region. “Alicante” on its own can be less precise because it may also refer to a Spanish geographical name.

The practical takeaway is simple: if you see Alicante Bouschet on a bottle, expect the possibility of deep colour and firm structure, but read the rest of the label—and taste the wine—before assuming exactly how it will behave.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Alicante Bouschet a red or white grape?

It is a red wine-grape variety, with dark skin and coloured flesh.

### Is Alicante Bouschet used in blends?

Yes. Its colour and structural contribution help explain its use as a blending component, although it can also be made as a varietal wine.

### Is Alicante Bouschet the same as Grenache?

No. Grenache Noir is one of Alicante Bouschet’s parents, but they are separate grape varieties.
