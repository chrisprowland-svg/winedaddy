# How to clean wine glasses

Clean wine glasses promptly after use with warm water, a small amount of mild dishwashing liquid and a soft, non-abrasive cloth or sponge. Rinse them thoroughly, then let them drain or air-dry. Polish the outside with a clean, lint-free cloth only if needed.

Hand-wash glasses marked “hand wash only”, delicate or valuable stemware, and glasses with missing care instructions. Use a dishwasher only when the glass is labelled dishwasher-safe and can be loaded securely without touching other items.

## Highlights

1. **Check the care instructions.** Separate hand-wash-only, delicate, valuable, decorated, chipped or cracked glasses. Do not use chipped or cracked glassware.
2. **Rinse promptly.** Rinse the glass soon after use so wine residue does not dry onto the surface. This is especially helpful after red wine.
3. **Wash with warm water.** Add a small amount of mild dishwashing liquid and use a soft sponge or cloth. Avoid abrasive scourers and powders, which may scratch the glass.
4. **Rinse thoroughly.** Rinse until there is no visible residue or detergent scent.
5. **Let it drain or air-dry.** Place the glass somewhere stable and allow water to run off naturally where possible.
6. **Polish the outside if necessary.** Use a clean, lint-free or microfibre cloth. Polishing is optional and cannot repair scratches, chips, cracks or permanent cloudiness.
7. **Store carefully.** Keep clean glasses away from strongly scented cleaning products, spices and perfumed items. Make sure they cannot knock against one another.

Use warm rather than boiling water. Hot water and steam can cause scalds, while sudden temperature extremes may be hard on glassware.

## How to hand-wash wine glasses safely

Wine glasses have three useful parts: the bowl, stem and foot. Hold the glass steadily and avoid twisting the stem while cleaning or drying it. Do not force a cloth into a narrow bowl, and take particular care with thin rims.

A soft sponge, cloth or non-abrasive brush is suitable for ordinary cleaning. Work gently around the bowl, rim and foot, paying attention to areas where lipstick, grease or dried wine may remain.

Use only enough dishwashing liquid to clean the glass. More detergent does not make the job better, and excess fragrance or residue is undesirable when you are about to drink wine. Rinse especially carefully if the glass still smells of detergent.

Glasses that are hand-wash-only, unusually shaped, decorated, antique or valuable should follow their maker’s instructions. “Crystal” is not one universal care category, so check the product guidance rather than assuming every crystal glass needs the same treatment.

## Can wine glasses go in the dishwasher?

Sometimes. The glass must be labelled dishwasher-safe or supported by clear manufacturer instructions.

Before starting the cycle:

- Check that the glass fits securely.
- Leave space between glasses so they cannot touch.
- Keep glassware away from metal utensils and the spray arms.
- Use a stemware holder or upper rack where the glass fits safely.
- Choose an appropriate low-temperature or glassware programme.
- Follow the glassmaker’s instructions as well as the dishwasher manufacturer’s instructions.
- Use dishwasher detergent and rinse aid only as directed; do not assume more is better.

Some manufacturer guidance gives approximately **50–55°C** as an example low-temperature range for suitable glassware programmes. This is not a universal setting for every Australian dishwasher or every wine glass.

A dishwasher-safe label is not a guarantee that a glass can never scratch, break or become cloudy. Glasses may be damaged if they touch other items, sit insecurely or are exposed to unsuitable washing conditions. Delicate or valuable stemware is the cautious choice for hand-washing.

## What if a wine glass is cloudy?

Cloudiness can have more than one cause.

A removable mineral or detergent deposit may leave a cloudy film or water spots. Tap-water mineral content varies between Australian households, so hard-water spotting may be a problem in some kitchens but not others.

If the glassmaker permits it, a small amount of white vinegar may help loosen some mineral deposits. Use it only as a conditional cleaning option, never mix it with bleach, ammonia or other cleaning chemicals, and do not assume it is suitable for decorated or specially treated glass.

If careful cleaning does not remove the cloudiness, the glass may be etched or affected by permanent glass corrosion. Ordinary cleaning and polishing cannot reverse that damage. Manufacturer instructions should take priority.

## How to remove wine odour, lipstick or grease

Prompt rinsing and ordinary washing usually deal with wine residue. For lipstick or grease, wash the affected area gently with warm water and a small amount of mild dishwashing liquid, then rinse thoroughly.

If a detergent smell remains, rinse again rather than adding more detergent. Strong odours can also transfer during storage, so keep glasses away from cleaning chemicals and other strongly scented products.

If a glass remains marked, scratched or cloudy after careful cleaning, do not keep scrubbing aggressively. More pressure or a harsher abrasive may damage the surface further.

## Quick answers

### Is it better to wash wine glasses by hand?

Hand-washing is the cautious choice for hand-wash-only, delicate, valuable, antique or unusually shaped glasses. Ordinary glasses labelled dishwasher-safe can be washed in a dishwasher when loaded and cycled according to the instructions.

### Should wine glasses be dried upside down?

Let them drain or air-dry in a stable position. Avoid placing a thin rim directly on a hard surface, and do not force a cloth into the bowl. If you polish the glass, support it carefully and avoid twisting the stem.

### Can polishing remove cloudy glass?

Polishing may improve the appearance of the outside, but it cannot repair etching, glass corrosion, scratches, chips or cracks.

### What should I do with a cracked wine glass?

Stop using it. Cleaning cannot make cracked or chipped glassware safe.
