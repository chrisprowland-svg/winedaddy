# What Is Xinomavro? A Beginner’s Guide

## Highlights

- Xinomavro is a Greek red grape, not the name of a single wine style.
- It is most strongly associated with northern Greece.
- Naoussa is a key reference point for structured, age-worthy red Xinomavro.
- Amyndeon shows the grape’s lighter, more floral and sparkling side.
- High acidity and firm tannin are two of its most recognisable features.
- Xinomavro can also be made as rosé, sparkling, light red, sweet and blended wine.
- It can be excellent with savoury, protein-rich food.
- Comparisons with Nebbiolo or Pinot Noir are only rough signposts—not identities.

## Xinomavro in brief

Xinomavro is a red wine grape from Greece, principally associated with northern Greece and especially the wine regions of Naoussa and Amyndeon.

Its classic red wines are often bright or relatively pale in colour, high in acidity and firm in tannin. They may show red fruit, flowers, tomato, olives, tobacco, dried fruit, nuts and spice. That combination can make Xinomavro feel fresh and savoury rather than simply rich or dark.

The important point is that Xinomavro is not one fixed flavour profile. Its character changes with region, vineyard site, ripeness, ageing and winemaking.

## Where does Xinomavro come from?

Xinomavro is primarily associated with central and northern Greece, particularly the historical wine area of Macedonia.

Two names are especially useful to remember:

### Naoussa

Naoussa is a northern Greek PDO—Protected Designation of Origin—where the wines are made from 100% Xinomavro. It is the clearest reference point for dry red wines with firm tannin, high acidity, savoury complexity and ageing potential.

### Amyndeon

Amyndeon, also written Amynteo, is a separate PDO in the Florina area of north-western Greece. Its cooler, higher-elevation setting is associated with fresher, lighter and more floral expressions. Amyndeon is also particularly important for rosé and sparkling Xinomavro.

Xinomavro is also used in other regional wines. In Goumenissa, it is blended with Negoska. In Rapsani, it is blended with Krassato and Stavroto. These are separate regional designations, not alternative names for the grape.

## What does Xinomavro taste like?

A classic dry red Xinomavro may have:

- bright acidity, which gives the wine freshness;
- firm tannin, creating a drying or grippy sensation;
- a relatively pale or bright red colour;
- red cherry and other red-fruit flavours;
- floral aromas;
- savoury notes such as tomato, olives, tobacco and dried herbs;
- spice, nuts or dried-fruit characters, particularly with age or oak maturation.

The colour can be surprising. A wine can look lighter than expected while still having plenty of tannin and structure. In other words, pale colour does not necessarily mean a delicate or simple wine.

Young, structured examples may feel austere or tightly wound. With bottle age, they can become more complex and savoury. Other examples—particularly rosé and sparkling wines—can be lighter, more immediately refreshing and less dominated by tannin.

## What styles of wine can Xinomavro make?

Xinomavro is best known for dry red wine, but it is more versatile than that reputation suggests.

- **Dry red:** typically structured, fresh, savoury and suitable for ageing.
- **Rosé:** often bright and red-fruited, with less tannic weight than a dry red.
- **Sparkling:** especially associated with Amyndeon, including rosé styles.
- **Light red:** a fresher, less heavily extracted interpretation.
- **Sweet:** documented, although less central to the grape’s beginner-friendly identity.
- **Blended wine:** particularly important in appellations such as Goumenissa and Rapsani.

This variety is one reason it is better to ask what style of Xinomavro you are looking at, rather than assuming the grape tells you everything.

## Is Xinomavro like Nebbiolo?

Xinomavro is sometimes compared with Nebbiolo because both can combine pale colour, high acidity, firm tannin and the ability to develop savoury complexity with age.

That comparison can be useful as a structural signpost, but Xinomavro is not “Greek Nebbiolo”. It is a distinct grape with its own character. It may also remind some drinkers of Sangiovese through its acidity and savoury food affinity, or Pinot Noir through its relatively lighter colour and aromatic lift. None of these comparisons guarantees that the wines will taste alike.

## How should you serve Xinomavro?

Serve a structured red Xinomavro slightly cool rather than warm. A practical starting point is around 15–18°C, adjusting according to the wine’s body and style.

A young, tannic red may benefit from time in a decanter, although decanting is optional. Older bottles should be handled gently. Rosé and sparkling versions should be served chilled in line with their style.

## What food goes with Xinomavro?

Acidity and tannin make Xinomavro particularly useful with savoury food containing protein, fat and salt. Try it with:

- grilled or roasted lamb or beef;
- rich stews;
- mushroom dishes;
- tomato-based pasta or vegetable dishes;
- charcuterie;
- aged, hard cheeses;
- savoury dishes with umami flavours.

Its freshness can cut through richness, while its structure can stand up to substantial food. Very sweet dishes may make a dry, tannic Xinomavro taste harder and more angular.

## What should Australian wine learners expect?

Most Xinomavro encountered by Australian learners is likely to be associated with Greece, particularly northern Greek regions such as Naoussa and Amyndeon.

Australian industry interest in the variety exists, but the available evidence does not support describing Xinomavro as a major Australian grape. If you see it on a wine list or in a bottle shop, expect an opportunity to explore a distinctive Greek variety rather than a familiar Australian category.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you pronounce Xinomavro?

A commonly used approximate English pronunciation is “ksee-noh-MAH-vroh”. Treat this as a pronunciation guide rather than a definitive linguistic transcription.

### Is Xinomavro always tannic?

No. Classic dry reds often have firm tannin, but rosé, sparkling and lighter styles can feel much less tannic.

### Is Xinomavro always dark in colour?

No. Many examples are relatively pale or bright in colour for a structured red wine.

### Is Xinomavro a red-only grape?

No. It is used for red, rosé, sparkling, light red, sweet and blended wines.

### Is Xinomavro grown in Australia?

There is Australian industry interest and some evidence of the variety being considered in Australian viticulture, but the available evidence does not establish significant national production.
