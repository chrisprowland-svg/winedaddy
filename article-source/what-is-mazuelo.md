# What is Mazuelo?

Mazuelo is a red wine-grape variety. It is best known internationally as **Carignan**, while **Cariñena** and **Mazuela** are other names associated with the same grape.

The name can be confusing because it is used differently from place to place. In Spain, Mazuelo is particularly associated with Rioja. Cariñena is also linked with Aragón and the town and wine region of Cariñena. In France and much international wine writing, **Carignan** is the name you are more likely to see.

## Highlights

- Mazuelo is a red wine grape, not a wine style.
- Carignan is its best-known international name.
- It is generally considered to have originated in Aragón, in northeastern Spain.
- The grape can contribute deep colour, lively acidity and firm tannin.
- It is often used in blends, but can also make a single-variety wine.
- Its final flavour depends on climate, ripeness, site, winemaking and blending.
- In Australia, Carignan is a more visible name than Mazuelo.

## What does Mazuelo mean on a wine label?

When you see Mazuelo on a label, the wine is identifying the grape variety. It does not, by itself, tell you exactly how the wine will taste.

A Mazuelo from Rioja may reflect Rioja’s climate, soils, vine age and local winemaking traditions. A Carignan from another country may be made in a different style. Even two wines made from the same variety can differ substantially depending on when the grapes were picked, how much fruit was extracted during fermentation, whether the wine was matured in oak and whether other varieties were blended with it.

The name is therefore a useful starting point, rather than a complete tasting prediction.

## Is Mazuelo the same as Carignan?

Yes. Authoritative grape references identify Mazuelo with the variety more widely known as Carignan or Carignan Noir.

You may also encounter:

- **Mazuela** — a closely related Spanish spelling.
- **Cariñena** — a Spanish name for the grape, also strongly connected with a place in Aragón.
- **Carignane** — an alternative international spelling.
- **Carignano** — a name associated particularly with Sardinia.
- **Samsó** or **Sansó** — regional names reported for the variety, though naming practices can be complicated.

One important Australian warning: do not assume that **Mataro** means Mazuelo. In Australia, Mataro is widely understood as another name for Mourvèdre. Some extended historical synonym lists can create confusion, but Mataro is not a practical synonym to rely on when reading an Australian label.

## Where does Mazuelo come from?

Mazuelo is generally considered to have originated in Aragón, in northeastern Spain. The name Cariñena connects the variety with the area around the town and wine region of Cariñena.

The grape also has a strong association with Rioja, where Mazuelo or Mazuela is used as a traditional name for Carignan. It later became important in other Mediterranean wine-growing areas, especially southern France, where it is generally called Carignan.

That history explains why one grape can appear under several names. Wine regions often preserve their own traditional terminology, even when international grape references use a different preferred name.

## What does Mazuelo wine taste like?

Mazuelo can produce deeply coloured red wines with noticeable acidity and firm tannin. Tannin is the drying, mouth-gripping sensation you can feel on your gums, while acidity gives wine freshness and shape.

Depending on the growing conditions and winemaking, you may find:

- red or dark-fruit characters;
- spice;
- savoury notes;
- substantial colour;
- firm structure and freshness.

These are tendencies, not guarantees. Mazuelo does not automatically make a heavy, harsh or high-alcohol wine. Climate, ripeness, crop level, vine age, extraction, maturation and blending all influence the result.

In warmer conditions, ripe fruit and alcohol may become more prominent. In other settings, the wine may show more savoury character, acidity or tannin. Winemaking choices can also soften, emphasise or reshape the grape’s natural structure.

## Why is Mazuelo used in blends?

Mazuelo is commonly used as a blending grape. Its acidity, colour and tannin can add structure to a blend, helping give the finished wine energy and shape.

The Australian Wine Research Institute identifies Carignan as being blended with varieties including Syrah, Grenache and Mourvèdre. The exact role of Mazuelo depends on the other grapes and the winemaker’s approach; there is no single recipe that defines a Mazuelo blend.

Mazuelo can also be made as a varietal wine, meaning a wine centred on one grape variety. These wines can give the drinker a clearer view of the grape, although the region and production choices still matter.

## Will I find Mazuelo in Australia?

In Australian wine-industry references, **Carignan** is the more visible name. Wine Australia’s official grape-variety and synonym list records Carignan, Carignane and Mazuela, while the Australian Wine Research Institute’s variety profile is headed “Carignan”.

The variety has a documented but comparatively minor presence in Australian wine records. Wine Australia’s 2025 National Vintage Report lists Carignan among red varieties reported by at least three wineries. A McLaren Vale regional report recorded five hectares of Carignan in 2021. These figures are useful evidence that the variety is present, but they are not a complete national planting total.

So, if you are shopping in Australia, a bottle labelled **Carignan** may be referring to the same grape that a Spanish wine calls Mazuelo. The Australian industry also discusses Carignan in the context of varieties that may cope comparatively well with extreme heat, although vineyard site and management remain important.

## How should I approach a bottle labelled Mazuelo?

Think of the name as a clue to the wine’s possible structure:

1. Expect a red wine that may have noticeable freshness, colour and tannin.
2. Check whether the wine is labelled Mazuelo, Carignan or another regional name.
3. Look for information about the region and blend, because these can significantly change the style.
4. Do not assume the grape alone predicts body, alcohol, oak or fruit character.

The simplest answer is this: **Mazuelo is Carignan by another important name—a red grape capable of structured, colourful and lively wines, but one whose finished style is shaped by much more than the variety itself.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Mazuelo a red or white grape?

Mazuelo is a red wine-grape variety.

### Is Mazuelo the same as Cariñena?

Authoritative references identify Cariñena as a name associated with the same variety. The term is also connected with the Aragón town and wine region of Cariñena, so context matters.

### Is Mazuelo the same as Mazuela?

Mazuelo and Mazuela are Spanish spellings used for the same grape variety.

### Is Mazuelo always tannic?

No. The grape can contribute firm tannin, but ripeness, extraction, maturation, blending and other winemaking choices affect the texture of the finished wine.

### What is the Australian name for Mazuelo?

Carignan is the more visible name in Australian industry references. A particular Australian wine may use another supported name, but Mazuelo is less likely to be the primary term.
