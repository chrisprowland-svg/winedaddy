# What Is Touriga Nacional? A Beginner’s Guide

## Highlights

- Touriga Nacional is a dark-skinned Portuguese wine-grape variety.
- It is strongly associated with Portugal, especially the Douro and Port production.
- The same grape can also make dry, still red wine.
- Typical examples can be deeply coloured, aromatic and structured, with fresh acidity.
- Touriga Nacional is not the same thing as Port, and its name does not automatically mean sweet or fortified wine.
- In Australia, it is a small-scale alternative variety used in some varietal wines and blends.

## Quick answer

Touriga Nacional is a Portuguese red-wine grape variety best known for its role in Port and for producing deeply coloured, aromatic red wines.

The important distinction is simple: **Touriga Nacional is the grape; Port is a fortified wine style and protected Portuguese wine designation.** A wine labelled Touriga Nacional is therefore not automatically Port, sweet or high in alcohol. It may be a dry, still red wine, either made from the variety alone or blended with other grapes.

The variety can contribute dark colour, expressive aromas, rich fruit, fresh acidity and firm structure. These are tendencies rather than guarantees. Climate, ripeness, yields, extraction, blending and maturation all affect the finished wine.

For Australian drinkers, Touriga Nacional is still a niche variety rather than a mainstream one. Wine Australia recorded 672 tonnes crushed nationally in 2025, representing less than 0.1 per cent of the reported red-grape crush.

## Where does Touriga Nacional come from?

Touriga Nacional is strongly associated with Portugal. The Douro is particularly important because the variety is used in the region’s Port-related viticulture and wines. It is also closely associated with dry red wine in Portugal, including the country’s broader table-wine tradition.

It is worth keeping the names separate:

- **Touriga Nacional** is a grape variety.
- **Douro** is a wine region.
- **Port** is a fortified wine style and protected regional designation.

Touriga Nacional is also distinct from Touriga Franca. The names sound similar, but they refer to separate grape varieties.

In some variety references, **Touriga** is recorded as a synonym for Touriga Nacional. On a bottle, however, “Touriga Nacional” is the clearer name to look for. Do not assume that every use of “Touriga” means the same thing in every market.

## What does Touriga Nacional taste like?

Touriga Nacional often has the raw materials for a substantial red wine: strong colour, concentrated fruit, aromatic lift and structure.

You may notice:

- dark-fruit character;
- floral or spicy aromas;
- a rich or powerful texture;
- fresh acidity;
- tannin that gives the wine grip and shape.

Not every bottle will show all of these qualities. A dry, lightly handled wine may feel fresher and more open, while a richer or more extracted wine may feel denser and firmer. Fortification and sweetness also change the way the wine tastes.

“Tannin” is the drying, mouth-gripping sensation you may notice on your gums. It can help give red wine structure, but it does not mean every Touriga Nacional will be strongly tannic.

Likewise, dark fruit does not necessarily mean sweetness. A dry wine can smell richly of ripe fruit while containing little residual sugar.

## Can Touriga Nacional make dry red wine?

Yes. This is one of the most useful things to know about the variety.

Touriga Nacional can be used for:

- dry, still varietal red wine;
- dry red blends;
- fortified Port styles.

A dry still wine has had most of its grape sugar converted into alcohol during fermentation. It can still taste fruity, rich or concentrated. Port, by contrast, is fortified during production, creating a different style and often retaining sweetness.

So the grape name tells you **what variety is involved**. It does not, on its own, tell you whether the wine is dry, sweet, still or fortified.

## Touriga Nacional in Australia

Wine Australia describes Touriga Nacional as an alternative variety suited to warmer conditions. It has been associated with plantings or experimentation in parts of South Australia, Victoria and New South Wales, including examples around Bendigo, the Goulburn Valley, Rutherglen, the Hunter Valley, Mudgee and the Canberra District.

These associations should not be read as proof that every one of those areas is a major Touriga Nacional region. Australian production remains comparatively small. The variety may appear as a varietal wine, in a blend, or in Portuguese-style fortified and table-wine experimentation.

Australian examples are useful because they show how a variety can travel. A grape with Portuguese roots may retain its colour and structure in Australia while showing different levels of ripeness, freshness, spice or alcohol according to the site and winemaking choices.

## How should you read a Touriga Nacional label?

Start by asking what the label is telling you:

1. **Is Touriga Nacional named as the grape?** This points to variety identity.
2. **Does the label identify Port or another fortified style?** This tells you more about production style.
3. **Is a region named?** That provides place context, but the region and grape remain separate pieces of information.
4. **Does the wine say it is dry or sweet?** Do not infer sweetness from the grape name alone.

If you enjoy structured reds with expressive aromas and dark fruit, Touriga Nacional may be an interesting variety to explore. A dry Australian example can be very different from a fortified Portuguese wine, even though both use the same grape.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Touriga Nacional the same as Port?

No. Touriga Nacional is a grape variety. Port is a fortified wine style from Portugal that can include Touriga Nacional among several permitted varieties.

### Is Touriga Nacional always sweet?

No. It can be made into dry, still red wine as well as sweet fortified styles.

### Is Touriga Nacional the same as Touriga Franca?

No. They are separate Portuguese grape varieties.

### Is Touriga Nacional grown in Australia?

Yes. Wine Australia records Australian production and identifies its use in several states. It remains a small-scale alternative variety rather than a mainstream Australian grape.

### Is Touriga Nacional a full-bodied wine?

It can produce rich or powerful wines, but the finished style varies. Climate, ripeness, winemaking, blending, maturation, sweetness and fortification all matter.
