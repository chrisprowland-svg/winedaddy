# What is Arinto?

Arinto is a white wine-grape variety from Portugal. It is best known for its naturally high acidity, freshness and lively citrus-like character.

If you see Arinto on a wine list or bottle, expect a white wine that may feel bright and refreshing, with flavours such as lemon, apple or mineral-like notes. These are broad tendencies, not guarantees: the finished wine also depends on where the grapes grew, when they were picked, whether Arinto was blended with other varieties and how the wine was made.

## Highlights

- Arinto is a grape variety, not a region or a general style of wine.
- It is strongly associated with Portugal, especially Bucelas near Lisbon.
- In Vinho Verde, Arinto is known as **Pedernã**.
- Its most recognisable feature is usually high acidity and freshness.
- It can be made as a single-variety wine or used in a blend.
- Some examples may be suitable for sparkling wine because of their naturally fresh structure.
- Australian availability and labelling can vary between producers, importers and retailers.

## What does Arinto wine taste like?

Arinto is commonly valued for its freshness and acidity. In practical terms, that can make the wine feel lively in your mouth rather than soft or heavy.

Common flavour descriptions include lemon, apple, citrus and mineral notes. Some wines may be relatively light- to medium-bodied, while others can feel broader or rounder, particularly when the fruit is riper or the wine has been given more time on lees, in oak or through other maturation methods.

The important point is that Arinto does not guarantee one fixed flavour profile. A fresh young wine from a cooler site may emphasise crispness and citrus, while a riper or more developed example may show greater texture and complexity.

## Where is Arinto grown?

Arinto is principally associated with Portugal. One of its most important regional associations is **Bucelas**, a wine area north of Lisbon. Bucelas is a protected wine designation, whereas Arinto is the grape variety. They are related, but they are not the same thing.

Arinto is also used in **Vinho Verde**, where it is known as **Pedernã**. It is planted in other parts of Portugal as well, including warmer areas where its acidity can help bring freshness and balance to a blend.

You may therefore encounter several kinds of wording:

- **Arinto**: the grape name.
- **Arinto de Bucelas**: a fuller regional or historical form of the grape’s name.
- **Pedernã**: the name used for Arinto in Vinho Verde.
- **Bucelas**: a regional wine designation, not another name for the grape.

Similarly named forms, such as Arinto dos Açores, should not automatically be treated as identical to mainland Arinto.

## Is Arinto a single-variety wine or a blend?

It can be either. A bottle labelled with Arinto may be made predominantly or entirely from the grape, or Arinto may be blended with other white varieties.

In a blend, Arinto’s acidity can contribute freshness without determining the whole wine’s flavour. This is particularly useful in warmer regions, where acidity can help a wine feel more balanced.

Arinto’s fresh structure also makes it useful in some sparkling wines. That does not mean every Arinto is sparkling, dry or particularly light-bodied; the label and the producer’s winemaking choices still matter.

## How should you approach a bottle of Arinto?

If you enjoy fresh, lively white wines, Arinto may be a useful grape name to explore. Look for clues on the label about whether it is a varietal wine, a blend or a regional wine such as Bucelas or Vinho Verde.

A fresh Arinto is a natural partner for seafood, shellfish, grilled fish, salads and fresh cheeses. These are starting points rather than rules. A richer or oak-influenced version may suit more substantial food.

For Australian drinkers, Arinto may appear on some imported or Australian wine labels, but availability and labelling vary. The safest expectation is not “this will always taste the same”, but “this grape often brings freshness and acidity to the wine”.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Arinto a white wine?

Arinto is a white grape variety. Wines made from it are generally white, although Arinto can also be used in blends and some sparkling wines.

### Is Arinto the same as Vinho Verde?

No. Arinto is a grape variety. Vinho Verde is a Portuguese wine region and style. Arinto is one of the varieties used there, under the name Pedernã.

### Is Arinto the same as Bucelas?

No. Bucelas is a Portuguese wine designation strongly associated with Arinto. Arinto is the grape.
