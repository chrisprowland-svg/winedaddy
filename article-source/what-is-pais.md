# What is País?

## Highlights

- País is a historic red wine grape, strongly associated with Chile.
- It is also known as Listán Prieto and Mission in different historical and national contexts.
- País is a grape variety, not a wine region or a fixed style.
- Its wines can be light and juicy, rustic and savoury, or more structured and concentrated.
- Vineyard conditions, vine age, yields and winemaking choices can make a significant difference.
- Carbonic maceration can give País a brighter, fruitier and more approachable character.

## Quick answer

País is a historic red wine-grape variety, especially associated with Chile. Spanish missionaries brought it to the Americas, where it became one of the foundational grapes of early Chilean viticulture.

The variety is also known as **Listán Prieto** and **Mission**. DNA evidence identifies Chilean País as the same variety as Listán Prieto and the Mission grape historically grown in California. You may also see the name **Criolla Chica** in historical or regional records.

País is most closely associated with central and southern Chile, including dryland areas around Maule and Itata. Although it was once central to Chile’s wine industry, it was later displaced commercially by international varieties. Interest has since returned to old-vine and heritage-focused País wines.

## What does País wine taste like?

There is no single País flavour profile. The grape can produce noticeably different wines depending on the vineyard, vine age, crop level, ripeness and winemaking.

Some País wines are relatively light in colour, fresh and juicy, with a straightforward, easy-drinking character. Others are more rustic, savoury or structured, with firmer tannin and greater concentration. Wines from older vines or carefully managed sites may show more depth and detail.

Winemaking can also change the impression of the grape. **Carbonic maceration**, a fermentation method often used to create bright, aromatic fruit character, can make País taste lighter, juicier and more immediately approachable. That is a choice made in the winery, rather than a defining feature of every País wine.

So if you see País on a label, think of it as a starting point—not a guarantee that the wine will be light-bodied, full-bodied, low in tannin or rustic.

## Why is País important in Chile?

País has deep historical roots in Chile. It spread through the Americas with Spanish missionaries during the colonial period and adapted well in parts of central-southern Chile, including rainfed and dryland vineyards.

The variety was historically widespread and played a major role in the development of Chilean wine. Some remaining vineyards are described as centennial or old-vine plantings. However, that does not mean every País vineyard is old, dry-farmed or made in the same way.

Today, País is often discussed in connection with Chilean heritage grapes and renewed interest in lighter, fresher or more distinctive styles. Its history is important, but the wine in your glass still depends on how and where it was grown and made.

## País, Mission and Listán Prieto

Names can be confusing because grape varieties travelled between countries and were often given local names. In this case, authoritative genetic evidence supports País, Listán Prieto and California Mission as names for the same variety.

That does not mean every grape called “Mission”, “Criolla” or a similar historical name is automatically País. Local naming traditions can be complicated, so the country of origin and the producer’s label information still matter. País should also not be casually equated with Listán Negro.

## How might Australians encounter País?

Australian drinkers are most likely to encounter País in imported Chilean wine, specialist retail selections or conversations about heritage and natural wine. Current evidence does not support describing País as a major Australian grape variety or claiming widespread Australian production.

When reading a label, look for País as the grape name. Mission or another historical name may appear depending on the wine’s country of origin and labelling tradition.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is País a red wine or a grape?

País is a grape variety. It is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* grape that can be used to make red, rosé and other wine styles.

### Is País the same as Mission?

Yes. DNA evidence supports País and the California Mission grape as the same variety. Mission is the name most closely associated with its historical use in California.

### Is País always a light red wine?

No. País wines vary. Some are light, fresh and juicy, while others can be more structured, savoury or concentrated.

### Is País a Chilean wine region?

No. País is the name of a grape variety. Chile is its most important modern geographic and historical context, but the grape itself is not a region or appellation.
