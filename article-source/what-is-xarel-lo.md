# What is Xarel-lo?

Xarel-lo is a white wine grape variety from Catalonia, especially associated with the Penedès area of Spain. It is used to make still white wines and is one of the principal traditional grapes used in Cava, where it can contribute body, structure and freshness.

You may also see the grape written as **xarel·lo**, its Catalan form, or **Xarello**. In Alella, another Catalan wine region, it is known as **Pansa Blanca**.

## Highlights

- Xarel-lo is a **grape variety**, not a region or a wine style.
- It is strongly associated with **Catalonia and Penedès**.
- It is one of the traditional grapes used in **Cava**, alongside Macabeo and Parellada.
- Wines made from it can be fresh and fruity, or fuller and more textured.
- Its typical profile includes useful acidity, floral and fruit notes, and a structured feel.
- The grape’s name may appear as **Xarel-lo, xarel·lo or Xarello**.
- Pansa Blanca is a recognised regional synonym, particularly in Alella.
- In Australia, you are most likely to encounter it in imported Spanish or Catalan wines, especially sparkling wines and Cava.

## What does Xarel-lo taste like?

There is no single Xarel-lo flavour. The finished wine depends on where the grapes are grown, when they are picked and how the wine is made.

As a broad guide, Xarel-lo often produces wines with **medium or useful acidity**, medium to fuller body, and a firm or structured texture. Floral and fruity aromas are common descriptions. Young still wines may feel smooth, fresh and lively, while riper or barrel-aged examples can be fuller, warmer and more complex.

That difference is important. A grape variety provides tendencies, not guarantees. A fresh sparkling blend and a mature still wine made entirely from Xarel-lo may have very different personalities.

Some examples can also show savoury or unusual aromas. These are possibilities in particular wines, not characteristics that every bottle should display.

## Why is Xarel-lo used in Cava?

Cava is a Spanish sparkling-wine appellation. Its traditional grape context includes **Macabeo, Xarel-lo and Parellada**, although Cava regulations also allow other varieties.

In the blend, Xarel-lo is valued for the contribution it can make to **body, structure and freshness**. It can also support the sense of longevity in longer-aged Cavas.

You do not need to know the exact proportion of each grape to understand the idea: Macabeo, Xarel-lo and Parellada can work together, with Xarel-lo often providing useful weight and structural presence.

Xarel-lo is not limited to sparkling wine, however. It is also made as a still varietal wine, meaning a wine made primarily or entirely from that one grape.

## Where is Xarel-lo grown?

Xarel-lo’s strongest traditional association is with **Catalonia**, an autonomous region in north-eastern Spain. Within Catalonia, **Penedès** is the most recognisable centre of its identity and use.

It is also associated with the wider Cava production area and with **Alella**, where the name Pansa Blanca is used. These names refer to wine places and naming traditions, not to separate grape varieties for the purposes of this explainer.

So, if you see Pansa Blanca on a bottle associated with Alella, it may be referring to the same grape known elsewhere as Xarel-lo. Regional naming can vary, which is one reason the grape may seem unfamiliar even when you have encountered it before.

## Why can two Xarel-lo wines taste different?

Several winemaking decisions can change the result:

- **Harvest timing:** Earlier-picked grapes can retain more freshness and moderate potential alcohol, which can suit sparkling base wine.
- **Ripeness:** Riper grapes can make fuller wines with more alcohol and texture.
- **Fermentation and ageing:** Barrel fermentation or ageing can add weight and complexity compared with a young, fresh style.
- **Blending:** Xarel-lo used in Cava will taste different from a still wine made mainly from the variety.
- **Bottle age:** Ageing can change a wine’s texture and aromas over time.

These factors are why it is better to describe Xarel-lo as a grape with a range of expressions rather than assigning it one fixed taste.

## How might Australians encounter Xarel-lo?

For an Australian wine drinker, the most straightforward places to encounter Xarel-lo are imported Spanish or Catalan wines, Cava and other sparkling blends, or still white wines labelled Xarel-lo.

The label may use **Xarel-lo**, **xarel·lo** or **Xarello**. It may also use **Pansa Blanca** in the appropriate regional context. Australian labelling rules recognise grape-variety names through approved reference systems, but that does not mean every possible synonym will appear on every label.

There is not enough evidence to describe Xarel-lo as widely planted, rare or commercially significant in Australia. If you find an Australian example, treat it as an opportunity to explore rather than assuming it represents the variety everywhere.

## What food goes with Xarel-lo?

Start with the style of the wine.

Fresh, youthful Xarel-lo can work as a starting point for seafood, eggs, fresh cheeses and lightly prepared dishes. A fuller or barrel-aged version may be more comfortable alongside richer food.

Sparkling Xarel-lo, including a Cava blend, can be approached as a sparkling wine first: its bubbles and freshness make it useful with a wide range of lighter foods and snacks. These are broad pairing ideas, not rules. The wine’s sweetness, weight, acidity and texture still matter.

## Is Xarel-lo the same as Cava?

No. **Xarel-lo is a grape variety. Cava is a Spanish sparkling-wine appellation.** Xarel-lo can be used to make Cava, but it can also be made into still white wine.

That distinction is useful whenever you read a wine list or label. One name tells you about the grape; the other tells you about a regulated wine category and place of origin.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you pronounce Xarel-lo?

A definitive pronunciation was not established in the available evidence, so it is better not to rely on a fixed English phonetic guide. You can simply use the spelling shown on the label.

### Is Xarel-lo a white or red grape?

Xarel-lo is a white wine grape variety.

### Is Xarel-lo the same as Pansa Blanca?

Pansa Blanca is a recognised synonym associated particularly with Alella. Regional label use can vary, so treat it as a naming variant rather than automatically assuming every use in every context is identical without checking the wine’s origin.

### Is Xarel-lo only used in sparkling wine?

No. It is used in Cava and other sparkling contexts, but it is also made as a still white varietal wine.

### Should Xarel-lo always taste highly acidic?

No. Xarel-lo can provide freshness, but the evidence supports describing its acidity as useful or medium rather than claiming that every example is sharply high in acid.
