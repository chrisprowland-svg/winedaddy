# How to Open Sparkling Wine Safely

## Highlights

- Chill the bottle well and let it settle if it has been moved or shaken.
- Keep the bottle pointed away from people, pets, windows and fragile objects.
- Hold the cork firmly while loosening the wire cage.
- Rotate the bottle slowly—not the cork—to ease the cork out.
- Aim for a controlled sigh, not a flying cork or loud pop.
- Never use a corkscrew on a standard sparkling-wine cork.
- Do not open a bottle with cracked glass, a damaged neck or an unsafe-looking closure.

## Quick answer

To open sparkling wine safely, chill the bottle first and avoid shaking it. Remove the foil while keeping one hand firmly over the cork. Loosen the wire cage without releasing the cork, then point the bottle away from everyone and anything fragile. Hold the cork still and slowly rotate the bottle until the cork eases out with a quiet sigh.

This method is for a standard sparkling-wine bottle sealed with a cork and wire cage. Sparkling wine is under pressure, so the cork can move suddenly if it is not controlled.

## Step-by-step: opening sparkling wine safely

### 1. Chill and settle the bottle

Chill the bottle well before opening. A refrigerator or an ice bucket is suitable; around 8–10°C is a useful serving range for Champagne and similar sparkling wines.

If the bottle has just been transported, driven around or handled roughly, let it settle before opening. Do not shake it for a dramatic release. Movement can make the cork come out more quickly.

### 2. Choose a safe direction

Hold the bottle securely and point it away from faces, people, children, pets, windows, lights, glassware and other fragile objects. Keep this direction fixed while the cork is still in place.

A sparkling-wine bottle is pressurised. Comité Champagne gives approximately 5–6 bar for Champagne bottles, although pressure varies between wines and formats. You do not need to measure it: simply treat the cork as capable of moving suddenly.

### 3. Remove the foil while holding the cork

Dry the bottle if it is wet or slippery. A clean tea towel can provide extra grip.

Remove the foil or capsule using its tab where available. Keep one hand firmly over the cork throughout. Your hand—not the wire cage alone—keeps the cork under control.

### 4. Loosen the wire cage

The wire cage, also called a *muselet*, sits over the cork and neck. Loosen its wire loop while continuing to hold the cork firmly.

If the cork feels unstable after the wire has been loosened, do not force the next step. Keep control of the cork and seek assistance rather than improvising with a tool.

### 5. Angle the bottle

Tilt the bottle slightly—approximately 30–45°—while keeping it pointed away from people and fragile objects. This is a practical angle, not a rigid safety threshold. Firm control and a safe direction matter most.

### 6. Rotate the bottle, not the cork

Hold the cork still through the cloth or with your hand. With your other hand, slowly rotate the bottle. Let the cork rise gradually under your control.

Do not pull the cork forcefully or twist it hard. The preferred result is a quiet sigh as the cork releases, rather than a loud pop or cork flying across the room.

Never use a corkscrew on a standard sparkling-wine cork. Do not use a knife, sabre or improvised tool.

## What if the bottle or closure looks damaged?

Stop if the glass is cracked, the bottle neck is damaged, the cork looks defective or the closure appears unsafe. Do not try to open it carefully at home. Set it aside and ask the retailer or producer for advice.

These instructions also do not cover every format. A bottle with a crown cap, screw cap, unusual closure or producer-specific instructions may need different handling. Follow the producer’s directions rather than forcing the closure.

## After opening

Pour slowly and regularly to reduce foaming, and avoid overfilling glasses. If the bottle will not be finished, use a proper sparkling-wine stopper and keep it cold and upright.

For an Australian host, the simplest rule is also the most important: open the bottle before passing it around, keep guests clear of its direction and do not treat a flying cork as entertainment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should sparkling wine be cold before opening?

Yes. Chill it well before opening. A refrigerator or ice bucket is preferable to relying on a freezer, where a bottle may be forgotten and over-frozen.

### Should I twist the cork or the bottle?

Keep the cork still and slowly rotate the bottle. This gives you more control over the release.

### Should I remove the wire cage?

For a standard bottle, loosen and remove the cage while holding the cork firmly. If the cork feels unstable, keep it controlled and do not force the opening.

### Is a quiet sigh safer than a loud pop?

A quiet sigh indicates a controlled release and is the preferred serving result. It is not a formal safety test, so continue to handle the bottle carefully.
