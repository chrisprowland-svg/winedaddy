# What is spinning cone technology?

Spinning cone technology is a controlled separation process used in wine production to remove selected volatile compounds from a liquid. More precisely, it uses a piece of equipment called a **spinning cone column**.

Inside the column, rotating and stationary cones spread wine into very thin films. A controlled gas stream and reduced pressure help volatile substances move out of the wine. Depending on the process, those substances may include aroma compounds and alcohol.

A winemaker might use the technology to recover aroma, reduce a wine’s alcohol concentration or produce a de-alcoholised wine. However, spinning cone technology is broader than dealcoholisation, and it does not guarantee that treated wine will taste exactly like the starting wine.

## Highlights

- A spinning cone column contains alternating rotating and stationary cones.
- The cones spread wine into thin films, increasing contact between the wine and a gas stream.
- Reduced pressure and controlled conditions help selected volatile compounds leave the wine.
- Aroma compounds may be collected and potentially blended back into the treated wine.
- The process can adjust alcohol rather than remove most of it.
- Results depend on the original wine, the treatment goal and the way the equipment is operated.
- Aroma, flavour, acidity, body and sensory balance may change during treatment.

## How does a spinning cone column work?

Imagine spreading a small amount of liquid over a series of shallow surfaces rather than heating a deep tank of wine. A thin film has a larger surface area in contact with the surrounding gas, making it easier for volatile substances to transfer from the liquid into the gas phase.

A spinning cone column uses this principle in a vertical column:

1. Wine enters the column.
2. Rotating cones spread it into thin films across the cone surfaces.
3. A controlled gas stream passes through or across those films.
4. Reduced pressure helps volatile compounds transfer out of the wine.
5. A separated fraction is collected.
6. The wine may pass through a further controlled stage, such as an alcohol-reduction step.
7. Recovered aroma material may be blended back into the treated wine.

This is a simplified explanation rather than a universal operating recipe. Different systems can use different configurations and treatment targets.

The term **volatile compound** simply means a substance that transfers into the gas phase relatively easily. Many wine aroma compounds are volatile, but that does not mean every volatile compound is desirable or that every one will be recovered in the same proportion.

## Why recover aroma compounds?

Alcohol is not just a number on a label. In wine, it contributes to texture and overall balance, while volatile compounds contribute to fruit, floral and varietal aromas. Removing alcohol can therefore change the wine’s character as well as its alcohol concentration.

Aroma recovery creates an opportunity to retain or restore some of the wine’s aromatic character. In a common conceptual process, aroma material is removed and collected before a later alcohol-reduction stage. It may then be returned to the treated wine.

That does not mean the original aroma is perfectly preserved. Some compounds may be lost, changed or returned in different proportions. The aim is to manage the change, not to promise that no change will occur.

## Is spinning cone technology the same as dealcoholisation?

No. **Spinning cone technology is a separation technology; dealcoholisation is one possible application.**

A winemaker may use a spinning cone column for:

### Partial alcohol reduction

Some wine’s alcohol can be removed to adjust the final concentration. This is sometimes called alcohol adjustment or alcohol reduction. It does not necessarily involve removing most of the alcohol.

### De-alcoholised wine

Wine Australia describes de-alcoholised wine as fermented wine from which most of the alcohol has been removed before bottling. Spinning cone technology can form part of that process, including the recovery and possible reintroduction of aroma compounds.

The words **low alcohol**, **reduced alcohol** and **de-alcoholised** should not be treated as interchangeable. They describe different product concepts, and the appropriate description depends on the finished beverage and applicable requirements.

In Australia, the process used does not by itself decide how a beverage may legally be labelled. The finished product, its alcohol concentration, ingredients or processing aids and relevant Food Standards Code requirements all matter. A process description alone is not a label claim.

## How is it different from boiling, distillation and reverse osmosis?

Spinning cone technology is related to familiar separation methods, but it is not simply ordinary boiling.

- **Ordinary evaporation** uses the tendency of volatile substances to leave a liquid, commonly with heat. Spinning cone columns instead use thin films, gas contact, reduced pressure and controlled processing.
- **Distillation** separates substances according to differences in volatility, usually by creating vapour and then condensing it. A spinning cone column uses the same broad idea that volatile substances behave differently, but applies it through a specialised thin-film, gas-stripping and reduced-pressure design.
- **Reverse osmosis** uses pressure to move part of a liquid through a membrane. It is a membrane-separation process, not a cone column or gas-stripping process.

These technologies can be used as different approaches to alcohol reduction. None should automatically be assumed to produce the same result.

## What are the potential advantages?

A spinning cone column can offer:

- **Controlled separation:** Different volatile fractions may be targeted through staged or carefully managed treatment.
- **Aroma-recovery potential:** Aroma material removed before alcohol reduction may be collected for possible reintroduction.
- **Reduced thermal exposure compared with ordinary atmospheric boiling:** Reduced pressure and thin-film operation can support separation without treating the wine like a pot left boiling in the open air.
- **Flexibility:** The technology may be used for partial alcohol adjustment, more extensive alcohol removal or aroma-related applications.

These are potential process advantages, not guarantees of better wine. The result still depends on the wine and the decisions made during treatment.

## What are the limitations?

Spinning cone technology requires specialised equipment, pumping, gas handling, pressure control and trained operation. It is more complex than simply heating wine.

The wine may also change during treatment. Studies report changes in volatile compounds and sensory properties, and alcohol reduction can affect perceived acidity, flavour, body and balance. Aroma recovery may reduce some losses, but it cannot restore every compound in exactly its original form.

The outcome depends on factors such as:

- the starting wine’s variety and aroma profile;
- its alcohol concentration and acidity;
- the desired level of alcohol reduction;
- the process configuration; and
- the operating choices used for that particular wine.

That is why the safest conclusion is simple: spinning cone technology can help manage wine’s alcohol and aroma, but the treated wine is not guaranteed to be identical to the original.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does spinning cone technology remove only alcohol?

No. It can remove or recover selected volatile compounds, including aroma material. Alcohol reduction is one application, not the definition of the technology.

### Does it make wine alcohol-free?

Not automatically. The finished alcohol concentration depends on the treatment. A product’s description and labelling depend on the finished beverage and applicable requirements.

### Does aroma recovery make the wine taste unchanged?

No. Aroma recovery is intended to help retain or restore character, but some volatile and sensory changes may still occur.

### Is spinning cone technology a type of reverse osmosis?

No. Spinning cone columns use thin liquid films, gas contact and reduced pressure. Reverse osmosis uses a pressure-driven membrane.

### Why would a winemaker use it?

A winemaker may use it to adjust alcohol, support production of de-alcoholised wine or recover aroma compounds during a controlled separation process.

## The simple takeaway

Spinning cone technology is a specialised way of separating volatile substances from wine. Rotating cones create thin films, while gas flow and reduced pressure help move selected compounds out of the liquid. Aroma fractions may be recovered and returned, and alcohol can be reduced in a later or separately controlled stage.

It is a tool for managing wine’s composition—not a guarantee that the final wine will be unchanged.
