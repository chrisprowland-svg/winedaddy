# Burgundy Premier Cru explained

## Highlights

- **Premier Cru is a regulated Burgundy designation**, not a grape variety or broad region.
- It applies to specifically recognised and delimited vineyard sites called **Climats**.
- Premier Cru sits between village-level and Grand Cru designations in the commonly presented Burgundy hierarchy.
- A Premier Cru label usually connects a village appellation with a named Climat.
- Premier Cru does not guarantee that every wine will taste better or suit every drinker.
- Burgundy’s system is more site-specific than Australia’s usual GI labelling framework.
- Premier Cru wines can be red or white, depending on the appellation and permitted grape varieties.

## Quick answer: what does Burgundy Premier Cru mean?

Burgundy Premier Cru means that a wine is made under a relevant Burgundy village appellation from grapes grown in a specifically recognised vineyard site, known in Burgundy as a **Climat**.

The term is therefore both geographic and regulatory. It tells you that the wine comes from a defined place within a village appellation and that the site has Premier Cru status under the applicable appellation rules.

It does **not** tell you that the wine is made from one particular grape, has one universal flavour profile, comes from one producer or will automatically be better than every village-level wine. Producer choices, vintage, farming, winemaking, bottle age and your own preferences still matter.

## Where is Burgundy Premier Cru found?

Burgundy is a long, north–south wine region in eastern France. Its wine areas run broadly between the Auxerre area in the north and Mâcon in the south.

For orientation:

- **Auxerre** is a useful regional reference point for Chablis.
- **Dijon** helps orientate the northern Côte d’Or.
- **Beaune** is a key centre for the Côte de Beaune and Burgundy wine geography.
- **Mâcon** is a useful reference point for the Mâconnais.

From Paris, Burgundy is broadly south-east and is described by the Burgundy wine authority as roughly two hours away by road. Treat that as general orientation, not a precise travel promise.

Premier Cru is encountered in several Burgundy areas, including:

- **Chablis and the Grand Auxerrois**
- **Côte de Nuits**
- **Côte de Beaune**
- **Mâconnais**

That means Premier Cru is not limited to the Côte d’Or. Chablis Premier Cru and Pouilly-Fuissé Premier Cru, for example, show how the designation appears in different parts of Burgundy.

For broader context, see our guides to [Burgundy](/burgundy/), the [Côte de Nuits](/cote-de-nuits/) and the [Côte de Beaune](/cote-de-beaune/).

## How the Burgundy hierarchy works

A useful beginner’s version of the Burgundy hierarchy is:

1. **Regional appellations**
2. **Village appellations**
3. **Village Premier Cru appellations or Premier Cru Climats**
4. **Grand Cru appellations**

This is a helpful way to understand the increasing specificity of the place named on the label. But it should not be read as a simple league table of guaranteed quality.

Premier Cru operates through the rules of the relevant village appellation. Grand Cru appellations, by contrast, have their own individual specifications. So Premier Cru is not one single Burgundy-wide appellation with one universal rulebook.

This is also why it is better to think of Premier Cru as a regulated classification of particular sites within village appellations, rather than as a separate subregion alongside the Côte de Nuits or Côte de Beaune.

## What is a Climat?

A **Climat** is a named, historically recognised and precisely delimited vineyard site in Burgundy. Its identity relates to its location and physical characteristics.

On a label, a Climat name gives more specific information than the village appellation alone. However, a named place is not automatically Premier Cru. Burgundy also uses the term **lieu-dit** for a named place, and a lieu-dit may be classified at village level rather than Premier Cru level.

In simple terms:

- **Village appellation:** the broader legally defined area.
- **Climat:** the named, specific vineyard site.
- **Premier Cru:** the recognised classification status of certain Climats.
- **Lieu-dit:** a named place, which is not automatically Premier Cru.

## How to read a Premier Cru label

A common label structure is:

**[Village appellation] Premier Cru [Climat name]**

A useful example is:

**Chablis Premier Cru Montée de Tonnerre**

Here:

- **Chablis** identifies the village appellation.
- **Premier Cru** identifies the classification status.
- **Montée de Tonnerre** identifies the named Climat.

You may also see **1er Cru**, a shortened form used in Burgundy label presentation. Exact wording and order depend on the relevant appellation rules and the label design.

A label may also show the vintage, producer and bottler. Those details describe the particular bottle, but they are not what makes the site Premier Cru.

Premier Cru does not necessarily need to be accompanied by a grape variety on the front label. Do not assume the grape solely from the words “Premier Cru”. For more general label help, see [how to read a wine label](/how-to-read-a-wine-label/).

## Premier Cru versus Grand Cru

Both Premier Cru and Grand Cru refer to Burgundy’s structured approach to origin and site classification, but they are not interchangeable.

A Premier Cru wine is linked to a village appellation and a recognised Premier Cru Climat. A Grand Cru generally has its own individual appellation specification, and the Grand Cru name commonly takes prominence on the label rather than being presented as a village-plus-Premier-Cru combination.

The important point is structural. “Grand” does not mean that every Grand Cru will be more enjoyable to every person, just as “Premier” does not guarantee that every Premier Cru wine will outperform every village wine.

## What do Premier Cru wines taste like?

There is no single Premier Cru taste profile.

Many Premier Cru wines from the Côte de Nuits are red wines based on Pinot Noir. The Côte de Beaune includes both red and white Premier Cru expressions. Chablis Premier Cru is associated with Chardonnay, as is Pouilly-Fuissé Premier Cru in the Mâconnais.

That means a Premier Cru wine may be red or white, and its style depends on the appellation, grape variety, site, vintage and winemaking choices. A site classification can make the wine’s origin more specific, but it does not prescribe one fixed level of body, flavour, acidity, oak or ageworthiness.

You can explore the main grape contexts in our guides to [Pinot Noir](/what-is-pinot-noir/) and [Chardonnay](/what-is-chardonnay/).

## What should Australian wine drinkers compare it with?

Australian Geographic Indications, or GIs, identify geographic origin under the Australian wine labelling framework. Australian zones, regions and subregions help communicate where grapes or wine come from.

Burgundy Premier Cru adds another layer: it identifies recognised vineyard sites within relevant village appellations. It is therefore not the French equivalent of an Australian GI, and an Australian region, subregion or named vineyard should not automatically be translated as Premier Cru.

The two systems organise geographic information differently. That difference does not mean one country’s wines are inherently better. It simply means that Burgundy’s labels often express a particularly detailed relationship between village, vineyard site and classification.

## The takeaway

When you see **Premier Cru** on a Burgundy label, think:

**A recognised vineyard site within a village appellation, with a more specific origin designation than ordinary village wine, but not a guarantee of personal preference or absolute superiority.**

That one idea makes Burgundy labels much easier to understand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Premier Cru a grape variety?

No. Premier Cru is a regulated Burgundy designation connected to recognised vineyard sites. It is not a grape variety.

### Is Premier Cru better than village wine?

Premier Cru occupies a higher level in the conventional Burgundy hierarchy, but that does not guarantee that every Premier Cru wine will be better or more enjoyable than every village wine.

### Is Premier Cru the same as Grand Cru?

No. Premier Cru is linked to village appellations and recognised Climats. Grand Cru appellations have their own individual specifications.

### Are all Burgundy Premier Cru wines red?

No. Premier Cru wines can be red or white. The colour and grape depend on the relevant appellation.

### Is every named Burgundy vineyard Premier Cru?

No. A named lieu-dit or vineyard site is not automatically classified as Premier Cru.
