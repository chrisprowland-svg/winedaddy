# How Long Does Wine Last After Opening?

## Highlights

- Most opened still wine is best enjoyed within a few days, not weeks.
- Refrigerated white wine and rosé usually offer about two to three days.
- Red wine commonly holds for about three to five days when resealed and kept cool.
- Sparkling wine is a separate case because its bubbles escape after opening.
- Fortified and sweet wines can last much longer, but the style matters.
- Refrigeration slows quality loss; it does not reverse oxidation or restore fizz.
- These are quality windows, not fixed safety deadlines. Smell and taste the wine before deciding what to do with it.

## Quick answer

How long wine lasts after opening depends on its style, temperature, closure, oxygen exposure and how much wine remains in the bottle.

As a practical guide, promptly resealed and refrigerated **white wine and rosé are usually best within two to three days**. **Red wine commonly remains enjoyable for three to five days**, although delicate wines may fade sooner and some structured reds may hold longer.

**Sparkling wine may retain useful fizz for several days** with a proper sparkling-wine stopper and refrigeration. **Fortified wine can last from about a week to several months**, depending on whether it is a delicate style such as Fino Sherry or a more oxidative style such as Madeira. **Sweet wines often last more than a week** when sealed and refrigerated.

These timeframes describe likely quality and enjoyment, not a guaranteed expiry date. A wine can be past its best while still being harmless, or it may be unsuitable because it smells abnormal, has been contaminated or has been poorly handled.

## How long different wines last

| Wine style | Practical quality guide after opening |
|---|---|
| White wine and rosé | About two to three days in the fridge when resealed |
| Still red wine | About three to five days when resealed and kept cool |
| Sparkling wine | Several days with a proper sparkling-wine stopper, though fizz fades |
| Fino or Manzanilla Sherry | About one week when closed and refrigerated |
| Other Sherry styles | Up to about two months, depending on style |
| Sweet wine | Often more than a week in the fridge |
| Madeira and Marsala | May last months, depending on the wine |

Treat these as useful starting points rather than promises. A nearly empty bottle usually declines faster because it contains more air above the wine.

## Why opened wine changes

Opening a bottle allows air to enter. A little oxygen can initially help some wines open up, but continued exposure gradually changes the wine. This process is called **oxidation**.

As oxidation progresses, fresh fruit can become muted and the wine may taste dull or stale. Its colour may darken or turn brown, and it may develop bruised-apple, nutty or honeyed characters. You can learn more in [What is oxidation in wine?](/what-is-oxidation-in-wine/).

Temperature also matters. Warm conditions speed up quality changes, so an opened bottle left on a kitchen bench is likely to fade faster than one kept cold. This is particularly useful to remember in Australian homes during summer.

## How to give an opened bottle the best chance

You do not need cellar conditions for ordinary opened-wine storage. A domestic fridge is usually the most practical option.

- Reseal the bottle promptly after pouring.
- Keep it upright.
- Refrigerate opened white, rosé and sparkling wine.
- Refrigerate red wine too if extending its quality is the priority; allow it to warm slightly before drinking.
- Keep the bottle away from heat and direct light.
- If only a small amount remains, consider transferring it to a clean, smaller bottle with a secure closure to reduce the air space.

This page focuses on how long wine lasts and how to judge it. For the fuller storage procedure, see [How to store an open bottle of wine](/how-to-store-open-wine/).

Refrigeration slows quality loss but cannot reverse oxidation. It also cannot bring back bubbles that have already escaped.

## Sparkling wine needs different treatment

Sparkling wine loses carbon dioxide after opening, which is why it can become flat even when its underlying flavour remains acceptable.

Use a proper sparkling-wine stopper that grips the bottle securely and keep the bottle cold. An ordinary loose closure is not equivalent. A teaspoon placed in the bottle neck does not reliably preserve the bubbles.

For safe opening technique, see [How to open sparkling wine safely](/how-to-open-sparkling-wine-safely/).

## How to tell whether wine is past its best

Smell the wine before tasting it. Look for:

- fruit that seems faded or muted;
- a brown or unexpectedly dark colour;
- bruised-apple, nutty, honeyed or stale aromas;
- vinegar-like or nail-polish-like notes;
- a flat, tired character;
- reduced fizz in sparkling wine.

Some wines are deliberately made with oxidative characters, so a nutty or honeyed note is not automatically a fault. The question is whether the wine still smells and tastes like the style it was intended to be.

If it smells mouldy, strongly vinegary, chemically unpleasant or otherwise abnormal, discard it. Do not taste a wine you are uncomfortable with.

## Quality is not the same as safety

There is no useful universal Australian rule saying that an opened bottle becomes unsafe after exactly three, five or seven days. Most post-opening advice concerns quality and drinkability.

That does not mean every old bottle should automatically be consumed. If the wine has been contaminated, handled poorly or seems abnormal, throw it out. When in doubt, discard it rather than trying to rescue it.

## Can you cook with opened wine?

Wine that has lost some freshness but still smells clean and suitable can often be used in cooking, such as in a sauce, gravy, soup or risotto. Freezing leftover wine in small portions is another practical option.

Cooking does not reliably fix wine that smells mouldy, strongly vinegary, chemically unpleasant or contaminated. If you would not want its character in the dish, discard it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does red wine need to go in the fridge after opening?

It can. Refrigeration slows quality change, even though red wine is usually served warmer than white wine. Let it move towards its preferred serving temperature before drinking.

### Can opened wine last a week?

Sometimes, but it depends on the style and storage. A delicate white or rosé may fade before then, while some sweet or fortified wines can remain enjoyable for a week or much longer.

### Does a screwcap make opened wine last longer than a cork?

A secure closure helps limit further air exchange, but the closure alone does not determine the wine’s lifespan. Style, temperature, headspace and how quickly the bottle was resealed also matter.

### Is wine still drinkable after its suggested timeframe?

Possibly. The timeframes are quality guides, not automatic deadlines. Smell and taste a suitable-looking wine carefully, and discard it if it seems abnormal or unpleasant.

### Is storing opened wine the same as cellaring wine?

No. Opened-bottle storage is about slowing short-term quality loss. [How to cellar wine](/how-to-cellar-wine/) concerns longer-term storage of unopened bottles.
