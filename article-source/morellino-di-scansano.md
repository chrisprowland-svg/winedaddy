# Morellino di Scansano: A Beginner’s Guide to This Tuscan Red

## Highlights

- Morellino di Scansano is a protected red-wine designation from southern Tuscany, Italy.
- It comes from the hilly Province of Grosseto, in the broader Maremma area.
- The wine is based on Sangiovese, but Morellino di Scansano is a place name—not a grape variety.
- Standard and Superiore wines must contain at least 85% Sangiovese; Riserva must contain at least 90%.
- The main regulated categories are Morellino di Scansano, Morellino di Scansano Superiore and Morellino di Scansano Riserva.
- Expect a dry red that may combine ripe fruit, Mediterranean warmth, freshness and tannic grip.
- A label may foreground the appellation rather than the grape, which is different from the way many Australian wines are labelled.

## Quick answer: what is Morellino di Scansano?

Morellino di Scansano is a protected Italian red-wine designation from the hills of the Province of Grosseto in southern Tuscany’s Maremma. It is an Italian **DOCG** and a European Union **PDO**—both systems protect a geographic name and connect it with defined production rules.

The wine is Sangiovese-led. Standard Morellino di Scansano and Morellino di Scansano Superiore must contain at least 85% Sangiovese, while Riserva must contain at least 90%. Other permitted non-aromatic red grapes suitable for cultivation in Tuscany may make up the balance.

The important recognition lesson is simple: **Morellino di Scansano is the name of a place and appellation, not the name of a grape.** Sangiovese is the principal grape. “Morellino” is also a local name historically associated with Sangiovese in this area.

## Where is Morellino di Scansano?

Morellino di Scansano sits within:

**Morellino di Scansano → Province of Grosseto → Tuscany → Italy**

The appellation is in southern Tuscany’s broader Maremma. Maremma is a larger geographic area, however, and is not a synonym for the Morellino di Scansano DOCG.

Scansano is the defining town. The formal production area includes the entire municipality of Scansano, plus parts of Manciano, Magliano in Toscana, Grosseto, Campagnatico, Semproniano and Roccalbegna. “Parts” matters here: the appellation does not automatically cover every vineyard or locality within each municipality.

Grosseto is the nearest useful regional centre. From Rome, Scansano is approximately 165 kilometres by road and around 2 hours 17 minutes in normal conditions. Treat that as orientation rather than a fixed travel promise: routes, traffic and starting points vary.

On a map, the legal area is the hilly belt between the Ombrone and Albegna rivers. The specification also excludes valley-floor sites from suitable vineyard land. A broad orientation map such as **VIS-024** can help, provided it does not shade the entire Maremma or every named municipality as part of the DOCG.

## What grapes are used?

Sangiovese must make up the majority of every Morellino di Scansano category:

- **Standard Morellino di Scansano:** at least 85% Sangiovese
- **Morellino di Scansano Superiore:** at least 85% Sangiovese
- **Morellino di Scansano Riserva:** at least 90% Sangiovese

The remaining wine may include other permitted non-aromatic red grapes suitable for cultivation in Tuscany. There is no single mandatory supporting blend, so bottles need not all taste or be composed in exactly the same way.

For an Australian learner, this is a useful contrast with a familiar label such as “Sangiovese”, which foregrounds the grape. An Italian appellation label may instead foreground the protected place name.

## What styles and categories should you expect?

The standard category is simply labelled **Morellino di Scansano**. The specification also recognises two regulated mentions:

### Morellino di Scansano Superiore

Superiore has a later permitted release date and higher minimum natural alcohol requirements than standard Morellino. It is a regulated category, not merely a producer’s description.

### Morellino di Scansano Riserva

Riserva requires at least two years of ageing, including at least one year in wooden vessels. That requirement can affect texture and development, but “Riserva” is not a universal guarantee that every bottle will suit every drinker.

The DOCG system describes the wines as red, ruby-coloured and tending towards garnet with age, with an intense and fine aroma. The palate is described as dry, warm and slightly tannic.

In everyday language, a Morellino may show ripe fruit, Mediterranean warmth, freshness and light-to-moderate tannic grip. That is a useful starting expectation—not a tasting guarantee. Site, vintage, supporting grapes, winemaking and ageing can all change the result.

## What do place and climate contribute?

The appellation covers varied terrain, from lower coastal sections and gentle inland slopes to steeper eastern hills. Industry descriptions also point to a broadly Mediterranean climate, sea influence, warm dry summers, varied elevations and different soil types.

These conditions help explain why a Sangiovese-based wine from the area can combine generous fruit and warmth with freshness or savoury structure. They do not mean every bottle will show the same balance. Avoid assuming that all Morellino is light, powerful, rustic or full-bodied.

## How might the name appear on a label?

You may see:

- **Morellino di Scansano DOCG**
- **Morellino di Scansano Superiore DOCG**
- **Morellino di Scansano Riserva DOCG**

The broader geographic name **Toscana** may also appear in a regulated position after the protected designation and below the DOCG or PDO wording. It refers to the wider region, not to a different grape.

A useful label-reading shortcut is:

- **Morellino di Scansano:** the protected place and designation
- **Sangiovese:** the principal grape
- **DOCG:** the Italian controlled and guaranteed designation of origin
- **Superiore or Riserva:** regulated category terms
- **Toscana:** the broader Tuscan regional reference

A generic annotated label visual such as **VIS-009** could reinforce this distinction.

## How is it different from other Tuscan wines?

Morellino di Scansano is one specific designation within Tuscany. It is not the same as Tuscany generally, or the broader Maremma.

It is also separate from Chianti, Chianti Classico, Brunello di Montalcino, Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, Montecucco and Bolgheri. Some of these wines may also be associated with Sangiovese or southern Tuscany, but each name refers to a different geographic and regulatory system. Bolgheri, for example, is a separate coastal appellation in the Province of Livorno.

The point is not to rank these wines. It is to understand that similar grape varieties or nearby landscapes do not make appellations interchangeable.

## What should an Australian drinker do with this information?

If you encounter Morellino di Scansano in Australia, the name tells you that you are looking at a protected Italian red from a defined part of southern Tuscany. You can also expect Sangiovese to form the majority of the blend and the wine to be dry by category.

For a general food starting point, consider savoury dishes such as tomato-based pasta, mushrooms, grilled meats or hard cheese. Serve it as a dry red rather than assuming it will resemble an Australian Sangiovese or another Tuscan appellation exactly.

Most importantly, read the place name as carefully as you would read the grape name. With Morellino di Scansano, the geography is doing much of the talking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Morellino di Scansano a grape?

No. It is a protected geographic wine designation. Sangiovese is the principal grape used in the wine.

### Is Morellino di Scansano the same as Tuscany?

No. Tuscany is the larger Italian region. Morellino di Scansano is a specific DOCG within Tuscany.

### Is Morellino di Scansano always made entirely from Sangiovese?

No. Standard and Superiore require at least 85% Sangiovese, while Riserva requires at least 90%. Other permitted Tuscan red grapes may make up the balance.

### Does DOCG mean the wine is automatically better?

No. DOCG indicates a regulated Italian geographic designation. It does not guarantee that every DOCG wine will be better suited to every drinker than every wine with another classification.

### What does Riserva mean here?

For Morellino di Scansano, Riserva is a regulated category requiring at least two years of ageing, including at least one year in wood.
