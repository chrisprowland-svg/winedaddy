# Cuvée meaning: what does cuvée mean in wine?

## Highlights

- **Cuvée** is a French-derived wine term with different meanings depending on the producer and context.
- It often identifies a particular **blend, batch, lot, selection or named bottling**.
- A cuvée may contain several grape varieties—or just one.
- On a sparkling wine, *cuvée* can have a technical production meaning.
- In Champagne, it can also refer specifically to the first pressing fraction.
- The word does **not automatically mean premium, expensive, sweet, sparkling or better quality**.
- On an Australian label, read *cuvée* alongside the variety, vintage, GI and product description.

## Quick answer: what does cuvée mean?

In wine, **cuvée usually means a particular wine selection**. It might be a blend of different grape varieties, vineyard parcels, vintages or finished wines. It could also be a particular tank, barrel selection, production batch or named bottling.

The important point is that **there is no single universal meaning**. A producer may use *cuvée* to distinguish one wine from another, while a technical source may use it for a wine prepared for a particular stage of sparkling-wine production.

So, if you see “Cuvée” on a bottle, treat it as a clue about the wine’s identity or production context—not as a guarantee of quality or style.

## Cuvée and blend: are they the same?

*Cuvée* and *blend* overlap, but they are not exactly the same.

**Blending** is the winemaking action of combining wines, grape varieties, vineyard lots, barrels or vintages. A **cuvée** may be the resulting selection, batch or named wine.

For example, a cuvée might be:

- a blend of Shiraz and Grenache;
- a selection from several vineyard parcels;
- a combination of wines from different vintages;
- a single-variety wine from a particular lot;
- a producer’s named bottling.

That means a cuvée does not necessarily contain multiple grape varieties. Nor is every blend called a cuvée.

For more detail, see [wine blends](/wine-blends/) and [blending wine](/blending-wine/).

## What does cuvée mean for still wine?

For a still wine, *cuvée* commonly identifies a distinct bottling, blend, batch or selection. It may be used in the wine’s name, such as a hypothetical “Cuvée Blanc”.

That name alone does not tell you:

- which grape variety is used;
- whether the wine is a blend;
- where the grapes came from;
- whether it is dry or sweet;
- whether it is aged in oak;
- whether it is sparkling.

To understand the bottle, look for the rest of the label. A grape variety, vintage or Australian Geographic Indication (GI) is separate information from the word *cuvée*.

## What does cuvée mean in sparkling wine?

In sparkling-wine production, *cuvée* can refer to the wine or mixture prepared for a later fermentation stage. The International Organisation of Vine and Wine describes a sparkling-wine cuvée as a product destined for secondary fermentation, which may include base wine, must or combinations of these materials.

For a beginner, the useful takeaway is simple: **in this context, cuvée can describe the wine prepared for making sparkling wine**.

That technical meaning is not the same as saying that every bottle labelled *cuvée* is sparkling. A still wine can also use the word as part of its name.

You can learn more about the style in [what is sparkling wine?](/what-is-sparkling-wine/) and [traditional-method sparkling wine production](/traditional-method-sparkling-wine-production/).

### A Champagne-specific meaning

In Champagne production, *cuvée* has a more specific technical meaning. It refers to the first pressing fraction of the grapes, separated from the later *taille* fraction.

This is a Champagne production term. It should not be treated as the definition of *cuvée* on every Australian or imported wine label.

## Does cuvée mean premium wine?

No. **Cuvée does not automatically mean premium or superior quality.**

Some producers use the word for a flagship or specially selected wine. The phrase *prestige cuvée* can indicate a producer’s top-positioned bottling, particularly in Champagne discussions. But that is positioning within a producer’s range, not a universal quality grade.

A cuvée may be expensive or inexpensive, complex or straightforward, sparkling or still. The word itself does not settle those questions.

Similarly, *reserve cuvée* combines two terms whose meanings depend on the producer and jurisdiction. It does not automatically establish a formal quality tier. See [reserve wine meaning](/reserve-wine-meaning/) for the separate meaning of *reserve*.

## Cuvée versus other wine terms

### Cuvée versus reserve

A *cuvée* usually identifies a particular selection, batch, blend or bottling. *Reserve* often identifies a separate producer selection or range, but its meaning varies.

The terms can appear together, but neither word should be assumed to guarantee quality.

### Cuvée versus single-varietal wine

A single-varietal wine is made principally from one grape variety under the applicable labelling rules. It can still be a cuvée if the producer uses *cuvée* to identify a particular lot or bottling.

So, *cuvée* describes the wine’s selection or identity; it does not necessarily describe how many varieties it contains.

### Cuvée versus field blend

A field blend usually involves different grape varieties grown together and harvested or fermented together. A cuvée may instead combine wines or lots that were produced separately.

They are different ideas: one concerns a vineyard and its harvesting relationship, while the other can concern selection, blending or bottling.

### Cuvée versus vintage

*Cuvée* does not tell you the harvest year. A sparkling cuvée may be vintage or non-vintage, and the vintage should be identified separately on the label.

If a year is important to you, look for an explicit vintage statement rather than inferring one from the word *cuvée*. You can read more in [vintage wine versus non-vintage wine](/vintage-wine-vs-non-vintage-wine/).

## How should Australians read cuvée on a label?

An Australian wine drinker may encounter *cuvée* on:

- an Australian still wine;
- an Australian sparkling wine;
- imported sparkling wine;
- a named bottling;
- a wine labelled with a variety, vintage or GI.

On an Australian bottle, the safest approach is to treat *cuvée* as context-dependent descriptive or product-name language. Read it separately from information such as:

- the product description;
- alcohol content and standard drinks;
- volume;
- country of origin;
- grape variety;
- vintage;
- GI;
- lot and allergen information where applicable.

Wine Australia treats variety, vintage and GI claims as separate claims that must meet applicable substantiation and blending requirements. Those claims should not be inferred from *cuvée*. Australian labelling rules also require product descriptions to convey the true nature of the food.

In practical terms, “Cuvée” may tell you that the producer has given a particular selection or bottling its own identity. The rest of the label tells you what kind of wine it is.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is cuvée always sparkling?

No. It can appear on still wine as well as sparkling wine. In sparkling production, it has a technical meaning, but the word alone does not prove that a bottle is sparkling.

### Is cuvée always a blend?

No. A cuvée may be a blend, but it may also be a selected single-variety lot, batch or bottling.

### Does cuvée mean sweet wine?

No. It does not indicate sweetness or dryness.

### Does cuvée mean Champagne?

No. Champagne has a specific technical use of the term, but *cuvée* is also used in other regions and on other types of wine.

### How do you pronounce cuvée?

It is commonly pronounced approximately **“koo-vay”**. The accent on the final *é* indicates an “ay” sound.

## The simplest way to remember it

Think of **cuvée as a name for a particular wine selection**. It might be a blend, batch, lot, pressing fraction or named bottling, depending on the context.

When you see it on a bottle, stay curious—but check the rest of the label before deciding what the wine is.
