Gattinara: a beginner’s guide to this northern Italian Nebbiolo wine

Gattinara is a DOCG red-wine appellation in Piedmont, Italy. The name also belongs to the municipality where the appellation’s grapes must be grown.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Gattinara is both a town in northern Italy and the name of a protected red-wine appellation. Its wines come from the municipality of Gattinara in Piedmont’s Province of Vercelli and are based primarily on Nebbiolo.

The result is a distinctive northern Piedmont expression of Nebbiolo: generally dry, fresh and structured, with firm tannin, noticeable acidity and the capacity to develop with age.

Highlights

  • Gattinara is a municipality and a regulated wine appellation.
  • It sits in Piedmont, in northern Italy’s Province of Vercelli.
  • Gattinara DOCG covers the entire municipality of Gattinara—not the whole of Alto Piemonte.
  • Nebbiolo, locally called Spanna, must make up 90–100% of the wine’s vineyard composition.
  • Limited amounts of Vespolina and Uva Rara may also be permitted.
  • The wines are typically dry, acid-driven and tannic, with spice and floral notes.
  • Gattinara is a separate appellation from Barolo and Barbaresco, not a lesser version of either.
  • “DOCG” describes Italy’s protected-origin system; it is not a direct legal equivalent of an Australian GI.

Quick answer: what is Gattinara?

Gattinara is a DOCG red-wine appellation in Piedmont, Italy. The name also belongs to the municipality where the appellation’s grapes must be grown.

A useful way to understand the hierarchy is:

Italy → Piedmont/Piemonte → Province of Vercelli → Municipality of Gattinara → Gattinara DOCG

Gattinara DOCG was recognised in 1990. Its production area covers the entire municipality of Gattinara. That means a bottle labelled Gattinara refers to a protected place-based wine name, rather than simply telling you that the wine contains Nebbiolo.

For an Australian wine learner, the closest broad comparison is with the idea of a protected geographic identity. However, DOCG and the Australian Geographic Indications system use different legislation, terminology and administrative structures.

Where is Gattinara?

Gattinara is in northern Piedmont, in the Province of Vercelli. It forms part of the broader Alto Piemonte area, a useful geographic term for several northern Piedmont wine districts.

The nearest useful regional reference point is Vercelli, south of Gattinara. Turin is roughly to the south-west and is approximately a one-and-a-half-hour drive away, depending on the route, traffic and starting point. This is an orientation rather than a precise travel promise. Milan is another practical major-city reference, lying to the south-east.

An orientation map can help here, provided it distinguishes the municipality of Gattinara from the wider Alto Piemonte area. Nearby places such as Ghemme, Lozzolo, Romagnano Sesia and Roasio may help you picture the surrounding landscape, but they are not automatically part of Gattinara DOCG.

Gattinara is therefore not the same thing as Alto Piemonte. Alto Piemonte includes several separate denominations, including Ghemme, Boca, Bramaterra, Lessona and Sizzano.

What does Gattinara on a label mean?

On a bottle, Gattinara identifies the protected wine appellation and its place of origin. It does not name the grape variety.

The principal grape is Nebbiolo, known locally in Alto Piemonte as Spanna. The rules allow Nebbiolo/Spanna to make up 90–100% of the vineyard composition. Vespolina and Uva Rara may be included within the permitted limits, with the supplementary varieties together not exceeding 10%; Vespolina is capped at 4%.

So Gattinara is not automatically a 100% Nebbiolo wine, although Nebbiolo is its defining variety.

You may also see Riserva on a label. In Gattinara, this is not merely a decorative word. It relates to longer minimum ageing requirements.

How is Gattinara made?

The Consortium Tutela Nebbioli Alto Piemonte summarises the minimum ageing requirements as:

  • Gattinara: 35 months total ageing, including at least 24 months in wood.
  • Gattinara Riserva: 47 months total ageing, including at least 36 months in wood.

These are regulatory minimums, not a guarantee that every bottle will taste the same. Vineyard conditions, vintage and winemaking choices still shape the finished wine.

Gattinara’s vineyards are associated with hilly sites and porphyry ignimbrites—volcanic-derived rock linked to the ancient Valsesia volcanic system. These soils are an important part of the area’s geographic identity. They should not, however, be treated as a simple flavour recipe: volcanic geology does not automatically mean that every wine will taste “mineral”.

What does Gattinara wine taste like?

The official sensory descriptions refer to ruby-to-garnet colour, refined or fine aromas, spice, violet notes and a dry, harmonious or rounded palate with a characteristic slightly bitter finish.

In a glass, you may find:

  • lively acidity;
  • firm but refined tannin;
  • red-fruit and floral notes;
  • spice and savoury flavours;
  • a dry, structured palate;
  • more earthy, dried-fruit or savoury complexity as the wine develops.

These are tendencies rather than guarantees. A useful summary is that Gattinara is generally a fresh, structured Nebbiolo-based red with the acidity and tannin to develop over time.

The required ageing helps explain why Gattinara should not be thought of as a simple, generic northern Italian red. It has a regulatory framework designed around maturation, while the individual bottle’s readiness and ageing potential will still depend on its vintage, producer, storage and style.

Gattinara compared with Barolo and Barbaresco

Gattinara, Barolo and Barbaresco are separate Piedmontese DOCG appellations associated with Nebbiolo. They come from different geographic areas and have their own production rules.

Gattinara is in northern Piedmont’s Province of Vercelli and the wider Alto Piemonte area. Barolo and Barbaresco are in the Langhe, farther south in Piedmont. Gattinara is associated with porphyry and volcanic-derived geology, while the Langhe is commonly associated with marl and other sedimentary formations.

Broadly, Gattinara is often described as fresh, savoury and northern in character. Barolo and Barbaresco are often associated with more powerful or fuller expressions of Nebbiolo, although there is considerable variation within all three appellations.

The important point is not which is “better”. Gattinara is not failed Barolo or a cheaper substitute for Barbaresco. It is a distinct place with its own history, rules, geology and expression of Nebbiolo.

How should an Australian drinker approach a bottle?

When you see Gattinara on a wine list or bottle label:

  1. Read it as a protected place name, not as a grape variety.
  2. Expect Nebbiolo to be central, but remember that limited supplementary varieties may be allowed.
  3. Treat DOCG as an Italian protected-origin category, not as a direct translation of an Australian GI.
  4. Understand Riserva as a term connected to longer required ageing.
  5. Expect a dry, structured red rather than a soft, uncomplicated style.
  6. Pair its acidity and tannin with savoury foods such as braised meat, mushrooms, game-style dishes or firm cheese.

Gattinara is not a label you need to know in order to enjoy Italian wine. But once you understand the name, it becomes much easier to see what the bottle is telling you: this is Nebbiolo from a specific northern Piedmont place, governed by its own DOCG rules.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gattinara a grape?

No. Gattinara is a municipality and a protected wine appellation. Its principal grape is Nebbiolo, locally called Spanna.

Is Gattinara the same as Alto Piemonte?

No. Alto Piemonte is a broader geographic area containing several separate wine denominations. Gattinara DOCG refers specifically to wine from the municipality of Gattinara.

Is Gattinara the same as Barolo?

No. Both are separate Piedmont DOCG appellations based principally on Nebbiolo, but they come from different areas and have different regulations and site conditions.

Does Gattinara always contain 100% Nebbiolo?

No. Nebbiolo/Spanna must make up 90–100% of the vineyard composition, while limited amounts of permitted supplementary varieties may be included.

What does Gattinara Riserva mean?

It identifies a Gattinara wine subject to longer minimum ageing requirements than standard Gattinara, including longer ageing in wood.
