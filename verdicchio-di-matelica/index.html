Verdicchio di Matelica

Verdicchio

Highlights

  • Verdicchio di Matelica is a protected Italian wine appellation, not simply another name for the Verdicchio grape.
  • It comes from the inland Marche, around Matelica and the Esino Valley.
  • The standard wine must contain at least 85% Verdicchio.
  • The appellation also covers regulated spumante, passito and Riserva categories.
  • Its wines tend to be dry, fresh, firm and structured, although every bottle will vary.
  • Verdicchio di Matelica and Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi are separate appellations.
  • On an Australian label, “Verdicchio” identifies the grape; “di Matelica” identifies the protected place.

Quick answer

Verdicchio di Matelica is a protected wine appellation in Italy’s Marche region. It is centred on the inland town of Matelica and the Esino Valley, in an area covering parts of municipalities in the provinces of Macerata and Ancona.

The name combines a grape and a place. Verdicchio is the principal white grape variety, while di Matelica identifies the legally defined area where the wine is made. The standard appellation requires at least 85% Verdicchio, with up to 15% other white grapes permitted for cultivation in Marche.

For a beginner, the simplest description is a dry Italian white with notable freshness, firm acidity and structure. Regional and industry descriptions often suggest a more brisk, mineral-toned and savoury style than the broader, more approachable character commonly associated with Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi. These are tendencies, not guarantees.

Where is Verdicchio di Matelica?

Verdicchio di Matelica is in Italy → Marche → the inland Esino Valley, around Matelica. It is broadly south-west of Ancona, away from the Adriatic coast and close to the Apennine landscape.

Matelica is the most useful local place name for understanding the appellation. Other familiar reference points include Fabriano and Camerino. The formal production area includes parts of:

  • Matelica, Esanatoglia, Gagliole, Castelraimondo, Camerino and Pioraco in Macerata Province;
  • Cerreto d’Esi and Fabriano in Ancona Province.

The word parts matters. The appellation does not automatically cover every vineyard or every part of each named municipality.

Ancona, the capital of Marche, is a useful major-city reference. Matelica is roughly 1.5–2 hours by car from Ancona, depending on the route and starting point. Treat that only as broad orientation, not a fixed travel time.

What does the name mean?

Italy uses regulated origin terms to connect certain wines with defined places and production rules.

Verdicchio di Matelica is identified in the official material as an Italian DOC and European PDO/DOP appellation. DOC means Denominazione di Origine Controllata, or controlled designation of origin. PDO and DOP refer to the European protected designation of origin framework.

These terms are useful clues about regulated origin, but they are not exact Australian legal equivalents. For more background, see Italian DOC and DOCG.

The key distinction is:

  • Verdicchio — the grape variety;
  • Verdicchio di Matelica — a protected appellation based on that grape and a defined place;
  • Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi — a separate protected appellation in the Marche.

The broader grape topic is covered in What Is Verdicchio?.

What wines does the appellation cover?

The official specification recognises several categories:

  • Verdicchio di Matelica — the standard still wine;
  • Verdicchio di Matelica Spumante — sparkling wine;
  • Verdicchio di Matelica Passito — a naturally sweet style made from dried grapes;
  • Verdicchio di Matelica Riserva — a separately regulated still-wine category with stricter requirements.

For the standard wine, Verdicchio must make up at least 85% of the blend. Other white varieties permitted for cultivation in Marche may account for the remaining proportion.

This does not mean every wine made from Verdicchio qualifies for the appellation. A bottle must come from the defined area and meet the applicable production rules.

What does Verdicchio di Matelica taste like?

Official descriptions of the standard wine emphasise a pale straw-yellow colour, a delicate and distinctive aroma, a dry and harmonious palate and a pleasantly bitter finish.

In everyday tasting language, you might notice:

  • fresh acidity;
  • citrus, green-fruit or orchard-fruit impressions;
  • floral notes, particularly in younger wines;
  • a firm or structured texture;
  • savoury or mineral-toned impressions;
  • a gently bitter or almond-like finish.

The appellation’s inland setting is associated with cold winters, warm summers and significant day–night temperature variation. Vineyards are described by the regional wine body at approximately 250–650 metres above sea level. These conditions may contribute to the region’s typical freshness and shape, but they do not dictate the flavour of every bottle. Winemaking, vineyard site and vintage also matter.

If terms such as “acidity” or “body” are unfamiliar, WineDaddy’s guides to acidity in wine and wine body can help.

Matelica versus Castelli di Jesi

Both appellations are based principally on Verdicchio, but they are not interchangeable names.

Verdicchio di Matelica Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi
Broad setting Inland Esino Valley around Matelica Separate hills and hinterland around Jesi
Common style tendency Firmer, brisker and more structured Often rounder or more immediately approachable
Legal identity Separate protected appellation Separate protected appellation

The comparison is a starting point, not a ranking. Wines from either appellation can vary according to site, vintage and production choices.

How to recognise it in Australia

When reading a bottle, think in layers:

  • Verdicchio tells you the principal grape.
  • di Matelica tells you the protected geographic origin.
  • Riserva, Spumante or Passito signals an additional regulated category or style.
  • DOC, DOCG, DOP or PDO refers to Italian or European origin-control systems.

An Australian bottle labelled only Verdicchio is not automatically Verdicchio di Matelica. Likewise, an Australian wine made from Verdicchio grapes does not acquire the Italian appellation simply because it uses the same variety.

Look for the complete protected name if you want to identify the Italian appellation. The full name is the practical clue that distinguishes it from generic Verdicchio and from Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi.
