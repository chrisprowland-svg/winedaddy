How corkage works

Understand corkage, BYO permission, venue policies and Australian differences before bringing wine to a restaurant or event.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Corkage is a venue’s charge for handling and serving wine you bring yourself.
  • Corkage and BYO permission are separate: paying a fee does not automatically give you permission to bring a bottle.
  • The fee might cover opening, glasses, chilling and pouring—but the venue decides what is included.
  • There is no single corkage rule across Australia.
  • Always ask about the current policy before arriving, especially for clubs, functions and special events.
  • Corkage is separate from the price of your bottle and from the venue’s own wine-list prices.

Quick answer: what is corkage?

Corkage is a fee charged by a restaurant or other venue when it agrees to accept and serve wine brought by a customer.

Depending on the venue, corkage might cover some combination of opening the bottle, providing glassware, chilling the wine and pouring it. It may also simply be the venue’s charge for accommodating customer-supplied wine. There is no standard national fee or fixed list of services.

The important distinction is this: corkage does not automatically mean BYO is allowed. First, ask the venue whether you may bring wine. Then confirm the corkage amount, how it is calculated and what the fee includes.

The bottle’s cost is yours; corkage is the venue’s separate charge. It is also different from the price of a bottle bought from the venue and should not automatically be treated as a general service charge.

How corkage usually works

The process is usually straightforward:

  1. Ask before booking or arriving. Confirm that the venue accepts BYO wine.
  2. Check the conditions. Ask about the corkage fee, bottle limits, wine types, bookings and events.
  3. Bring the approved bottle. Do not assume that permission for one booking applies to every visit.
  4. Present it to staff. On arrival, let the venue explain how it will be opened and served.
  5. Follow the venue’s service arrangements. Staff may provide the glasses, chill the wine and pour it, or may offer only some of these services.
  6. Check the bill. Make sure the charge matches what you were told.

The word “corkage” can sound as though it refers only to removing a cork. In practice, it is a hospitality term for a BYO-related charge. It does not mean the bottle must have a natural cork closure.

What might the fee include?

There is no universal package. A venue may associate corkage with:

  • opening the bottle;
  • providing or using the venue’s glassware;
  • chilling white, rosé or sparkling wine;
  • pouring the wine;
  • handling the bottle during the meal.

Some venues may do all of these; others may set different arrangements. Decanting, tasting the wine first or assessing whether it is faulty should not be assumed unless the venue has agreed to it.

Corkage may be calculated per bottle, per person, per table or in another way. Because there is no national standard, ask rather than relying on what happened at another restaurant.

Corkage, BYO and other charges

These terms are related, but they are not interchangeable.

BYO permission means the venue allows you to bring your own alcohol. It may be governed by the venue’s policy and, depending on the circumstances, by a licence, permit or registration framework.

Corkage is the venue’s charge connected with accepting and servicing that bottle. A venue may allow BYO and charge corkage, allow BYO without a corkage charge, or not allow BYO at all.

The bottle price is what you paid for the wine elsewhere. Corkage does not replace that cost.

The venue’s wine price applies when you buy a bottle from the restaurant or venue. It is a separate transaction from bringing your own bottle.

A service charge is a broader term and is not automatically the same thing as corkage. If a bill contains an unfamiliar charge, ask what it covers.

Why Australian corkage rules vary

Australia does not have one national BYO or corkage rule. Requirements can differ between states and territories, and may also depend on the venue’s type, authorisation and circumstances.

For example:

  • In Queensland, government guidance says restaurants may refuse BYO and advises checking local council requirements. It also identifies circumstances involving some licensed premises and public events where bringing alcohol may be prohibited.
  • In New South Wales, a BYO restaurant does not necessarily need a liquor licence to operate as a BYO restaurant, but it still has liquor-related responsibilities.
  • In Victoria, a BYO permit framework applies in some circumstances, while some licence categories may already allow BYO subject to applicable rules.
  • In the Northern Territory, some businesses that allow customers to bring alcohol while the business serves it may need registration.
  • In Western Australia, guidance says holders of certain liquor licences decide whether to allow BYO, subject to the applicable law and conditions.

These examples are not a complete legal guide. They show why a statement such as “all Australian restaurants allow BYO” or “corkage is compulsory” would be misleading. The venue’s current policy and the relevant jurisdiction both matter.

Good corkage etiquette

Corkage is usually a courtesy arrangement, not a right to run your own wine service at the table.

Give the venue advance notice, particularly if you are bringing more than one bottle or arranging a large booking. Bring only the type and quantity of wine that was approved. A venue may accept bottled wine but not beer or spirits, or may apply different conditions to functions and special events.

When you arrive, hand the bottle to staff rather than opening it yourself. Accept the venue’s glassware and service process unless you agreed something else beforehand. Staff may have a house style for opening, chilling and pouring the wine.

You are not expected to know every service detail. The useful habit is simply to ask clearly, agree on the arrangement and respect the venue’s answer.

Your pre-booking checklist

Before bringing wine, ask:

  • Is BYO wine permitted for this booking?
  • What is the corkage charge?
  • Is it charged per bottle, person, table or another basis?
  • What does the fee include?
  • Are there limits on the number or type of bottles?
  • Are wines already listed by the venue excluded?
  • Does the policy change for functions, clubs, private events or large bookings?
  • Will the venue provide glasses, chilling and pouring?
  • Should the bottle be presented to staff on arrival?
  • Could the policy or fee have changed since your last visit?

If the answer is unclear, check again when booking. A quick conversation can prevent an awkward surprise at the door—or on the bill.

Frequently asked questions

Does corkage mean BYO is allowed?

No. Corkage is a charge; BYO permission comes first. Ask the venue whether it accepts customer-supplied wine.

Does corkage always include opening and glasses?

No. Those services may be included, but the venue decides what the fee covers.

Is corkage charged per bottle?

It can be, but venues may use another method. Confirm the calculation before arriving.

Can I bring any alcohol I like?

Do not assume so. A venue may accept only wine or impose other conditions. Ask about beer, spirits, bottle limits and event restrictions.

Do I have to bring a bottle with a cork?

No. “Corkage” is a hospitality term. It does not describe the bottle’s closure.

Is corkage the same as a service charge?

Not necessarily. Ask the venue what each charge covers.
