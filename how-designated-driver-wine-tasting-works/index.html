How designated-driver wine tasting works

Learn how designated-driver arrangements work at Australian wine tastings, what tickets may include and how to plan safely if you are driving.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • A designated driver attends the tasting but plans to remain alcohol-free.
  • The driver can usually enjoy the social side of the event, but alcoholic tastings may not be included.
  • Tickets and inclusions vary between venues and events.
  • Spitting is a tasting practice, not a guarantee that someone is safe or legal to drive.
  • If you are driving, the safest plan is not to drink at all.
  • If anyone wants to drink, arrange alternative transport or another sober driver.

Quick answer

A designated-driver wine tasting is a winery visit, festival or organised tasting where one person in the group agrees not to drink alcohol so they can drive everyone safely afterwards.

The driver may still join the group, enjoy the setting, learn about the wines and take part in non-alcoholic parts of the event. However, do not assume that a “tasting” includes a special designated-driver arrangement. Some events offer a non-drinker or designated-driver ticket; others simply expect the driver to buy a standard ticket and abstain from alcohol.

Ask the organiser what applies before booking. Australian road rules still apply whether the alcohol was consumed at a winery, cellar door, festival or restaurant.

What happens at the event?

The process is usually straightforward:

  1. Check the policy before booking. Look for a designated-driver or non-drinker ticket and read what it includes.
  2. Book the right ticket. It might provide entry, food, entertainment, water or non-alcoholic drinks, while excluding wine tastings.
  3. Tell staff when you arrive. Confirm that you are the non-drinking driver and ask what areas and activities are available to you.
  4. Stay with the agreed plan. If you are driving, do not consume alcohol.
  5. Have a backup plan. If you or someone in the group decides to drink, arrange a taxi, rideshare, shuttle, public transport, overnight stay or another driver who will remain alcohol-free.

Some Australian events offer free or discounted designated-driver entry, along with options such as mocktails or other non-alcoholic drinks. Other events may only recommend that groups nominate a non-drinking driver. There is no single package used by every Australian winery or festival.

Does the driver get to taste wine?

Not necessarily. A designated-driver ticket may exclude alcoholic tastings altogether. Depending on the organiser, the driver might have access to non-alcoholic alternatives or the social parts of the event, but the details can differ even between venues at the same festival.

Spitting may be available as part of the tasting experience. It should not, however, be treated as proof that you are sober, below a legal limit or safe to drive. Do not try to calculate a “safe” number of samples from pour size or rely on spitting as a driving plan.

Questions to ask the organiser

Before booking, ask:

  • Is there a designated-driver or non-drinker ticket?
  • What does it include?
  • Are alcoholic tastings excluded?
  • Are water, food or non-alcoholic drinks provided?
  • Can the driver enter the same areas as the rest of the group?
  • Does every attendee need a ticket?
  • Are shuttles or other transport options available?
  • What happens if the nominated driver decides to drink?
  • Do participating venues have different conditions from the main event?

These questions matter because an event organiser may set general rules while individual venues control their own sessions.

Australian drink-driving advice

Drink-driving rules are administered through Australian state and territory systems. Specific requirements can vary with your jurisdiction, licence type and vehicle. Do not treat one published blood-alcohol limit as a universal answer for every Australian driver.

National health and road-safety guidance takes a simpler approach: if you are driving, do not drink. If anyone plans to drink at the tasting, organise transport that does not depend on that person driving home.

FAQs

Can a designated driver attend a wine tasting?
Usually, yes, but the available activities and ticket inclusions depend on the organiser.

Are designated-driver tickets always free?
No. Some events offer free or discounted entry; others do not offer a separate ticket.

Can I spit wine and then drive?
Do not treat spitting as a guarantee of driving safety or legal compliance. The safest plan is to avoid alcohol entirely if driving.

What if the driver changes their mind?
Do not drive after drinking. Arrange alternative transport, an overnight stay or another driver who will remain alcohol-free.
