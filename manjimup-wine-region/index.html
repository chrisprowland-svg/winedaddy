Manjimup wine region: a beginner’s guide

Discover where the Manjimup wine region is, how it fits into Western Australia’s GI system, and the Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Chardonnay and Verdelho styles associated with it.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • Manjimup is a protected Australian wine GI in the south-west of Western Australia.
  • It is a region within the South West Australia zone.
  • The town of Manjimup is the most useful reference point, but the town and the GI do not necessarily have identical boundaries.

Quick answer: what is the Manjimup wine region?

Manjimup is a protected wine Geographical Indication (GI) in Western Australia. In Australia’s wine geography, it is a region within the South West Australia zone.

The region is best understood around the town of Manjimup, in Western Australia’s Southern Forests area. From Perth, Manjimup is roughly three and a half hours south by road, depending on your route and destination. Wine Australia identifies Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Chardonnay and Verdelho as the principal varieties.

In broad terms, Manjimup’s inland and elevated position, cool conditions, ocean influence and relatively dry late growing season may help retain freshness while allowing grapes to ripen steadily. The region is particularly associated with Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, fresh Chardonnay, and lively Verdelho. Those are regional tendencies, not rules: vineyard site, vintage, producer and winemaking choices all matter.

Where is Manjimup?

Manjimup is in the south-west of Western Australia, inland from the state’s better-known coastal wine areas. The town of Manjimup is the clearest practical orientation point and functions as the area’s principal regional centre.

The broader area is often called the Southern Forests, a practical and regional-industry term associated with Manjimup and Pemberton. It is important not to confuse that broader identity with a single legal GI. Manjimup and Pemberton are separate wine regions.

On a bottle, the protected regional wording a drinker is most likely to see is Manjimup. Broader terms such as Western Australia or South West Australia may also appear, but they describe a wider geographic level. “Southern Forests” is useful context, while “Manjimup” is the specific GI name.

Manjimup is also separate from Margaret River, Geographe and Blackwood Valley. These are related Western Australian wine regions in the wider south-west landscape, not subdivisions of Manjimup.

What are Manjimup’s climate and landscape like?

Wine Australia describes Manjimup as an inland, elevated region influenced by both the Indian and Southern oceans. Compared with coastal Margaret River, it has a more continental character because it is higher and farther inland.

The regional profile describes cold winters, wet spring conditions and relatively dry summer and autumn conditions. It also records an approximate altitude range of 108 to 345 metres and around 233 millimetres of growing-season rainfall. These are regional profile figures—not conditions found uniformly at every vineyard or in every vintage.

The region’s soils include red, gravelly loams and ironstone-influenced profiles associated with underlying granite and the Warren River and its tributaries. That information helps describe Manjimup’s physical setting, but soil is only one part of the picture. Drainage, slope, exposure, vine variety, season and winemaking decisions also influence the finished wine.

This is a useful way to think about regional character: climate and landscape can help create the conditions for a style, but they do not act like a recipe. A cooler site may help retain acidity; a warm, exposed site may ripen fruit differently. The bottle reflects the interaction of those factors rather than one simple cause.

Which grapes is Manjimup known for?

Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot

Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot are Manjimup’s clearest red-wine associations. Wine Australia identifies them among the region’s dominant plantings and describes Cabernet–Merlot blends as part of Manjimup’s wine identity.

A Manjimup red made from these varieties may show a combination of dark fruit, structure and freshness, with the exact balance depending on vintage, site and winemaking. It is better to expect a regional tendency than a fixed formula.

Cabernet Sauvignon naturally brings structure and tannin, while Merlot can contribute softer texture and ripe fruit character. To understand those terms more confidently, see our guides to Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and tannins in wine.

Chardonnay

Manjimup Chardonnay is associated with elegant, relatively light- to medium-bodied wines showing melon and citrus character. Freshness is an important part of that expectation, although oak, lees work, bottle age, vintage and site can all change the wine’s texture and flavour.

“Light- to medium-bodied” does not mean flavourless. It describes the wine’s weight and texture in the mouth, not how much aroma or character it has. Our guide to wine body explains the distinction.

Verdelho

Verdelho is one of Manjimup’s most distinctive regional associations. Wine Australia describes local examples as having fresh, lively acidity, tropical fruit flavours and a rounded mouthfeel.

That combination can make Verdelho an approachable way to explore the region: it may feel bright and energetic at first, while still having enough texture to avoid seeming thin. The result will vary from wine to wine, so treat this as a useful expectation rather than a guarantee. You can also explore our guide to acidity in wine.

Other varieties

Pinot Noir, Sauvignon Blanc and Shiraz are also mentioned in broader Southern Forests material. Because those descriptions often combine Manjimup and Pemberton, they should be treated as additional regional context rather than proof that they are Manjimup’s leading varieties.

Manjimup compared with nearby wine regions

Pemberton: The closest important comparison. Manjimup and Pemberton share the broader Southern Forests identity and a related inland south-west setting, but they are separate GIs.

Margaret River: A separate, more coastal GI. Wine Australia describes Manjimup as more continental because of its inland position and higher altitude, making Margaret River a useful comparison without implying a quality hierarchy.

Geographe: A separate GI in the South West Australia zone. It belongs to the same broad Western Australian wine conversation, but Manjimup should not be described as part of Geographe.

Blackwood Valley: Another separate south-western Western Australian GI. The names may appear together in regional discussions, but they represent distinct geographic identities.

What should you expect from a Manjimup-labelled wine?

A bottle labelled Manjimup tells you where the grapes came from under Australia’s protected GI system. It does not guarantee that the wine is Cabernet Sauvignon, red, dry, full-bodied, unoaked or made in one particular style.

A useful starting point is:

  • Cabernet Sauvignon or Merlot for structured red wines;
  • Chardonnay for fresh, elegant white wine;
  • Verdelho for bright acidity, tropical fruit and rounded texture.

Then look beyond the region name. The grape variety, vintage, alcohol level, producer information and your own taste preferences will help you understand the bottle more accurately.

Most importantly, “Manjimup” is an invitation to expect a place-based character—not a promise that every wine from the region will taste the same.

Frequently asked questions

Is Manjimup the same wine region as Pemberton?

No. They are separate protected Australian wine GIs, although both are commonly associated with the broader Southern Forests area.

Is Manjimup part of Margaret River?

No. Margaret River is a separate Western Australian GI. Manjimup is an inland and elevated region within the South West Australia zone.

What wine is Manjimup best known for?

Wine Australia identifies Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Chardonnay and Verdelho as the principal varieties. Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot are especially important red-wine associations, while Chardonnay and Verdelho provide key white-wine styles.

Does a Manjimup label guarantee a particular taste?

No. A GI identifies geographic origin. Taste also depends on variety, vineyard site, vintage, producer and winemaking.

How far is Manjimup from Perth?

As a broad orientation, Manjimup is roughly three and a half hours south of Perth by road. The exact distance and time vary with the route and destination.
