Highlights

  • Condrieu is a protected French wine appellation, not a grape variety.
  • It belongs to the Northern Rhône, on the Rhône River’s right bank.
  • Condrieu is made from Viognier alone.
  • The appellation is reserved for still white wine.
  • Its wines are often highly aromatic, floral, ripe-fruited and rounded.
  • Condrieu is not automatically sweet, and every bottle will not taste the same.
  • Australian Viognier is not Condrieu unless it comes from the Condrieu appellation and follows its rules.

Quick answer: what is Condrieu?

Condrieu is a defined French AOC/AOP wine appellation in the Northern Rhône. AOC means Appellation d’Origine Contrôlée, while AOP is the European protected-designation term. In simple terms, Condrieu is a regulated place name with rules about where the grapes come from and how the wine is made. You can learn more about this system in our guide to French AOC and AOP.

The appellation is reserved for still white wine made entirely from Viognier. That makes Condrieu a place-based wine identity, while Viognier is the grape variety. A bottle of Australian Viognier may be made from the same grape, but it is not Condrieu unless it is produced within the Condrieu appellation and meets its requirements.

Condrieu is usually recognised for its expressive perfume, ripe-fruit character and generous texture. Common descriptions include white flowers, violet, peach and apricot. These are tendencies, not guarantees: sweetness, acidity, alcohol, oak influence and texture can vary from wine to wine.

Where is Condrieu?

Condrieu is in France, within the Rhône Valley, and more specifically the Northern Rhône. It sits on the Rhône’s right bank, about 40 kilometres south of Lyon. Vienne is the nearest useful regional centre, approximately 10 kilometres away.

From Lyon, Condrieu is roughly a 35–50-minute drive south, depending on your starting point, route and traffic. Treat that as a practical orientation rather than a precise travel promise.

The appellation lies between Côte-Rôtie to the north and Saint-Joseph to the south. These are separate Northern Rhône appellations, not alternative names for Condrieu.

The legal appellation area covers seven communes:

  • Condrieu
  • Chavanay
  • Malleval
  • Saint-Michel-sur-Rhône
  • Saint-Pierre-de-Bœuf
  • Vérin
  • Limony

They are spread across the Rhône, Loire and Ardèche departments. A commune name and the Condrieu appellation name are not interchangeable: only specifically delimited areas qualify for the appellation.

What grape is used in Condrieu?

Viognier is the only permitted grape variety for Condrieu. This is one of the most useful facts to remember when reading a label.

Viognier is naturally associated with floral and ripe-fruit aromas, and it can produce a wine with a broad, soft or rounded mouthfeel. Those characteristics help explain why Condrieu is often described as aromatic and generous.

However, not every Viognier is Condrieu. Viognier is grown in many places, including Australia. An Australian bottle labelled “Viognier” identifies the grape variety; it does not claim the French appellation. For a broader explanation of the grape itself, see What is Viognier?.

What does Condrieu taste like?

Condrieu is commonly an intensely aromatic white wine. You may notice floral scents alongside ripe stone-fruit characters such as peach and apricot. Some descriptions also mention tropical-fruit notes.

On the palate, Condrieu may feel supple, rounded, rich or full-bodied compared with a very light, crisp white wine. It can still have freshness or liveliness, but it should not be reduced to a single acidity profile. Ripeness, alcohol, residual sugar, oak and winemaking choices all influence the final result.

One important misconception is that Condrieu is always sweet. Under the current specification, Condrieu may be dry or may retain fermentable sugar. A sweet-smelling wine is not necessarily sweet-tasting, either: ripe fruit and floral aromas can make a wine seem generous without meaning that it contains much sugar.

The appellation name tells you about place and production rules. It does not guarantee one identical flavour profile in every bottle. This is a useful way to think about terroir: geography can contribute to wine character, but it does not act like a flavour recipe.

Why does the landscape matter?

Condrieu’s vineyards occupy very steep slopes above the Rhône. Many are arranged on narrow terraces, with retaining walls and difficult vineyard access. The slopes have favourable exposures and are described in the appellation specification as having a Lyonnais mesoclimate.

The soils and underlying geology are varied, including fractured acidic material, granite and metamorphic components, clay-rich fissures and some loess. These features help create conditions in which Viognier can ripen, while exposure and local climate can contribute to balance and freshness.

That is a more useful explanation than saying the soil “creates” a particular aroma. Climate, slope and geology are influences among several. They do not force every Condrieu to taste the same. Our guides to how climate affects wine and wine aroma explore these ideas further.

How should an Australian learner read the name?

When you see Condrieu on a wine label, read it as a protected French geographic designation. It means more than “this wine contains Viognier”.

When you see Viognier on an Australian label, read it primarily as a grape indication. The wine may share some broad aromatic or textural ideas with Condrieu, but its place, climate, production choices and legal framework may be different.

This does not make one category automatically better. It simply means the two labels answer different questions:

  • Condrieu: Where is the wine from, and which appellation rules apply?
  • Viognier: Which grape variety is the wine made from?

How might you serve Condrieu?

Condrieu’s aromatic and rounded character can make it a useful white-wine choice when you want something more generous than a very light, sharply crisp style. Serve it cool, but avoid treating “colder” as automatically better: excessive chilling can mute aroma.

At home in Australia, let the wine warm slightly in the glass if its perfume seems restrained. A broad white-wine glass can also help you notice its aroma. For more general guidance, see wine serving temperature explained and what is wine body?.

FAQs

Is Condrieu a grape?

No. Condrieu is a protected French appellation. Viognier is the grape variety used for Condrieu.

Is Condrieu always sweet?

No. Condrieu can be dry or contain fermentable sugar. Do not assume that every bottle is a dessert wine.

Is Condrieu the same as Viognier?

No. Condrieu is a specific place-based appellation made from Viognier. Viognier wines from Australia or elsewhere are not automatically Condrieu.

Is Condrieu part of the Northern Rhône?

Yes. It is a Northern Rhône appellation within the broader Rhône Valley wine region.

Does Condrieu always taste floral and rich?

Those are common tendencies, but they are not guarantees. Wine style can vary with ripeness, alcohol, acidity, residual sugar, oak and winemaking choices.
