What single vineyard means

Learn what “single vineyard” means on an Australian wine label, how it differs from estate, block, variety and GI claims, and what it does not guarantee.

Foundation guide · Beginner friendly · Australian context

Highlights

  • “Single vineyard” generally means the grapes came from one identified vineyard or vineyard site.
  • It is a claim about where the grapes came from, not a guarantee of quality.
  • It does not automatically mean estate-grown, estate-bottled, single block or single variety.
  • A single-vineyard wine may still be made from more than one grape variety.
  • In Australia, the phrase has no specific legal definition or universal percentage threshold.
  • Read it alongside the vintage, grape variety, region or GI, and producer information.

Quick answer

When a wine label says single vineyard, the producer is presenting the wine as made from grapes sourced from one vineyard site, rather than fruit blended from several vineyards.

The phrase is mainly about provenance—the source of the grapes—and may indicate that fruit from that site was kept separate during production. It can help a producer identify a particular place or vineyard as part of the wine’s story.

But single vineyard does not automatically mean better wine, a more distinctive flavour, older vines, organic farming, estate ownership or greater ageing potential. The wine’s character also depends on its grape variety, vintage, growing conditions, winemaking and maturation.

What does it mean in Australia?

In Australia, “single vineyard” is not a defined legal wine term with a standard percentage requirement. Wine Australia says the phrase is subject to the broader requirement that label claims must not mislead or deceive. It considers the claim potentially misleading if the wine was not made from fruit sourced from a single vineyard.

That means the safest way to read the phrase is as a producer’s claim that the grapes came from one identified vineyard or site—not as a formal quality category or certification.

A vineyard name also does not automatically have the status of a geographical indication (GI). A GI is a registered geographic area, such as a region or locality. A named vineyard is a more specific site reference. A label may show both:

2024 Shiraz — “Hill Block” — Barossa Valley — Single Vineyard

In this fictional example, “Hill Block” suggests a named site, while “Barossa Valley” provides broader regional context. The wording does not, by itself, establish that Hill Block is a registered GI, that the wine is estate-grown or that it will taste a particular way.

Single vineyard is not the same as…

A multi-vineyard blend: Fruit from two or more vineyard sites is combined. This can be a deliberate choice for balance, consistency or style. Single-vineyard sourcing is not automatically superior.

Estate-grown: This usually describes fruit grown in an estate or producer-controlled context. An estate may include several vineyards, so estate-grown does not necessarily mean single vineyard.

Estate-bottled: This concerns the relationship between growing, winemaking and bottling. It is a different claim from single vineyard.

Single block: A block is usually a more specific subdivision within a vineyard. A single-block wine may come from one part of a larger vineyard, while a single-vineyard wine may include fruit from more than one block. Usage can vary, so treat this as a general distinction rather than a fixed Australian rule.

Single variety: This describes the grape composition, not the source. A single-variety wine can combine fruit from several vineyards, while a single-vineyard wine may potentially contain more than one variety.

Regional or GI-labelled wine: A GI identifies a recognised region or locality and may cover many vineyards. Single vineyard is a narrower site claim.

What should you look for next?

Treat “single vineyard” as one piece of label information. Check the grape variety, vintage, region or GI, producer notes and any available tasting description. Those details can tell you more about what is in the bottle and what style the producer intended.

The phrase may make a wine’s source easier to understand, but it cannot predict whether you will enjoy it. That is still a matter of the wine’s variety, vintage, style and your own taste.

Frequently asked questions

Does single vineyard mean the producer owns the vineyard?

No. The phrase describes the claimed source of the grapes. It does not, by itself, establish ownership or estate control.

Does single vineyard mean single variety?

No. One term concerns grape origin; the other concerns grape composition.

Are single-vineyard wines always better?

No. A single-vineyard claim does not guarantee quality, price, age-worthiness or a particular flavour profile.
